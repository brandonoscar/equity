# Republished datasets yield de-identified judgments, not debtors

No free, no-login, currently maintained republished dataset names US civil judgment debtors with amounts in bulk. The largest new free source of judgment amounts is the **New York State OCA housing-court judgments table** on a public S3 bucket. It has a 193.5 MB `oca_judgments.csv` with `totaljudgmentamount` and status, it covers courts statewide, and it was refreshed on **2026-11-22**. But the index number is hashed, the parties table has no names, and the licence is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, which is non-commercial. The only named, republished civil tables are two frozen, unlicensed GitHub scrapes. One holds **53,465 Cleveland eviction cases from 2014–2020**, with names, property addresses and costs but no judgment amounts. The other holds **9,036 Oregon evictions from 2020–2021**, with names, addresses and a "judgment creates lien" flag but no dollar figures. The government-program angle is thinner still. The best new publishers are **New Jersey's Lawyers' Fund for Client Protection** (quarterly PDFs naming attorneys with amounts paid) and **Maryland's Home Improvement Commission** (yearly pages naming contractors with Guaranty Fund awards). Both sit on blocked hosts, both are small, and both name debtors who are by definition uncollectible. County recorders and state lien registries add almost nothing free. An ArcGIS, Hub and Socrata sweep found no county recorder judgment index anywhere. The one new reachable recorder-derived layer is Tucson's **517 code liens** pulled from the Pima County Recorder. The rest of the recorded-judgment universe (Texas abstracts, Pennsylvania prothonotaries, Ohio certificates of judgment, Georgia's statewide GSCCCA lien index, most Florida clerks) is sold or obtainable only by public-records request. The practical conclusion is that republished data is a source of **aggregate and screening signal, not inventory**. The trend toward stripping names (Case Harvester, virginiacourtdata.org, Debt Collection Lab, Eviction Lab) means this will not improve.

## Researchers strip debtor names before they republish

The well-known court-data projects all hold named records upstream but publish them de-identified or behind approval. The **Housing Data Coalition's `oca` repo** turns XML that OCA delivers by SFTP into eleven public CSV tables: index, causes, addresses, parties, events, appearances, outcomes, motions, decisions, judgments and warrants ([repo README](https://github.com/housing-data-coalition/oca)). The judgments table carries judgment type, filed and entered dates, with-possession flag, latest status, **total judgment amount**, and creditor and debtor roles. The parties table (about 689 MB) has role, person-or-business type and representation, but **no names**. The addresses table stops at city, state and zip ([oca_judgments.csv](https://oca-2-dev.s3.amazonaws.com/public/oca_judgments.csv); [oca_parties.csv](https://oca-2-dev.s3.amazonaws.com/public/oca_parties.csv); [last-updated](https://oca-2-dev.s3.amazonaws.com/public/last-updated-date.txt)). Its coverage is broader than New York City and includes courts such as Yonkers City Court and Suffolk District Court ([oca_index.csv](https://oca-2-dev.s3.amazonaws.com/public/oca_index.csv)). For a judgment buyer, it gives statewide volume, amount distributions and satisfaction rates by zip. It does not give debtors. The README also says the same data are "publicly available in XML format" on the court system's site. Whether that raw XML carries names is the most important unanswered question in this report.

Virginia and Maryland show the same pattern. **virginiacourtdata.org** offers bulk CSVs of General District and Circuit civil cases heard through the end of 2024, last updated April 4, 2025. But the files are "Anonymized: Case numbers, names, and dates of birth have been removed". Named data goes only to approved journalists, non-profits, researchers and government bodies through a Firebase sign-in, so it **fails the no-login rule** ([virginia-court-data repo](https://github.com/bschoenfeld/virginia-court-data); [index.html](https://github.com/bschoenfeld/virginia-court-data/blob/master/index.html)). The newer **VirginiaJusticeData** release is CC BY 4.0 and names defendants across 15.9 million records. It is criminal only, though, and its dollar fields are fines and costs, not civil judgments ([VirginiaJusticeData](https://github.com/VirginiaJusticeData/virginia-court-data)). Maryland's **Case Harvester** has retired its public site and monthly table exports. Its relaunch at caseharvester.org is "licensed access", and it withholds the details of eviction tenants ([CaseHarvester README](https://github.com/dismantl/CaseHarvester)). The Debt Collection Lab site repo publishes a case-level `lawsuit_data.csv` with plaintiff, date, default-judgment flag and amount, keyed to a census tract instead of a defendant. A sample row is LVNV Funding at $1,574.09 in Harris County, 2020 ([lawsuit_data.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hoshmn/debt-collection-lab/master/data/lawsuit_data.csv)). That makes it useful for **profiling which debt buyers win default judgments, and for how much**, but not for finding debtors.

The two named exceptions are hobbyist snapshots. `sdl60660/cleveland_eviction_mapping` holds **53,465 Cleveland Municipal Court eviction filings from 2014-01-02 to 2020-10-20**. Its fields are defendant names, property address, plaintiff, costs and disposition. Most rows ended in dismissal or a magistrate hearing, and there is no judgment-amount column ([eviction_data.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sdl60660/cleveland_eviction_mapping/master/data/eviction_data.csv)). `devinmacarthur/TopEvictors2021` holds **9,036 mostly Multnomah County cases filed 2020-01-01 to 2021-07-22**. Its fields are defendant names, defendant address and judgment type: **1,315 "General Creates Lien" and 352 "General; Supplemental Creates Lien"**, against 3,910 dismissals ([flat_file.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/devinmacarthur/TopEvictors2021/master/data/full_scrape_07_22_2021/flat_file.csv)). The roughly 1,667 lien-creating Oregon judgments are the only rows in the whole sweep that are named, addressed and flagged as liens. None of them carries an amount, and they are now five years old. Searches of Hugging Face and Kaggle found no named US civil-judgment or eviction dataset, only aggregates. Those hosts could not be opened directly, so this is weak evidence of absence ([New America overview](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/us-eviction-court-data/)). Oklahoma's OSCN scrape repo is 4.4 GB of scanned docket TIFFs with no structured table ([oscn-scraper](https://github.com/darrenjaworski/oscn-scraper)).

## Government programs publish defaulters only after the money is gone

No statewide court-ordered-debt program publishes a named delinquent file. Arizona's FARE program collects court-ordered obligations that are more than 90 days delinquent through MVD holds and intercepts of tax refunds, lottery and gaming winnings, and it publishes no list ([azcourts.gov FARE](https://azcourts.gov/courtservices/Consolidated-Collections-Unit/FARE); [AO 2023-195](https://www.azcourts.gov/Portals/0/22/admorder/Orders23/2023-195.pdf)). The closest local artifacts are Texas municipal-court **capias pro fine** warrant PDFs. A capias pro fine issues when "an individual fails to satisfy a judgment according to its terms" ([Huntsville TX warrants](https://huntsvilletx.gov/388/Warrants); [Harker Heights Aug 2026 PDF](https://www.harkerheights.gov/images/PDF/WARRANTLISTING08092026xls.pdf)). These are criminal and traffic fine judgments, not civil liens, so they work only as a flag that a person has unpaid court debt.

The richer vein is **recovery and client-protection funds**. These funds pay victims who usually first had to obtain an uncollectible judgment against a licensee, so every payout names a proven non-payer. **New Jersey's LFCP** posts quarterly "claims paid" PDFs from 2022 to 2025 Q4. Each gives the attorney's name, town, disciplinary status, amount paid, a cumulative total and the claim basis ([NJ LFCP 2025 Q4](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/lfcp2025q4.pdf); [2024 Q2](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/attorneys/new-jersey-lawyers-fund-client-protection/lf-2quarter-2024.pdf)). Volume is small: **14 claims and over $85,000 in January 2026**, and 4,916 claims against 903 attorneys across the fund's history ([NJ press, Jan 2026](https://www.njcourts.gov/press-releases/2026/01/lawyers-fund-client-protection-awards-over-85000-14-claims)). **Ohio's LFCP** names attorneys in quarterly releases. In June 2026 it paid $408,112.67 to 22 victims, including $56,020 across seven clients of one attorney ([Court News Ohio, Jun 2026](https://courtnewsohio.gov/happening/2026/LFCP_061526.asp); [Ohio reimbursements](https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/courts/advisory/boards/client-protection/reimbursements/)). **Maryland's MHIC** posts yearly disciplinary pages for FY2014 to FY2025. Each entry gives the contractor name, trade name, case number and a Guaranty Fund award of roughly $500 to $30,000 ([MHIC FY2025](https://www.labor.maryland.gov/license/mhic/mhicdisc2025.shtml)). **Nevada's NSCB** posts Recovery Fund award PDFs from 2010 to 2024. One January 2024 cycle paid $394,068 to 24 homeowners ([NSCB awards 01-30-19](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/01-30-19-Recovery-Fund-Awards.pdf); [Jan 2024 release](https://nvcontractorsboard.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/01-22-2024-NEVADA-STATE-CONTRACTORS-BOARD-AWARDS-394000-TO-HARMED-HOMEOWNERS-THROUGH-RECOVERY-FUND-NEWS-RELEASE.pdf)). Florida's construction fund suspends a license automatically on payment, but it publishes cases only in board minutes ([Fla. Stat. 489.143](https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/489.143)). California's CSLB suspends licenses for unsatisfied construction judgments. Its free master list covers only active licenses. The full 700,000-record file costs $235, so it fails the free test, though the free list's record layout might expose a suspension field worth diffing monthly ([CSLB Data Portal](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/DataPortal/ContractorList); [layout LBL780J1](https://WWW.CSLB.CA.GOV/Resources/FormsAndApplications/License_Master_Record_Layout_LBL780J1.pdf)).

Read correctly, these lists are a **negative screen**. The fund is typically subrogated to the victim's claim, so the creditor is now the state, and the debtor has already shown that collection failed. Their best use in the graph is to mark contractors and attorneys whose other judgments are unlikely to pay. On the reachable side, NYC's **DOF Scofftow** dataset has **1,565,097 boot and tow cases** for unpaid parking-judgment debt, with `judgment_amount` and `total_amount_owed`, updated 2026-11-16. It is keyed by licence plate with no names, and the DMV records needed to join it to a name are barred by the federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act (DPPA) ([Socrata qmh3-uvgq](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/qmh3-uvgq.json)).

## Recorded-judgment indexes stay locked in clerk offices

An ArcGIS Online search found **zero items** for "abstract of judgment" or "certificate of judgment" ([ArcGIS search](https://www.arcgis.com/sharing/rest/search?q=%22judgment%20lien%22&f=json)). A federated Socrata catalog search found no county-recorder judgment index on any domain. The judgment-bearing hits there were government-creditor sets that are already known ([Socrata catalog](https://datacatalog.cookcountyil.gov/api/catalog/v1?q=judgment&only=dataset)). The one new reachable recorder-derived layer is **City of Tucson "Recorder Liens May 2019 2024"**. It has 517 code-enforcement liens with owner names, address, recording date, `Amount_Due`, payment status and a Pima County Recorder document link, last edited 2024-10 ([Tucson layer](https://services3.arcgis.com/9coHY2fvuFjG9HQX/arcgis/rest/services/Recorder_Liens_May_2019_2024/FeatureServer/0)). Missouri's child-support lien list on data.mo.gov is a vehicle-title lien set keyed by VIN, not a real-property judgment index ([data.mo.gov 7t5a-79ri](https://data.mo.gov/d/7t5a-79ri)).

Florida is the state where a free county index is most plausible, because Fla. Stat. 28.2221 forces clerks to put official records online. In practice only Hillsborough (already loaded) is confirmed free. **Broward** publishes daily pipe-delimited index extracts and images on FTP, covering a rolling 7 to 10 days, at an unconfirmed price ([Broward bulk data](https://www.broward.org/RecordsTaxesTreasury/Records/Pages/IndexFiles-Completed.aspx)). Miami-Dade, Lake, Palm Beach and St. Lucie sell subscriptions, at $110 a month per folder in Miami-Dade and $900 a year in Lake ([Miami-Dade FTP](https://www2.miamidadeclerk.gov/Developers/FTP); [Lake Clerk](https://www.lakecountyclerkfl.gov/?p=5362)). Florida's Department of State offers daily **federal tax lien** bulk files on `sftp.floridados.gov`. These are not judgments, and access uses a published shared credential. That is a login in form if not in substance, so the source needs a ruling against the brief before anyone relies on it. **Judgment-lien certificates under Fla. Stat. 55.202 are not in the bulk set** ([Sunbiz data downloads](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/other-services/data-downloads/)).

Elsewhere the statute creates the index and the clerk sells it. Texas clerks must record abstracts of judgment in the real-property records, yet county bulk data is sold. Fort Bend, for example, keeps a list of bulk purchasers ([Tex. Prop. Code 52.004](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/property-code/prop-sect-52-004/); [Fort Bend bulk sales](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/fort-bend-county-district-clerks-office/bulk-data-sales)). Ohio clerks must index certificates of judgment under ORC 2329.02. Franklin County requires a public-records request for data files and warns that "efforts to mine large quantities of data… will be detected and stopped" ([ORC 2329.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2329.02); [Franklin CIO](https://www.franklincountyohio.gov/Service-Finder-I-Want-To/Find-Court-CIO)). Philadelphia prices its judgment databases, and AOPC charges recurring fees for court data ([Phila fee schedule](https://www.courts.phila.gov/pdf/accesspolicy/Fee-Schedule-Public-Access.pdf); [AOPC](https://www.pacourts.us/page/1172)). Lancaster County's widely reported "free download" appears to be an in-office flash-drive service ([RCFP](https://www.rcfp.org/civil-records-free-download-pennsylvania-county/)). Georgia's **GSCCCA lien index** covers all 159 counties, with FiFa and judgment liens, since 2004. It is subscription-only, but it is the single most efficient paid target, because one contract replaces 159 records requests ([GSCCCA lien index](https://gsccca.org/learn/search-systems/lien-index)).

## Licences, sealing and FCRA

Three separate legal problems attach to these sources, and none is solved by the data being free. The first is **licence**. The OCA tables are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which bars commercial reuse without separate permission from the licensor ([OCA README](https://github.com/housing-data-coalition/oca)). The Cleveland, Oregon, Debt Collection Lab and OSCN repos carry **no licence at all**, which means all rights are reserved by default. The underlying court facts are public, but copying the compiled files wholesale is not clearly permitted. VirginiaJusticeData is the one cleanly licensed named set (CC BY 4.0), and it is criminal. Access terms are also tightening: Case Harvester moved to licensed access, virginiacourtdata.org gates names behind approval, and Oklahoma bars bulk distribution of court data, which clouds any OSCN derivative ([Case Harvester](https://github.com/dismantl/CaseHarvester); [OK 2009 order](https://law.justia.com/cases/oklahoma/supreme-court/2009/457676.html)).

The second is **sealing**. **17 states plus DC (Stateline, 2024), or 19 plus DC (NLIHC, 2026)**, now seal at least some eviction records. California automatically seals unlawful-detainer cases unless the landlord wins within 60 days, and Maryland enacted sealing in 2024 ([Stateline](https://stateline.org/2024/08/21/landlords-cry-foul-as-more-states-seal-eviction-records/); [NLIHC 2026](https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/AG-2026/CH7/2_Eviction_Record_Sealing_Expungement_Chapter_7.pdf); [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/maryland-seals-eviction-records/728992/)). Frozen scrapes are the riskiest category, because a 2016 Cleveland or 2020 Oregon row may since have been sealed or set aside, and reloading it resurfaces a record the court withdrew. Ohio and Oregon sealing rules were not verified, so any load of those repos should be checked against the current docket first. The third is **FCRA**. Any output used for tenant screening, credit, insurance or employment decisions can make the operator a consumer reporting agency. Such an agency may not report civil judgments more than seven years old, and must follow reasonable procedures to ensure accuracy. Stale and sealed eviction rows violate both rules at once ([15 U.S.C. 1681c](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c); [15 U.S.C. 1681e](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681e)). The research notes did not fetch these statutes in this session. Their application to a judgment-purchase graph, as distinct from a screening product, depends on how outputs are used and needs counsel's review. Smaller flags apply too: Scofftow cannot be joined to names under DPPA, and Franklin County OH forbids bulk mining of its portal.

## Conclusion

This round closes off a hope rather than opening a channel. If named civil judgments existed anywhere in free bulk, the researchers who scraped them would be the likeliest republishers. Instead every serious project either de-identified its data or moved it behind approval, and the only named holdouts are stale, unlicensed and exposed to sealing. So the useful work shifts from finding datasets to **enriching what is already loaded**. The OCA amounts can calibrate expected recovery by New York zip. The Debt Collection Lab plaintiff-amount pairs show which creditors win default judgments, and for how much. The recovery-fund and client-protection lists become a cheap blocklist of licensees whose judgments will not pay. Genuinely new named inventory comes down to two bets. The first is the raw `ww2.nycourts.gov` XML, the one allowlist test that could change the picture. The second is a deliberate records-request programme aimed at Texas abstract indexes, Ohio certificate-of-judgment indexes and Pennsylvania prothonotaries, plus a cost-benefit look at the Georgia GSCCCA subscription as the only single contract that buys a whole state.
