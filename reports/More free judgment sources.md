# Detroit and New Jersey lead the free judgment frontier

Beyond what is already loaded, the best free bulk debtor-judgment source that is reachable and verified today is **Detroit's Department of Appeals and Hearings (DAH) Blight Tickets layer**. It has **915,218 adjudicated civil-violation records** and is the only new source that carries all five fields a judgment buyer wants: a money judgment, the property owner's name and mailing address, a parcel id, the judgment amount, and a balance-due and payment status. Close behind are **New Jersey Department of Labor's two monthly Excel lists** of final judgments and orders against employers, which give name, address, amount and (on one list) paid-or-owed status, and the **federal FJC Integrated Database copy on CourtListener's open S3 bucket**. That copy holds **413,510 federal civil cases won by the plaintiff with money received**, but names are truncated, there are no addresses, nothing was filed after March 2022, and only 23,419 of those cases ended in 2016 or later. Everything else that is reachable now is either a municipal code or tax lien keyed to a parcel (Miami-Dade, New Orleans, Louisville, Baltimore, Philadelphia, LA County, Maricopa) or a lead feed without amounts (Fulton County GA). No state publishes general private civil money judgments in free bulk, and no city portal outside New York publishes lis pendens or civil judgment dockets. The upside left is behind blocked hosts. The single most valuable host to allowlist is **Harris County Justice Courts' public extract service** (`jpwebsite.harriscountytx.gov`), the only plausible free source of private consumer-debt judgments with debtor addresses outside New York. It is followed by Pennsylvania L&I's lien lists (`www.pa.gov`), Florida DOR's monthly warrant and lien list (`floridarevenue.com`), and `data.texas.gov`.

Detroit's layer is the closest thing outside New York to the OATH judgment feed already loaded. The layer describes blight tickets as "adjudicated as civil cases by the Department of Appeals and Hearings." Each row carries `property_owner_name` with the owner's full mailing address, `parcel_id` (present on **890,691 rows**), `amt_judgment`, `amt_payment` and `amt_balance_due`. **374,145 tickets still have a balance due**, about 585k ended in a "Responsible" disposition, and 220,784 are marked paid in full ([Detroit ArcGIS layer](https://services2.arcgis.com/qvkbeam7Wirps6zC/arcgis/rest/services/blight_tickets/FeatureServer/0)). Two uncertainties matter before buying against it. First, it is not settled whether an unpaid DAH judgment becomes a lien on the parcel or is added to the tax roll under Michigan law. Second, the count of rows with a populated `judgment_date` could not be established, because the date filter failed. The debtor is the owner of record, so a name match also confirms that the debtor owns the parcel. That makes Detroit a debtor-discovery feed as well as a competing-lien feed.

New Jersey's labor lists are smaller but legally cleaner. The Wage Violation Watchlist, required by N.J.S.A. 34:11-58.5, lists each employer with address, final wages assessed, fees and penalties, the date of the final order and judgment, and a status. It shows **840 "Wages Still Owed" and 498 "Paid in Full"**, and paid employers stay on the list, which gives a rare free satisfaction signal ([NJDOL Watchlist](https://www.nj.gov/labor/ea/osec/wageviolationlist.shtml)). WALL covers unemployment and disability-insurance contributions (133), wage-and-hour (150), workers'-comp penalties (127) and gross income tax (16), totalling **$37.76M owed under final judgment or order** ([NJDOL WALL](https://www.nj.gov/labor/ea/osec/wall.shtml); [file](https://www.nj.gov/labor/ea/assets/PDFs/Wall_Dataset.xlsx)). That NJDOL certificates of debt are docketed as Superior Court judgments is plausible but was not verified for these lists. NJ Taxation's Top Debtors PDFs are expressly tied to certificates of debt that have "the same force and effect as a Docketed Judgment," with Superior Court DJ numbers. They have not changed since 2019–2020, however, so treat them as retired ([NJ Top Debtors](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/jdgdiscl.shtml)).

## Federal court data is free but thin, old and addressless

The CourtListener bulk bucket needs no login. Its quarterly snapshot (latest 2026-09-30) includes a full copy of the Federal Judicial Center's Integrated Database: **10,323,280 rows in a 280 MB file** ([S3 listing](https://com-courtlistener-storage.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=bulk-data/); [IDB file](https://com-courtlistener-storage.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/bulk-data/fjc-integrated-database-2026-09-30.csv.bz2)). Filtering for judgment for the plaintiff with a positive amount received yields **413,510 cases**. **147,835 of them have the United States as plaintiff**, which makes them candidates for a federal judgment lien under 28 USC 3201. That lien attaches to real property only once an abstract is filed where a federal tax lien notice would be, so the IDB identifies candidates but cannot prove a lien exists ([28 USC 3201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/3201)). The weaknesses are severe for a buyer. Names are truncated to about 30 characters, there is no address (only county FIPS), there is no satisfaction field, and the copy stops at filings through 2022-03-31. Only **23,419 qualifying cases terminated in 2016 or later**, so most of the pool is near or past enforceability horizons. The CourtListener `dockets` file (5.1 GB) adds case names and a join key to the IDB, but the party, attorney and docket-entry tables are not in the bulk export ([load script](https://com-courtlistener-storage.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/bulk-data/load-bulk-data-2026-09-30.sh)). The licence is also unsettled: the bulk page carries a Public Domain Mark, but the Free Law Project footer says CC BY-ND 4.0. The terms host was blocked, so commercial reuse needs a check before building ([Bulk Legal Data](https://courtlistener.com/help/api/bulk-data/)).

Every other federal source with debtor names was on a blocked host and is known only from search. EPA ECHO's ICIS-FE&C download includes `CASE_DEFENDANTS` and `CASE_PENALTIES` files with Superfund cost recovery. Under CERCLA 107(l), those cost-recovery cases point to liens on the cleaned-up property itself ([ECHO download summary](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads/icis-fec-download-summary); [EPA lien guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2013-09/documents/fed-sflien-mem.pdf)). SBA's 7(a)/504 FOIA loan files, updated quarterly, appear to carry borrower name, address and charge-off status. That would make them a strong distressed-debtor signal, though they are not judgments ([data.sba.gov](https://data.sba.gov/dataset/7a-504-foia)). DOL Wage and Hour compliance actions list back wages and penalties assessed since FY2005, but those are assessments rather than judgments, and the new API reportedly needs a free key ([data.gov WHD](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/wage-and-hour-division-compliance-action-data)). IRS tax liens, Treasury delinquent-debt data, FINRA disciplinary data and NLRB backpay are not published in bulk.

## Municipal lien layers net down equity more than they find debtors

Most new city and county finds are senior liens on the parcel, not private judgments. They matter because property-tax and many code liens rank ahead of a judgment lien and reduce the equity a buyer can reach. **Miami-Dade's Code Compliance Lien View** is the best-documented recorded lien set. It carries the folio, the Official Records book, page and recording date, and statuses including **1,190 "Lien Satisfied" and 308 "Lien Cancelled"**. Sister layers in the same org hold 15,222 cases referred to collections and 8,439 building-violation liens ([Miami-Dade layer](https://services.arcgis.com/8Pc9XBTAsYuxx9Ny/arcgis/rest/services/CodeComplianceViolation_Lien_View/FeatureServer/0)). A separate Miami-Dade `data_judgement` layer has 27,186 rows with `OWNER`, `FOLIO_NUMBER` and judgment and attorney fees, but its purpose (possibly solid-waste fee judgments) is unverified. New Orleans adds **17,660 adjudication cases with unpaid fees totalling $52.1M**, named and addressed. The city describes these as in-rem judgments that close "when the in rem judgement is paid" ([NOLA cases](https://data.nola.gov/d/wy29-i338); [data.gov listing](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/code-enforcement-active-pipeline)). Its sheriff-sale lien-foreclosure file names defendants and Civil District Court case numbers but stopped updating in 2018 ([NOLA d52w-8nva](https://data.nola.gov/d/d52w-8nva)). Louisville publishes 513 code-enforcement final orders with owner name, mailing address and citation amount, and 3,624 Metro lien foreclosures with Jefferson Circuit case numbers ([final orders](https://services1.arcgis.com/79kfd2K6fskCAkyg/arcgis/rest/services/lien_notification_final_orders/FeatureServer/0); [foreclosures](https://services1.arcgis.com/79kfd2K6fskCAkyg/arcgis/rest/services/Louisville_Metro_KY_Property_Foreclosures/FeatureServer/0)).

The tax-lien rolls are large but mostly lack owner names. LA County's Secured Defaulted Tax Roll has **1,746,758 rows** with redemption amounts by AIN, and its special-assessment lien roll has 2.43M rows ([LA defaulted roll](https://services.arcgis.com/RmCCgQtiZLDCtblq/arcgis/rest/services/Secured%20Defaulted%20Tax%20Roll/FeatureServer/0)). Maricopa lists 34,594 delinquent parcels by APN with no amounts ([Maricopa layer](https://services.arcgis.com/ykpntM6e3tHvzKRJ/arcgis/rest/services/ParcelLienDelinquent/FeatureServer)). Baltimore's 43,736 tax-lien-certificate-sale rows do name owners with block/lot, amounts and redemption dates, but they end at the 2023 sale ([Baltimore layer](https://services1.arcgis.com/UWYHeuuJISiGmgXx/arcgis/rest/services/Tax_Lien_Certificate_Sale_Properties1/FeatureServer/0)). Philadelphia's Carto `real_estate_tax_balances` table is separate from the RTT data already loaded. It names owners by OPA parcel, with $451M due and 579,765 rows carrying a lien number, but tax periods stop at 2016 ([Carto SQL](https://phl.carto.com/api/v2/sql?q=SELECT%20*%20FROM%20real_estate_tax_balances%20LIMIT%205)). On the court side, Fulton County GA's daily Odyssey extract of pending Superior Court cases gives "X vs Y" captions, filing dates and status, but no amounts. It is a lead feed whose rows drop out when cases close ([Fulton u8gi-zru8](https://sharefulton.fultoncountyga.gov/d/u8gi-zru8)). CT DEEP's 243 formal enforcement orders with respondent and penalty are reachable on data.ct.gov, but they are penalties, not liens ([CT DEEP t2bf-45ba](https://data.ct.gov/d/t2bf-45ba)).

Four more hosts would raise confidence across the board. `api.us.socrata.com` and `catalog.data.gov` would allow a full re-sweep of state Socrata portals, most of which refused connections. `www.courtlistener.com` and `wiki.free.law` would settle the CourtListener bulk licence. DOL's `enforcedata.dol.gov`, `data.dol.gov` and `apiprod.dol.gov` matter only if a free API key is acceptable under the brief. Several high-value-looking sources fail the brief outright and should not be chased by allowlisting: Illinois's and Massachusetts's delinquent-taxpayer lists, the Mississippi and South Carolina statewide tax-lien registries, and Pennsylvania's myPATH lien publication are all search portals. LSC's Civil Court Data Initiative and Eviction Lab share case-level data only under agreements. ProPublica's debt-collection data requires accepting its terms. Oklahoma bars bulk distribution of court data. Miami-Dade, Palm Beach, Lee and Lake clerks sell their index feeds ([LSC FAQ](https://civilcourtdata.lsc.gov/about/faq); [OK 2009 order](https://law.justia.com/cases/oklahoma/supreme-court/2009/457676.html); [Miami-Dade Clerk](https://www.miamidadeclerk.gov/clerk/commercial-data-services.page)). The strongest statewide lien sources, the MS and SC registries, are reachable only through a public-records request for an extract.

## Conclusion

The search changes what "more judgments" can realistically mean. Free bulk data outside New York yields government-creditor judgments: municipal code adjudications, state labor and tax judgments, and federal cases where the United States is plaintiff. It does not yield the private consumer and commercial judgments that make up a buyer's usual inventory. Detroit and New Jersey can be loaded today and parcel-joined the way OATH already is. But they are mostly claims a city or state holds and will not sell, so their main value is as debtor discovery and as a map of competing senior liens on a target parcel. The one clear path to private money judgments in free bulk runs through Texas justice courts, which makes allowlisting `jpwebsite.harriscountytx.gov` and reading its file layout the highest-return next step. After that, the remaining gap closes not through open data but through records requests: Florida clerks under Chapter 119, California superior-court case indexes (Santa Clara sells a quarterly index with party names for a nominal fee), and Texas county clerks' statutorily required abstract-of-judgment indexes ([Santa Clara index](https://santaclara.courts.ca.gov/online-services/case-information-online/requesting-cd-civil-case-index); [Tex. Prop. Code 52.004](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/property-code/prop-sect-52-004/)).
