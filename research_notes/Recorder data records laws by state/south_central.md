# Bulk county recorder index data under public records laws: AL, MS, TN, KY, AR, LA, OK, TX

Research method note: fetching full pages was blocked in this session. Several hosts failed to resolve: ag.ky.gov, capitol.texas.gov and alison.legislature.state.al.us. Every finding below therefore comes from web-search result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full text. Statements tagged **[prior knowledge, unverified this session]** come from the researcher's background knowledge and need checking against the official statute before anyone relies on them. No agency was contacted.

Which office records deeds in each state:
- AL: probate judge.
- MS: chancery clerk.
- TN: register of deeds.
- KY: county clerk.
- AR: circuit clerk, acting ex officio as recorder **[prior knowledge, unverified]**.
- LA: parish clerk of court; in Orleans, the civil clerk.
- OK: county clerk.
- TX: county clerk.

## 1. Public records statute name and citation

### Takeaway
All eight states have a general public records act. In AL, TN, AR and KY, the general act restricts who may request. In MS, LA, OK and TX it is open to any person **[MS/LA/OK/TX "any person" wording is prior knowledge; TX/OK partly supported below]**.

### Cited Findings
- **Alabama**: Open Records Act, Code of Ala. § 36-12-40. The 2024 amendment, SB 270, enacted as Act 2024-278, added procedures codified at §§ 36-12-40 through 36-12-46 — [Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/05/13/alabama-enacts-law-requiring-state-agencies-to-respond-to-open-records-requests/); [Act 2024-278 PDF (SHPDA copy)](http://shpda.alabama.gov/Act2024-278.pdf); [SB270 enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2024RS/SB270-enr.pdf)
- **Mississippi**: Mississippi Public Records Act of 1983, Miss. Code Ann. §§ 25-61-1 et seq. — [Justia, Title 25 Ch. 61](https://law.justia.com/codes/mississippi/title-25/chapter-61/); [MS Ethics Commission](https://www.ethics.ms.gov/thepublicrecordact)
- **Tennessee**: Tennessee Public Records Act, T.C.A. §§ 10-7-501 et seq. (core provision § 10-7-503). Related provisions are § 10-7-123 (electronic or remote access) and § 10-7-506 (commercial-value records) — [Justia § 10-7-123](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-10/chapter-7/part-1/section-10-7-123/); [Justia § 10-7-506](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-10/chapter-7/part-5/section-10-7-506)
- **Kentucky**: Open Records Act, KRS 61.870–61.884. The fee section is KRS 61.874 — [KY Legislature, KRS 61.874](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=23061); [KY AG ORA/OMA guide](https://www.ag.ky.gov/AG%20Publications/Open%20Records%20Open%20Meetings%20Act%20Guide%20per%20KRS%2015.257.pdf)
- **Arkansas**: Freedom of Information Act of 1967, Ark. Code Ann. §§ 25-19-101 et seq. (§ 25-19-105 covers access and fees; § 25-19-109 covers special electronic requests) — [AR AG FOIA Handbook, 22nd ed. 2026](https://media.ark.org/ag/APA-045-2026-FOIA-Handbook-22nd-Edition.pdf); [Onecle § 25-19-109](https://law.onecle.com/arkansas/title-25/25-19-109.html)
- **Louisiana**: Public Records Law, La. R.S. 44:1 et seq. (§ 44:31 covers the right of access; § 44:32 covers fees) **[section-level detail is prior knowledge]**. Statewide clerk remote access is governed by La. R.S. 13:754 — [Plaquemines Parish Clerk, LCRAA page](https://clerk25th.com/mortgage-conveyances-ucc/louisiana-clerks-remote-access-authority-lcraa/)
- **Oklahoma**: Oklahoma Open Records Act, 51 O.S. §§ 24A.1 et seq. (§ 24A.5 covers inspection, copying and fees) — [Justia 51 O.S. 24A.5](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/2014/title-51/section-51-24a.5/); [RCFP Oklahoma](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/oklahoma/)
- **Texas**: Public Information Act, Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552 — [Gov't Code ch. 552 (statutes.capitol.texas.gov)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/sotwdocs/gv/htm/gv.552.htm)

### Inferences
- In every state, a separate recorder or clerk fee statute sits alongside the general act. That fee statute, not the general act, usually sets the price of bulk land-record data (see Q2 and Q7).

### Gaps
- Current official statute text was not read for any state because fetches were blocked. The 2025–2026 session laws for LA, MS and AR were not checked.

## 2. Fee rules: duplication-only vs. staff/programming; does a recorder-specific fee statute override?

### Takeaway
Texas, Arkansas and (for non-commercial requests) Oklahoma come closest to a cost-of-duplication-only rule. Mississippi allows actual cost including staff time. Kentucky allows extra fees for commercial requests. Tennessee and Alabama allow "reasonable" fees set by the office. Recorder-specific statutes generally govern paper copies, and in several states they also authorize "reasonable" electronic-data or subscription fees.

### Cited Findings
- **TX**: Local Gov't Code § 118.011(e) provides that when a county clerk supplies a non-paper copy of a record it maintains, including real property records, the fee is set under Gov't Code §§ 552.231 and 552.262. Paper copies and filing fees stay on the Chapter 118 schedule. So for electronic copies, the PIA cost rules (the AG's cost rules under 552.262) control, not the per-page clerk fee — [texas.public.law § 118.011](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._local_gov't_code_section_118.011); [FindLaw § 118.011](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/local-government-code/loc-gov-t-sect-118-011/)
- **TX**: A House bill analysis of SB 1547 (89th Leg., 2025) says county clerks charge $0.10 per page for electronic copies of real property records. The bill would exclude real property records from the mandatory electronic-copy fee and tie charges for non-paper records to the PIA and AG rules. Enactment status was not confirmed — [SB 1547 House bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB01547H.htm)
- **TX**: Under § 552.231, if a request requires programming or "manipulation" of data, the body must give a written statement of the estimated cost under AG rules within 20 days, extendable by 10 days. "Manipulation" means modifying, reordering or decoding information with human intervention. An older AG cost presentation shows a programming rate of $28.50/hr — [texas.public.law § 552.231](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_552.231); [AG "Cost Basics" presentation](https://texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/files/divisions/open-government/conference/12-3CostBasics.pdf)
- **TX**: AG opinion GA-0152 (2004) reads the clerk's fee authority under § 118.011 narrowly. KP-0496 (Aug. 29, 2025) addresses the § 118.0216 records-management fee — [GA-0152](https://texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/opinion-files/opinion/2004/ga0152.pdf); [TX AG county clerks category](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/open-records/categories/county-clerks)
- **AR**: Copy fees under § 25-19-105(d)(3)(A)(i) are limited to actual reproduction costs (media, supplies, equipment, maintenance). Existing personnel time is excluded "unless § 25-19-109 or another law provides otherwise." If the custodian agrees to a special electronic request under § 25-19-109(b), it may charge actual, verifiable personnel time beyond 2 hours, at no more than the salary of the lowest-paid qualified employee, itemized. It may require advance payment over $25 — [AR FOIA Handbook](https://static.ark.org/eeuploads/arhim/HANDOUT_1_-_AR_Freedom_of_Information_Handbook.pdf); [FindLaw § 25-19-109](https://codes.findlaw.com/ar/title-25-state-government/ar-code-sect-25-19-109/); [AR AG Handbook 2026](https://media.ark.org/ag/APA-045-2026-FOIA-Handbook-22nd-Edition.pdf)
- **MS**: § 25-61-7 allows "actual cost" of searching, reviewing and copying. Staff or contract time is charged at the rate of the lowest-level competent employee or contractor. Fees are collected in advance. The statute also lets the body consider "the purpose or purposes for which the information has been requested and the commercial value of the information" — [RCFP Mississippi](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/mississippi/); [Justia MS ch. 61](https://law.justia.com/codes/mississippi/title-25/chapter-61/)
- **OK**: § 24A.5 caps copies at $0.25/page (8.5x14 or smaller). A search fee is allowed only if the request is solely for a commercial purpose or would cause excessive disruption. No search fee is allowed when release is in the public interest. Fee schedules must be posted. One source says no per-page fee may be charged for computer-readable electronic records — [Ballotpedia OK](https://ballotpedia.org/Oklahoma_FOIA_procedures); [Justia 24A.5](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/2014/title-51/section-51-24a.5/); [RCFP Oklahoma](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/oklahoma/)
- **OK**: The recorder statute, 19 O.S. § 245, lets the county clerk furnish copies for a fee under the Open Records Act or the county-clerk schedule at 28 O.S. § 32. "If the clerk provides records in an electronic format, the clerk may charge a reasonable fee." — [Justia 19 O.S. 245 (2023)](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-19/section-19-245/); [Justia 28 O.S. 32](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-28/section-28-32/)
- **OK**: Enrolled SB 535 (2025–26) amends § 24A.5, including allowing advance payment for certain requests. The final text and effective date were not confirmed — [SB 535 ENR](https://www.oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2025-26%20ENR/SB/SB535%20ENR.PDF)
- **KY**: KRS 61.874 allows a reasonable fee for copies requested for commercial purposes. The RCFP adds that if inspection costs the agency nothing, no fee may be charged even for commercial requesters. County clerks cite KRS 64.012 and 64.019 for their fees, and require commercial requesters to certify and sign a contract that sets the fee, "including labor and production costs" — [KRS 61.874](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=23061); [RCFP Kentucky](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/kentucky/); [Henry County Clerk policy](https://henrycountyclerk.ky.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Open-Records-Policy-Henry.pdf); [Warren County Clerk](https://warrencountyclerk.ky.gov/services/public-records/open-records-requests/)
- **TN**: The Comptroller's Office of Open Records Counsel (OORC) Schedule of Reasonable Charges does not vary charges by the requester's purpose. Purpose-based fees come from other statutes such as § 10-7-506(c) — [MTAS: Schedule of Reasonable Charges](https://mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/mrln/mknowledge/reference/20170119ScheduleofReasonableCharges.pdf); [CTAS: Records with Commercial Value](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/records-commercial-value)
- **AL**: The 2024 act lets an officer charge a reasonable fee that the officer sets, and withhold a substantive response until it is paid. Reporting notes the act does not cap fees. A $0.50/page cap and a no-fee-for-electronic-documents rule come from an executive order, which applies to executive agencies, not county probate offices — [Alabama Reflector, May 2024](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/05/06/alabama-house-approves-mandatory-response-times-for-public-records-requests/); [Smarsh summary](https://www.smarsh.com/blog/regulations/alabama-open-records-act/)
- **AL**: Sample probate fees: Hale County charges $1.00/page for copies. Montgomery County lists a $5.00 real-property index fee. Mobile County charges $0.10/page for images — [Hale County fee schedule](https://www.halecountyal.gov/sites/default/files/hale_county_al_fees_11.25.pdf); [Montgomery Probate fees](https://montgomeryprobatecourtal.gov/divisions/records-recording/records-recording-fees); [Mobile Probate records search](https://probate.mobilecountyal.gov/public-records/records-search)

### Inferences
- **TX** is the strongest legal footing for near-free bulk data. Section 118.011(e) moves non-paper real-property copies under the PIA cost rules. Under those rules, an export from an existing database is priced at programming/labor plus media, not $1/page. The main risk is a § 552.231 programming estimate.
- **OK** is mixed. The judgment-buying use would likely count as "commercial," which permits a search fee. And 19 O.S. § 245's "reasonable fee" for electronic format gives clerks room to price above direct cost.
- **AL** and **TN** leave pricing largely to the office ("reasonable"). AL probate offices also have local-act authority to sell index data (Q7).

### Gaps
- The current text of Tex. Local Gov't Code § 191.008 was not retrieved. It is indexed as the authority for a computerized electronic information system; from prior knowledge, it allows commissioners-court-set fees for remote or subscription access **[unverified]**.
- The cost-rule figures in 1 Tex. Admin. Code ch. 70 were not verified for 2026.
- MS chancery clerk fee statute (Miss. Code § 25-7-9) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**.
- LA R.S. 44:32(C) fee language **[prior knowledge: "reasonable fees" set by the custodian; unverified]**.
- KRS 64.019 text was not retrieved.
- TN OORC current per-page and labor rates were not verified for 2026 **[prior knowledge: first hour of labor free; $0.15/page black and white]**.

## 3. Database extracts: existing record vs. "no duty to create"; electronic-format requirements

### Takeaway
Mississippi has the clearest statutory duty to produce records in the format they are kept. Texas requires a cost estimate for programmed extracts and treats electronic copies as covered. Arkansas makes tailored extracts discretionary. Kentucky AG precedent (Zillow v. Fayette County Clerk) favors commercial bulk requesters. Tennessee and Alabama law contain no extract duty.

### Cited Findings
- **MS**: § 25-61-10(2) requires an agency to provide a record in the requested format if it keeps the record in that format, and lets it charge under § 25-61-7. Under § 25-61-10(1) and (4), proprietary or sensitive software may not reduce public access. An agency also may not enter a database creation or maintenance contract that impairs the public's ability to inspect or copy — [FindLaw § 25-61-10](https://codes.findlaw.com/ms/title-25-public-officers-and-employees-public-records/ms-code-sect-25-61-10/); [RCFP Mississippi](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/mississippi/)
- **AR**: Under § 25-19-109(a), the custodian *may, at its discretion*, compile or tailor electronic data, or provide it in a format it is not readily convertible to. Where cost and time are "relatively minimal," custodians "should agree" — [Onecle § 25-19-109](https://law.onecle.com/arkansas/title-25/25-19-109.html); [AR AG Handbook](https://media.ark.org/ag/APA-045-2026-FOIA-Handbook-22nd-Edition.pdf)
- **TX**: § 552.231 covers requests that require programming or manipulation of data. The body must give a written cost estimate, or state that compliance is not feasible, within 20 days plus a possible 10-day extension. It does not let the body simply refuse — [texas.public.law § 552.231](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_552.231)
- **KY**: In 18-ORD-124 (2018), Zillow Group asked the Fayette County Clerk for records, and the clerk denied the commercial request. The AG found the clerk violated the Open Records Act and held its reliance on KRS 64.019 "misplaced," adding that an expansive reading of KRS 64.019 "is not legally supportable" — [18-ORD-124](https://www.ag.ky.gov/Resources/orom/2018/18ORD124.doc) (full text not readable this session; summary from search results)
- **OK**: One source summarizes the law as barring per-page fees for records kept in computer-readable format, which implies electronic production is expected. Electronic data for news media is capped at direct cost — [Ballotpedia OK](https://ballotpedia.org/Oklahoma_FOIA_procedures); [RCFP Oklahoma](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/oklahoma/)
- **TN**: Under § 10-7-123, once a county official provides a remote electronic access system, access must be offered equally to every member of the public willing to pay, including commercial users. An AG opinion applied this to a vendor uploading register-of-deeds data for its own subscription service. Remote access is for inquiry only — [Justia § 10-7-123](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-10/chapter-7/part-1/section-10-7-123/); [CTAS: Remote Access to Computerized Records](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/remote-access-computerized-records)

### Inferences
- **TX**: Gov't Code § 552.228 requires production in the requested electronic medium if technologically feasible **[prior knowledge, unverified this session]**. Combined with § 552.231, Texas is the state where a CSV index export is most clearly owed at cost.
- **MS**: "the format kept" helps if the request is for the index database itself (e.g., "an export of the land-records index table in its native or delimited format"). Phrasing the request as "provide the existing database in the format maintained" is the strongest framing.
- **AR**: because tailoring is discretionary, ask for the data "as maintained" (an existing file or export) rather than a custom extract. The AG handbook's "relatively minimal" language is useful to quote.
- **KY**: the Zillow decision is the best precedent in the region for bulk clerk data going to a commercial data aggregator. The residency rule adopted after 2021 (Q4) now limits who can rely on it.

### Gaps
- No AL, TN, LA or OK case law or AG opinion was found that squarely holds a database export to be an "existing record."
- Whether TN's § 10-7-503 has explicit "no duty to create" language **[prior knowledge: TN courts and OORC say no duty to create a record; unverified]**.
- Arkansas Supreme Court cases on electronic formats were not located.
- The full holding of 18-ORD-124 (format, fee amount) could not be read.

## 4. Residency restrictions

### Takeaway
AL, TN, AR and KY restrict requests to residents or citizens. MS, LA, OK and TX do not **[non-restriction for MS/LA/OK/TX is prior knowledge]**. Kentucky's definition admits businesses with a Kentucky location or registered with the KY Secretary of State.

### Cited Findings
- **AL**: SB 270 (2024) defines a qualifying requester as a person permanently domiciled in Alabama with an expectation to remain, and lets agencies require proof of residency. The AL Secretary of State describes § 36-12-40 as making records available to Alabama citizens — [Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/05/06/alabama-house-approves-mandatory-response-times-for-public-records-requests/); [AL SOS public records request](https://www.sos.alabama.gov/public-records-request); [SB270 enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2024RS/SB270-enr.pdf)
- **KY**: Since June 29, 2021 (HB 312, Acts ch. 160), records are open to "any resident of the Commonwealth." KRS 61.870(10) defines a resident as:
  - an individual residing in KY;
  - a domestic business entity with a KY location;
  - a foreign business entity registered with the KY Secretary of State;
  - a news-gathering organization.
  Agencies may deny a request that lacks a residency statement — [KY AG 2021 advisory](https://ag.ky.gov/Documents/21.06.24%20-%20Open%20Records%20and%20Open%20Meeting%20Advisory%20%28final%29.pdf); [Jackson Kelly summary](https://jacksonkelly.com/health-law-monitor-blog/2021-changes-to-kentuckys-open-records-act); [HB 312 record](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/21RS/HB312.html); [2023 KY AG guide](https://www.ag.ky.gov/AG%20Publications/2023%20Open%20Records%20Open%20Meetings%20Guide.pdf)
- **AR**: § 25-19-105 refers to records being open to "any citizen of the State of Arkansas" — [AR FOIA Handbook](https://static.ark.org/eeuploads/arhim/HANDOUT_1_-_AR_Freedom_of_Information_Handbook.pdf); [RCFP Arkansas](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/arkansas/)
- **TN**: Remote access under § 10-7-123 must be equally available to all members of the public willing to pay, including commercial users. That is a separate route from the § 10-7-503 records request — [Justia § 10-7-123](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-10/chapter-7/part-1/section-10-7-123/)

### Inferences
- **KY**: a nonresident small business could qualify by registering as a foreign entity with the KY Secretary of State. That costs money and needs the owner's approval. A nonresident individual cannot qualify.
- **TN**: records requests under § 10-7-503 are limited to Tennessee citizens **[prior knowledge: § 10-7-503(a)(2)(A); upheld in McBurney v. Young, 569 U.S. 221 (2013), a Virginia case]**. But paid remote access under § 10-7-123 has no citizenship limit, so nonresidents can still subscribe.
- **AR**: the citizens-only rule matches McBurney. Whether Arkansas custodians enforce it against out-of-state requesters varies in practice (not verified).
- **AL**: residency can be demanded with proof. A request "honestly in a real person's name" from a non-Alabamian can be refused.

### Gaps
- Exact current text of the residency clauses in T.C.A. § 10-7-503(a)(2)(A) and Ark. Code § 25-19-105(a)(1)(A) was not retrieved.
- Whether AL's 2024 domicile definition reaches county probate judges, or only state agencies, is unclear (see Q6).

## 5. Commercial-purpose restrictions, certification or surcharges

### Takeaway
Kentucky (certification, contract and commercial fees) and Oklahoma (search fee) have explicit commercial-purpose regimes. Mississippi lets "commercial value" factor into fees. Tennessee surcharges commercial-value GIS/map data. Texas, Arkansas and Louisiana have no general commercial surcharge in the records act **[TX/AR/LA absence is prior knowledge]**.

### Cited Findings
- **KY**: KRS 61.874(4) allows a reasonable fee for commercial-purpose copies. County clerks require a certified statement of the commercial purpose and a signed contract that sets the fee, including labor and production costs. Examples: Warren, Henry, Meade, McLean and Rowan County Clerks — [KRS 61.874](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=23061); [Warren County Clerk](https://warrencountyclerk.ky.gov/services/public-records/open-records-requests/); [McLean County Clerk policy](https://mcleancountyclerk.ky.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Open-Records-Policy.pdf); [Meade County Clerk policy](https://meadecountyclerk.ky.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Open-Records-Policy-Meade.pdf); [Rowan County Clerk](https://rowancountyclerk.ky.gov/records/open-records-policy/)
- **OK**: A reasonable search fee for direct search cost is allowed if the request is "solely for a commercial purpose." Publication by news media is not commercial — [Ballotpedia OK](https://ballotpedia.org/Oklahoma_FOIA_procedures); [RCFP Oklahoma](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/oklahoma/)
- **MS**: § 25-61-7 lets fees reflect "the purpose or purposes for which the information has been requested and the commercial value of the information" — [RCFP Mississippi](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/mississippi/)
- **TN**: § 10-7-506(c) allows fees above copying cost for computer-generated maps and similar geographic data developed with public funds, when the request is for a record with "commercial value." That covers anything other than an individual's non-business use or news-gathering. Local GIS owners may recover maintenance and part of development costs — [Justia § 10-7-506](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-10/chapter-7/part-5/section-10-7-506); [CTAS GIS Records](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/geographic-information-systems-records); [Comptroller OORC Adv. Op. 09-02](https://www.comptroller.tn.gov/content/dam/cot/orc/documents/oorc/advisory-opinions/09-02_MemphisDailyNews.pdf)

### Inferences
- A judgment-buying business is "commercial" under every one of these definitions. The requests should disclose this honestly where certification is required (KY). In OK, expect a search fee.
- **TN**: § 10-7-506(c) targets maps and geographic data. A grantor/grantee index is probably not "geographic data," but clerks may try to stretch it (unverified).

### Gaps
- The KY commercial-fee components under KRS 61.874(4)(c) (cost of creation, acquisition, staff) **[prior knowledge, unverified]**.
- No OK AG opinion on bulk clerk data for commercial use was located.

## 6. Response deadlines

### Takeaway
KY: 5 business days. TX: "promptly," with 10 business days to seek an AG ruling and 20 days for a § 552.231 cost statement. AR: promptly, 3 working days if the record is in use or storage. AL: 2024 statutory timelines that may cover only state agencies. MS, TN, LA and OK details are partly unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **KY**: 5 business days, up from 3, under HB 312 (2021) — [Jackson Kelly](https://jacksonkelly.com/health-law-monitor-blog/2021-changes-to-kentuckys-open-records-act); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/fiscal-monetary-policy/1081656/amendments-to-kentucky-open-records-act-set-to-become-effective-june-29-2021)
- **AL**: The 2024 act sets acknowledgment within 10 business days and a substantive response within 15 business days for standard requests. Sources disagree on business versus calendar days and on extensions. Headlines describe it as requiring *state agencies* to respond — [Alabama Reflector (enacted)](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/05/13/alabama-enacts-law-requiring-state-agencies-to-respond-to-open-records-requests/); [Alabama Reflector (House passage)](https://alabamareflector.com/2024/05/06/alabama-house-approves-mandatory-response-times-for-public-records-requests/)
- **TX**: The § 552.231 written cost statement is due within 20 days, extendable by 10 — [texas.public.law § 552.231](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_552.231)
- **AR**, **MS**, **OK**: procedures are summarized at [Ballotpedia AR](https://ballotpedia.org/Arkansas_FOIA_procedures), [Ballotpedia MS](https://ballotpedia.org/Mississippi_FOIA_procedures) and [Ballotpedia OK](https://ballotpedia.org/Oklahoma_FOIA_procedures) (specific day counts not confirmed this session)

### Inferences
- **[Prior knowledge, unverified this session]**:
  - MS: 7 working days, extendable to 14 (§ 25-61-5).
  - TN: promptly, or within 7 business days grant, deny or estimate (§ 10-7-503(a)(2)(B)).
  - AR: immediately, or within 3 working days if in active use or storage (§ 25-19-105(e)).
  - LA: immediately if available, otherwise certify within 3 business days (R.S. 44:33).
  - OK: "prompt, reasonable access," with no fixed day count (§ 24A.5(6)).
  - TX: promptly; 10 business days to request an AG decision (§ 552.301).

### Gaps
- Whether Alabama's 2024 timelines bind county probate judges was not confirmed. The headlines say "state agencies."

## 7. Recorder-specific statutes for selling bulk or subscription data; typical prices

### Takeaway
Every state in the region has statutes or local acts letting recorders charge for remote, subscription or electronic access. Typical prices run tens of dollars a month for subscriptions. Bulk index-plus-image sales are priced locally and rarely published.

### Cited Findings
- **TN**: § 10-7-123 lets registers charge remote users a uniform fee covering only the cost of providing the service, excluding storage and maintenance. Access must be equal for all payers, including commercial users. Knox County Register charges $40/month plus a $25 setup fee. Hamilton County charges $50/month — [Justia § 10-7-123](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-10/chapter-7/part-1/section-10-7-123/); [Knox County ROD subscription](https://rod.knoxcounty.org/internet-service-subscription/); [Hamilton Co. ROD](https://regssl2.hamiltontn.gov/RegisterWeb/Home/Welcome.aspx)
- **LA**: The Louisiana Clerks' Remote Access Authority (LCRAA) was created in 2014 at the clerks' request. Under La. R.S. 13:754, as amended by Act 202 of 2018, clerks must participate and collect a $5 fee per recorded instrument. Images are sold through each parish's premium subscription:
  - Jefferson Davis: $20/day or $100/month.
  - Lafayette: $20/day or $50 per 30 days.
  - Sabine: $20/day or $1,200/year.

- **AL**: A Mobile County local act lets the probate judge sell index information, electronically or in print, "at a reasonable rate to be determined by the judge of probate." An Elmore County act has the probate judge set policy on the sale of or access to computerized index information and digitized images. A 2026 act lets Tallapoosa County charge an additional remote-access fee — [Justia Code of Ala. § 45-26-81.22 (Elmore)](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-45/chapter-26/article-8/part-2/division-2/section-45-26-81-22); [SB324 (2026) enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/SB324-enr.pdf); [HB491 (2026) enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB491-enr.pdf)
- **OK**: 19 O.S. § 245 allows a "reasonable fee" for electronic-format records. The UCC filing statute, 12A O.S. § 1-9-525, has a bulk-data fee for indexed records; that applies to UCC filings, not deeds — [Justia 19 O.S. 245](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-19/section-19-245/); [Justia 12A-1-9-525](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-12a/section-12a-1-9-525/)
- **TX**: Tarrant County Clerk sells "bulk data": buyers pay through its records site and then call 817-884-1069 to complete the purchase — [Tarrant County Real Estate Records](https://www.tarrantcountytx.gov/en/county-clerk/real-estate-records.html). § 191.008 is indexed as authority to set up a computerized electronic information system — [texas.public.law ch. 191](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._local_gov't_code_title_6_subtitle_b_chapter_191)
- **KY**: County clerks use KRS 64.019 and commercial contracts to price bulk data, but 18-ORD-124 limits reliance on KRS 64.019 to restrict commercial access — [18-ORD-124](https://www.ag.ky.gov/Resources/orom/2018/18ORD124.doc); [Henry County Clerk policy](https://henrycountyclerk.ky.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Open-Records-Policy-Henry.pdf)

### Inferences
- Where a subscription exists (TN, LA, AL counties), clerks will often point requesters to it instead of producing a free export. In TN, the cost-only cap in § 10-7-123 keeps prices low ($40–$50/month) but not free.
- Texas vendor-hosted county systems (Tyler, Kofile, etc.) often have bulk-data price lists. Tarrant's process shows bulk sales exist, but its price was not found.

### Gaps
- No published bulk index price for any specific county in MS, AR, OK, KY or TX was found.
- Mississippi chancery clerk remote-access statute and Arkansas circuit clerk data-sale statute were not located.

## 8. Known free statewide or county bulk recorder/land-records data

### Takeaway
No free statewide *bulk* recorder index was found in any of the eight states. Louisiana's LCRAA portal offers free statewide *index search*, not bulk download. Mobile County, AL offers free index search.

### Cited Findings
- **LA**: Mortgage and conveyance index searches on the statewide LCRAA/eClerks portal are free; images require paid parish subscriptions. Sources disagree on coverage ("most parishes") — [Louisiana Property Records portal](https://propertyrecords.louisianaofficialrecords.com/); [Jefferson Parish Clerk](https://www.jpclerkofcourt.us/louisiana-clerks-remote-access-authority-lcraa/); [Plaquemines Clerk](https://clerk25th.com/mortgage-conveyances-ucc/louisiana-clerks-remote-access-authority-lcraa/)
- **AL**: Mobile County Probate land-records index search is free after registration; images cost $0.10/page — [Mobile Probate](https://probate.mobilecountyal.gov/public-records/records-search)
- **OK**: Oklahoma County Clerk publishes an Open Records fee schedule — [Oklahoma County Clerk](https://oklahomacounty.org/Offices/County-Clerk)

### Inferences
- **[Prior knowledge, unverified]**: OKCountyRecords.com (a Kellpro service) offers free index search across many Oklahoma counties, with images for a fee. Several large Texas counties (e.g., Dallas, Harris, Travis, Bexar) offer free online index search, but bulk downloads are generally sold.
- Free online index search makes the "existing record" argument stronger: the index plainly exists in electronic form.

### Gaps
- No verified open-data (Socrata/ArcGIS) bulk deed-index dataset was found for any county in these states this session. Texas county open-data portals were not checked.

## 9. Scores: free or near-free machine-readable recorder index via a records request (1 = very unlikely, 5 = very likely)

### Takeaway
Texas ranks highest. Mississippi and Oklahoma are middling. Louisiana and Kentucky are low. TN, AR and AL are lowest for a nonresident requester.

### Cited Findings
- Scores are built from the findings in Q2–Q8 above. See those sections for sources.

### Inferences
- **Texas: 4.** Any person may request, and § 118.011(e) puts electronic real-property copies under the PIA cost rules. The § 552.231 cost-estimate duty means an existing-database export is owed at cost; the risks are programming charges and pending fee legislation (SB 1547).
- **Mississippi: 3.** Any person may request **[unverified]**, and § 25-61-10(2) requires the format kept. But § 25-61-7 allows staff time and lets fees reflect commercial value.
- **Oklahoma: 3.** Any person may request, and copy caps are low. But a commercial-purpose search fee applies, and 19 O.S. § 245 lets clerks charge a "reasonable fee" for electronic format.
- **Louisiana: 2.** Any person may request **[unverified]**, and the statewide index is free to search. But clerks monetize data through LCRAA and parish subscriptions, and no format or extract mandate was found.
- **Kentucky: 2.** The Zillow/Fayette decision (18-ORD-124) favors bulk commercial requests. But the post-2021 residency rule blocks nonresidents unless the business is KY-registered, and commercial fees by contract apply.
- **Tennessee: 1 for a nonresident (2 for a TN citizen).** Requests are citizens-only **[unverified cite]**, and pricing is by "reasonable charges." Paid remote access at $40–$50/month (§ 10-7-123) is the realistic route.
- **Arkansas: 1 for a nonresident (2–3 for an AR citizen).** Requests are citizens-only. Tailored extracts are discretionary under § 25-19-109, with personnel charges beyond 2 hours.
- **Alabama: 1.** Requesters must be domiciled in Alabama with proof, fees are uncapped and set by the officer, and probate judges hold local-act authority to sell index data.

### Gaps
- Scores assume the requester is not a resident of AL, TN, AR or KY. If the named requester lives in one of those states, that state's score rises by roughly 1–2.
- No county in this region was found to have actually provided a free CSV deed-index export; such evidence would be needed to confirm the scores.
