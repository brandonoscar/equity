# Bulk electronic county recorder index data under public records laws: ND, SD, NE, KS, MT, WY, CO, UT

Method note (read first): in this session, WebFetch could not reach any site (DNS blocked for ndlegis.gov, kslegislature.gov, nebraskalegislature.gov, codes.findlaw.com, attorneygeneral.nd.gov). Every finding below therefore comes from **search-engine excerpts of the cited pages**, not from full-text reads. Statute wording is paraphrased or quoted from those excerpts. ## 1. Public records statute name and citation

### Takeaway
All eight states have a general "any person" public records statute. County recorder offices (register of deeds, county recorder, or county clerk and recorder) are covered. Kansas case law says so expressly for registers of deeds.

### Cited Findings
- **North Dakota**: open records law, N.D.C.C. § 44-04-18 (access, fees), with AG opinions interpreting it — [ND AG 2014-O-18](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2014-O-18.pdf); [ND AG 2008-O-18](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2008-O-18.pdf)
- **South Dakota**: SDCL ch. 1-27. Informal requests fall under § 1-27-35 and written requests under § 1-27-37 — [SDCL 1-27-37](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/1-27-37); [SDCL 1-27-35 (Justia 2011)](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-dakota/2011/title1/chapter27/1-27-35/)
- **Nebraska**: Public Records Statutes, Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 84-712 to 84-712.09. § 84-712.01 says data that is a public record in its original form remains a public record when kept in computer files — [84-712](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=84-712); [84-712.01](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=84-712.01); [NE DNR compilation, Sept. 2024](https://dnr.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/about/statutes/20240911NebraskaPublicRecordsLaw.pdf)
- **Kansas**: Kansas Open Records Act (KORA), K.S.A. 45-215 et seq. Under 45-218, "All public records shall be open for inspection by any person" — [Ballotpedia KS](https://ballotpedia.org/Kansas_FOIA_procedures); [KLRD briefing 2021](https://klrd.gov/publications/briefing-book-2021/kansas-open-records-act/). *Data Tree v. Meek* held that register of deeds records are public records under KORA — [Data Tree v. Meek](https://16jd.kscourts.gov/Cases-Decisions/Decisions/Published/Data-Tree-v-Meek)
- **Montana**: Mont. Const. art. II § 9 [background, unverified], plus MCA Title 2, ch. 6, part 10 (2-6-1001 et seq.). § 2-6-1006 covers requests and fees, and § 2-6-1007 covers special fees — [MCA 2-6-1006](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0060/0020-0060-0100-0060.html); [MCA 2-6-1007](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0070/0020-0060-0100-0070.html)
- **Wyoming**: Wyoming Public Records Act, W.S. 16-4-201 to 16-4-205 — [LSO 2019 topic summary](https://www.wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2019/01-2019060314-01LSOTopicSummary-Judiciary-PublicRecords.pdf); [16-4-202 (Justia 2022)](https://law.justia.com/codes/wyoming/2022/title-16/chapter-4/article-2/section-16-4-202/)
- **Colorado**: Colorado Open Records Act (CORA), C.R.S. 24-72-201 et seq. — [C.R.S. 24-72-205](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_24-72-205); [Legislative Council CORA summary](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/Colorado%20Open%20Records%20Act%20%28CORA%29%20-%20Colorado%20Law%20Summary-accessible.pdf)
- **Utah**: Government Records Access and Management Act (GRAMA), Utah Code 63G-2-101 et seq. — [63G-2-201 (2026 version)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter2/C63G-2-S201_2026050620260701.pdf); [63G-2-203](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter2/C63G-2-S203_2022050420220504.pdf)

### Inferences
- Coverage of recorder offices is not in doubt in any state. The real obstacles are fees, recorder-specific fee statutes, and "no duty to create or reformat" clauses.

### Gaps
- Official full text was not readable for any statute in this session.
- The Montana constitutional citation and the full Nebraska section range are from background knowledge.

## 2. Fee rules: duplication only, staff time or programming, and recorder-specific overrides

### Takeaway
No state limits fees to duplication cost only. Most allow staff time, often after a free first hour or a threshold. Recorder-specific fee statutes are the key risk. North Dakota caps electronic data extracted from recorded instruments at $0.50 per instrument. South Dakota applies per-copy register fees to electronic copies. Colorado's clerk statute lets bulk subscription fees cover direct and indirect production costs.

### Cited Findings
**North Dakota**
- Locating records: up to $25/hour per request, excluding the first hour, when locating takes more than one hour (44-04-18(2)) — [ND AG 2025-O-07 / 2014-O-18 excerpts via search](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2014-O-18.pdf)
- A "reasonable fee" for copies is the actual cost of making them, "including labor, materials, equipment, and costs attributable to the use of information technology resources" — [ND AG 2006-O-13](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2006-O-13.pdf)
- Time spent on tasks other than locating records, such as reformatting or corresponding, generally cannot be charged under 44-04-18(2) — [ND AG 2014-O-18](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2014-O-18.pdf)
- In 2008-O-18, a county included charges for electronic records that the law did not allow — [ND AG 2008-O-18](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2008-O-18.pdf)
- **Recorder override**: N.D.C.C. 11-18-05 allows "for providing any electronic data extracted from the recorded instrument, a fee of not more than fifty cents per instrument." The recorder may set procedures for access to duplicate records. Duplicate records stored offsite for security are not accessible for reproduction (11-18-05(6)) — [FindLaw 11-18-05 (updated Jan 2024)](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-11-counties/nd-cent-code-sect-11-18-05/); [ND AG 2008-L-01](https://attorneygeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2008-L-01.pdf)
- 2008-L-01 also said "any arrangement that grants a private entity an ownership interest in an open, public record is contrary to the open records law" — [ND AG 2008-L-01](https://attorneygeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2008-L-01.pdf)
- A statute provision that the search tool described as covering computer programming fees for creating new records could not be confirmed — see Gaps.

**South Dakota**
- Where no other rate is set, Bureau of Administration rules set the maximum reproduction and retrieval rates (SDCL 1-27-35) — [SDCL 1-27-35](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-dakota/2011/title1/chapter27/1-27-35/)
- ARSD 10:10:01:03 sets $0.25/page for letter or legal size and $0.50/page for 11x17. Under ARSD 10:10:01:02, electronic transmittal (other than fax) carries no charge — [ARSD 10:10:01:03](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Rules/Rule/10:10:01:03.html?all=true); [ARSD 10:10 article](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Rules/Rule/10:10.html?all=true)
- **Recorder override**: SDCL 7-9-15 (register of deeds fees) applies the copy fee per copy, "whether hard copy, microfilm, electronic copy, or facsimile." Counties post $1/page uncertified and $5 first page / $1 each additional page certified, citing 7-9-15(2) — [SDCL 7-9-15](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/7-9-15.html?all=true); [Oglala Lakota Co. fees](https://oglalalakota.sdcounties.org/register-of-deeds/recording-fees); [Kingsbury Co. fees](https://kingsbury.sdcounty.gov/?p=1400)

**Nebraska**
- Fees are actual cost. A special service charge for labor may be added for time beyond four cumulative hours. The requester gets 10 business days to accept, narrow, or withdraw after a cost estimate (84-712) — [84-712 (Justia 2023)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nebraska/chapter-84/statute-84-712/); [LegalFix summary](https://www.legalfix.com/statutes/state-codes/nebraska/chapter-84/statute-84-712)
- *Nebraska Journalism Trust v. Dept. of Envt. & Energy* (Neb. 2024) addressed a special service charge that included non-attorney review time beyond four hours — [84-712 annotations](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=84-712)
- Register of deeds fee statute: Neb. Rev. Stat. 33-109 [background, unverified].

**Kansas**
- Each agency "may prescribe reasonable fees for providing access to or furnishing copies." Fees for copies may not exceed actual cost, "including the cost of staff time required to make the information available" (K.S.A. 45-219(c)) — [ksrevisor 45-219](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch45/045_002_0019.html); [Justia 2006](https://law.justia.com/codes/kansas/2006/chapter45/statute_18849.html)
- HB 2822 (2024) proposed extending state-agency fee limits to political and taxing subdivisions. Its passage was not confirmed — [HB 2822](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2023_24/measures/documents/hb2822_00_0000.pdf)
- **Recorder statute**: K.S.A. 28-115 sets register of deeds fees, and a county chart cites 28-115 for certified copies — [Ness Co. fee chart](https://www.nesscountyks.gov/departments/register-of-deeds/fee-chart)
- *Data Tree v. Meek*: a commercial bulk requester sought microfilm. The Sedgwick County register charged redaction fees to remove personal identifiers. The court found a reasonable basis and denied attorney fees, so the requester bore the redaction cost — [Data Tree v. Meek](https://16jd.kscourts.gov/Cases-Decisions/Decisions/Published/Data-Tree-v-Meek)
- AG Op. 2009-14 reviewed Logan County giving a private firm access to the register of deeds database. Its cost-recovery analysis was not visible in the excerpts — [AG Op. 2009-14](https://ksag.washburnlaw.edu/opinions/2009/2009-014.htm)

**Montana**
- An agency "may charge a fee for fulfilling a public information request," which "may include the time required to gather public information." Payment may be required in advance (2-6-1006) — [MCA 2-6-1006](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0060/0020-0060-0100-0060.html)
- HB 100 (2025, effective Oct. 1, 2025) amends 2-6-1006 and 2-6-1009 for agencies that are **not local governments**. Its title references an hourly fee limit and no charge for the first hour — [Legiplex HB 100](https://app.legiplex.com/mt/legislature/2025/2025-r/bills/hb100); [Mountain Line policy update](https://mountainline.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/20250925-5.4-Records-Request-Policy-Update.pdf)
- 2-6-1007 special fees cover the Department of Revenue property valuation database and the Historical Society. No recorder-specific special fee was found — [MCA 2-6-1007](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0070/0020-0060-0100-0070.html)
- 7-4-2631 sets county clerk fees for recording, filing, and certified copies. 7-4-2637 sets recording fees — [MCA 7-4-2631](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0070/chapter_0040/part_0260/section_0310/0070-0040-0260-0310.html); [MCA 7-4-2637](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0070/chapter_0040/part_0260/section_0370/0070-0040-0260-0370.html)

**Wyoming**
- 16-4-204: fees must be authorized by a duly adopted statute, rule, resolution, ordinance, or similar authority. A custodian may set a reasonable copying schedule and charge for staff supervision time — [Ballotpedia WY](https://ballotpedia.org/Wyoming_FOIA_procedures); [16-4-204 (Justia 2023)](https://law.justia.com/codes/wyoming/title-16/chapter-4/article-2/section-16-4-204)
- Electronic records rules let the custodian "charge the applicant the reasonable costs of producing and constructing a copy," with an advance estimate — [010-2 Wyo. Code R. § 2-4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/wyoming/agency-010/subagency-0009/chapter-2/010-2-Wyo-Code-R-SSSS-2-4)
- The Wyoming Supreme Court held that reasonableness limits charges — [Cheyenne Newspapers v. Bd. of Trustees (2016)](https://law.justia.com/cases/wyoming/supreme-court/2016/s-16-0059.html)
- 2026 HB0151 (introduced) would amend 16-4-204 regarding "programming and computer services necessary to construct electronic records." Its enactment status is unknown — [2026 HB0151](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2026/Introduced/HB0151.pdf)

**Colorado**
- Research and retrieval: first hour free, then a maximum of **$41.37/hour** (raised from $33.58 on July 1, 2024). The cap is inflation-adjusted every five years, and the fee policy must be noticed in advance — [Legislative Council memo June 2024](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/cora_fee_change_memo_june_2024.pdf); [Leg. Council fee page](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/colorado-open-records-act-maximum-hourly-research-and-retrieval-fee); [HB14-1193](https://legiscan.com/CO/text/HB1193/id/1012782/Colorado-2014-HB1193-Enrolled.pdf)
- CORA copies are $0.25/page. A "reasonable fee" may be charged when the custodian manipulates data to generate a record in a form the entity does not use (24-72-205) [background, unverified on exact wording] — [C.R.S. 24-72-205](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_24-72-205)
- **Recorder override**: a Title 30 statute (the search result cited C.R.S. 30-10-101) requires the clerk to make a "daily copy in bulk of all documents recorded and filed" available to subscribers by the third business day. The fee "shall be sufficient to cover the direct and indirect costs of production" — [C.R.S. 30-10-101 (Justia 2020)](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2020/title-30/article-10/section-30-10-101/). The section number is uncertain; see Gaps.
- C.R.S. 30-1-103 (clerk fees) is listed as repealed effective Dec. 31, 2029 (sunset) — [FindLaw 30-1-103](https://codes.findlaw.com/co/title-30-government-county/co-rev-st-sect-30-1-103/); [public.law 30-1-103](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-1-103)

**Utah**
- GRAMA (pre-March 2025 version): an entity may charge a reasonable fee covering its actual cost. It may not charge for reviewing whether a record is disclosable, or for inspection. The first quarter hour of staff time is free for some requesters. A waiver is available where release primarily benefits the public — [63G-2-203 (2022 version, superseded 3/17/2025)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter2/C63G-2-S203_2022050420220504.pdf)
- **Recorder statute**: former 17-21-18.5 lets the county legislative body set "a reasonable fee" for copies and fees for all services not listed. The official page shows it "Renumbered 11/6/2025" (Title 17 recodification) — [17-21-18.5](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title17/Chapter21/C17-21-S18.5_2023050320230503.html)
- Utah County copy fees are set by the County Commission (effective May 7, 2019). San Juan County charges $0.50 per page for electronic or printout copies — [Utah Co. recorder fees](https://recorder.utahcounty.gov/fees.html); [San Juan Co. code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/sanjuancountyut/latest/sanjuan_ut/0-0-0-5793)

### Inferences
- **ND**: the per-instrument cap of at most $0.50 likely governs bulk index extracts. At 100,000 instruments that is up to $50,000 (a cap, not a set price). Counties can charge less, and ND AG opinions read fees strictly.
- **SD**: a per-copy fee applied to an "electronic copy" leaves room to argue the fee applies per record or per page. That is unfavorable for bulk.
- **CO and UT**: county-legislated or cost-recovery recorder fees support the paid subscription products that counties already sell.

- Current text of N.D.C.C. 44-04-18 on charges for computer programming or creating new records: not confirmed.
- Exact current fee amounts in SDCL 7-9-15(2), Neb. Rev. Stat. 33-109, and K.S.A. 28-115: not confirmed.
- Whether HB 2822 (KS 2024) passed: unknown.
- Montana HB 100's hourly cap amount: unknown, and HB 100 does not apply to local governments (counties).
- The Colorado bulk-copy statute's correct section number (30-10-101 per the search tool) and current text: not verified.
- Utah's new section number for former 17-21-18.5, and the March 2025 amendments to 63G-2-203: not verified.

## 3. Database extracts: existing record vs. "no duty to create"; electronic-format requirements

### Takeaway
Every state has some "no duty to create or reformat" clause. Kansas (by AG opinion), Utah (by statute), Wyoming (by statute), and Colorado (by statute, unverified) lean toward giving records in electronic form when they are already kept that way. Nebraska and Montana are most restrictive about producing new formats.

### Cited Findings
- **ND**: an entity "is not required to create or compile a record that does not exist" — [ND AG 2014-O-18](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2014-O-18.pdf). "A computer file is not an available medium if no means exist to separate or prevent the disclosure of any closed or confidential information contained in that file" — [ND AG 2008-O-18](https://www.AttorneyGeneral.nd.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2008-O-18.pdf). That implies a computer file *is* an available medium where it can be produced cleanly. 11-18-05 expressly contemplates "electronic data extracted from the recorded instrument" — [FindLaw 11-18-05](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-11-counties/nd-cent-code-sect-11-18-05/)
- **SD**: no extract-specific authority was found. Electronic transmittal is free under ARSD 10:10:01:02 — [ARSD 10:10](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Rules/Rule/10:10.html?all=true)
- **NE**: § 84-712(f), as quoted in the search excerpt, says the section "shall not be construed to require a public body or custodian ... to produce or generate any public record in a new or different form or format than the original." § 84-712.01 says computer-file data remains a public record — [84-712 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nebraska/chapter-84/statute-84-712/); [84-712.01](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=84-712.01)
- **KS**: "Previous Attorneys General have determined that KORA requires, by implication, providing records that are kept or created upon computers in their electronic format" — [AG Op. 2009-14](https://ksag.washburnlaw.edu/opinions/2009/2009-014.htm). Under the same opinion, copies transmitted to a private vendor are no longer public records in the vendor's hands, though the originals remain public.
- **MT**: "A public agency is not required to alter or customize public information to provide it in a form specified to meet the needs of the requesting person." If the agency agrees to customize, it can charge for it (2-6-1006) — [MT DLI page quoting statute](https://bsd.dli.mt.gov/Building-Codes-Permits/permit-applications/building-permits/public-records-request); [MCA 2-6-1006](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0060/0020-0060-0100-0060.html)
- **WY**: under 16-4-202(d), the custodian must notify the requester when a record exists primarily or solely in electronic form. According to RCFP, (d)(ii) requires alternative formats unless impractical or impossible. (d)(iii) excuses compiling or extracting data or creating a new document only if doing so would impair the agency's ability to discharge its duties. (d)(iv) protects security and proprietary software. SF0082 (2020) amended (d)(ii)–(iii) — [RCFP WY guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/wyoming/); [16-4-202 (Justia 2022)](https://law.justia.com/codes/wyoming/2022/title-16/chapter-4/article-2/section-16-4-202/); [2020 SF0082 enrolled](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2020/Enroll/SF0082.pdf)
- **CO**: 24-72-205 allows a fee where the custodian "manipulates data" to make a record in a form the entity does not use. This implies extracts can be produced for a fee — [C.R.S. 24-72-205](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_24-72-205). [Background, unverified]: C.R.S. 24-72-203(3.5) (SB17-040) requires a custodian to provide digital records in a requested "structured, sortable, searchable" format if already available. This was not confirmed this session.
- **UT**: GRAMA 63G-2-201: "Although not required to do so, a governmental entity may, upon request ... compile, format, manipulate, package, summarize, or tailor" information. The entity must provide an electronic copy if the requester asks for one and the entity "currently maintains the record in an electronic format that is reproducible and may be provided without reformatting or conversion" — [63G-2-201 (2019)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter2/C63G-2-S201_2019051420190514.pdf); [63G-2-201 (2026)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter2/C63G-2-S201_2026050620260701.pdf)

### Inferences
- In every state, the best framing is to ask for an existing export or report the vendor system already produces, such as a daily index file or the subscriber feed. Do not ask for a custom query. A file that counties already produce for paying subscribers (CO, UT, KS) is plainly an "existing record." The question then becomes price, not existence.
- Utah's "without reformatting or conversion" test favors requesting the native export (CSV or DB dump) the recorder already generates.

### Gaps
- No court decision in these eight states squarely holding that a database export is an existing record was found within the tool budget.
- Colorado 24-72-203(3.5) text not verified.

## 5. Commercial-purpose restriction, certification, or surcharge

### Takeaway
Kansas is the main risk. K.S.A. 45-230 bars using names and addresses from public records to sell or offer property or services, and custodians may require a certification. No commercial surcharge was found elsewhere, but county subscription contracts (CO, UT) impose license limits.

### Cited Findings
- **KS**: 45-230 limits use of names and addresses "to sell or offer to sell property or a service." It does not bar commercial use generally. It applies to business names too. Third-party recipients are also bound. A civil penalty of up to $500 per violation applies. Custodians may require certification under 45-220(c)(2) — [KDWP FAQ](https://ftp.ksoutdoors.com/content/download/47479/484783/file/FAQs%20Names%20and%20Addresses.pdf); [KLRD briefing](https://klrd.gov/publications/briefing-book-2021/kansas-open-records-act/); [Wyandotte Co. KORA certification](https://www.wycokck.org/files/assets/public/clerk/documents/kora-terms-and-certification.pdf); [Justia 45-230 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/kansas/chapter-45/article-2/section-45-230/). AG Op. 94-104 applied the solicitation bar to an appraiser database — [AG Op. 94-104](https://ksag.washburnlaw.edu/opinions/1994/1994-104.htm)
- KORA requires no statement of purpose apart from that certification — [Ballotpedia KS](https://ballotpedia.org/Kansas_FOIA_procedures)
- **UT**: county subscription licenses (Salt Lake County) are "non-exclusive, non-transferable, limited, and revocable" and exclude private information — [SLCo data services terms](https://apps.saltlakecounty.gov/data-services/RecorderPaymentsUI/payments/terms); [SLCo digital services agreement](https://saltlakecounty.gov/globalassets/1-site-files/recorder/recorders-office-digital-services-agreement-v.2023.9.7.pdf)
- **ND**: the court system's bulk data contract (not the recorder's) bars resale in bulk form and requires proof of insurance — [ND Courts bulk data info](https://Www.Ndcourts.gov/Media/Default/court-administration/information-technology/bulk-data/Bulk%20Data%20-%20Information.pdf)
- **CO**: SB25-077 would have given commercial (non-media) requesters longer CORA deadlines. It was **vetoed** Apr. 17, 2025 — [SB25-077](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25-077); [CFOIC wrap-up](https://coloradofoic.org/wrap-up-veto-of-cora-bill-passage-of-nil-and-wildlife-damage-compensation-measures-marked-2025-colorado-legislative-session/)

### Inferences
- **Kansas risk for a judgment buyer**: if the graph is used to contact judgment creditors or debtors with offers (buying a judgment arguably "offers a service"), signing a 45-230 certification could conflict with that use. Counsel should review before certifying. A request for the index for research or graph-building, with no solicitation from names or addresses, is the honest framing. Do not sign a certification the business would then violate.
- **Utah GRAMA** has no commercial-purpose surcharge that was verified this session. The prompt's "Utah GRAMA commercial fees" could not be confirmed (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Utah GRAMA commercial-use fee provisions (if any in the current 63G-2-203): not found.
- MT 2-6-1025 (mailing list prohibition) [background, unverified] may limit agency distribution of mailing lists. Not checked.
- CO: no commercial certification found for land records.
- NE, SD, WY, ND: no commercial restriction surfaced.

## 6. Response deadline

### Takeaway
Deadlines range from 3 business days (KS, CO) to 10 business days (SD, UT) and 30 days (WY). ND requires a "reasonable time." Montana counties have only a "timely" standard.

### Cited Findings
- **CO**: 3 working days, plus up to 7 more for extenuating circumstances. SB25-077 (to 5+10) was vetoed in 2025, so 3+7 stands — [SB25-077](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB25-077); [CFOIC](https://coloradofoic.org/state-legislators-introduce-new-measure-to-extend-cora-response-deadlines/)
- **NE**: respond within 4 business days of actual receipt, excluding the day received. This requires a response, not necessarily production — [NE AG](https://ago.nebraska.gov/node/27); [MuckRock NE](https://www.muckrock.com/place/united-states-of-america/nebraska/exemption/constructive-denial-44/)
- **SD**: written request; the officer must respond within 10 business days or the request is deemed denied (1-27-37) — [SDCL 1-27-37](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/1-27-37); [MuckRock SD](https://www.muckrock.com/place/united-states-of-america/south-dakota/exemption/constructive-denial-51/)
- **WY**: 7 business days for records in active use, in storage, or not in custody. Otherwise release within 30 calendar days of acknowledged receipt absent good cause. A 2026 bill (SF0049, introduced) would require acknowledgment within 3 business days — [Ballotpedia WY](https://ballotpedia.org/Wyoming_FOIA_procedures); [2019 SF0057](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2019/Engross/SF0057.pdf); [2026 SF0049](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2026/Introduced/SF0049.pdf)
- **MT**: agencies must respond in a timely manner and give a time and fee estimate. HB 100 (2025) adds acknowledgment within 5 business days and up to 90 days with written notice, for **non-local-government** agencies only — [Legiplex HB 100](https://app.legiplex.com/mt/legislature/2025/2025-r/bills/hb100); [Mountain Line](https://mountainline.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/20250925-5.4-Records-Request-Policy-Update.pdf); [MCA 2-6-1006](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0060/0020-0060-0100-0060.html)
- **KS**: 3 business days to act on a request (45-218(d)) [background, unverified this session].
- **UT**: 10 business days (5 for expedited/media) under 63G-2-204 [background, unverified].
- **ND**: "within a reasonable time" (44-04-18) [background, unverified].

### Gaps
- KS, UT, and ND deadlines were not confirmed from sources this session.

## 7. Recorder-specific statutes and prices for bulk or subscription data

### Takeaway
Colorado has an explicit statutory daily bulk-copy subscription at cost. Counties charge about $500–$1,000/month for index feeds. North Dakota's statewide NDRIN portal is retail ($30/month, $7/day) and is not a bulk export. Utah counties sell subscriptions (Wasatch: $1,150/yr plus $1,000 setup). Kansas, Nebraska, South Dakota, Montana, and Wyoming showed no specific bulk statute.

### Cited Findings
- **ND – NDRIN**: $30/month auto-renewing subscription, or $7 day pass. McKenzie, Mountrail, and Williams counties have stopped publishing index and image data on NDRIN — [Cass Co. recorder](https://www.casscountynd.gov/our-county/recorder); [NDRIN login page](https://ndrinweb3.hplains.state.nd.us/recordernew/secure/web/newUser.jsp); [Renville Co. NDRIN](https://renvillecountynd.org/department/recorder/ndrin/). NDRIN has contracted with Fidlar for fraud alert service — [Cass Co. agenda](https://docs.casscountynd.gov/WebLink/0/doc/4667842/Page3.aspx)
- **ND – recorder statute**: up to $0.50 per instrument for extracted electronic data (11-18-05) — [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-11-counties/nd-cent-code-sect-11-18-05/)

- **CO – statute**: daily bulk copy available by the 3rd business day, with fees covering direct and indirect costs — [C.R.S. 30-10-101 per Justia result](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2020/title-30/article-10/section-30-10-101/)
- **CO – prices**:
  - Douglas County: $0.03/image and $0.04/grantor-grantee index entry, by agreement — [Douglas Co. 2023 fees](https://www.douglasco.gov/documents/recording-fee-schedule-2023.pdf/)
  - El Paso County: grantor/grantee index $1,000/month with agreement — [El Paso Co.](https://clerkandrecorder.elpasoco.com/recording/recording-fees/)
  - Jefferson County: daily index and image download $1,000/month; 1995–current archive $500/month — [Jeffco](https://www.jeffco.us/1599/Recording-Documents-and-Fees)
  - Fremont County: data extracts $650 per monthly, weekly, or daily delivery on CD, or $550 by web upload — [Fremont Co. 2025](https://fremontcountyclerkco.gov/webadmin/uploads/fee-schedule-clerk-&-recorder-s-office-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Colorado's bulk-copy statute cuts both ways. It confirms bulk index data is a standard product, but it gives clerks statutory cover to charge full cost rather than CORA rates.

### Gaps

## 9. Score (1–5): free or near-free machine-readable recorder index via a records request

### Takeaway
None of the eight states scores above 3. The best odds are Nebraska and Kansas, where general cost-based fees apply and no recorder bulk-price statute was found. North Dakota is possible for small date windows. Colorado and Utah are the least promising because counties already sell the data at commercial prices backed by recorder fee statutes.

### Cited Findings
Scores rest on the findings in §§2–7. Each reason cites its basis.
- **North Dakota — 2**: an electronic extract is clearly contemplated, but N.D.C.C. 11-18-05 lets recorders charge up to $0.50/instrument, and the NDRIN portal is retail-only — [FindLaw 11-18-05](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-11-counties/nd-cent-code-sect-11-18-05/)
- **South Dakota — 2**: SDCL 7-9-15 applies per-copy register fees to electronic copies, though the 10-day deadline and free electronic transmittal help — [SDCL 7-9-15](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/7-9-15.html?all=true)
- **Nebraska — 3**: computer data stays public and fees are actual cost (staff charge only after 4 hours), but there is no duty to produce a new format — [84-712](https://law.justia.com/codes/nebraska/chapter-84/statute-84-712/)
- **Kansas — 3**: the AG reads KORA to require electronic format and fees are actual cost, but staff time is billable, 45-230 certification limits solicitation use, and *Data Tree* lets registers bill redaction — [AG Op. 2009-14](https://ksag.washburnlaw.edu/opinions/2009/2009-014.htm); [Data Tree v. Meek](https://16jd.kscourts.gov/Cases-Decisions/Decisions/Published/Data-Tree-v-Meek)
- **Montana — 2**: 2-6-1006 says agencies need not "alter or customize," fees can include gathering time, and HB 100's limits do not cover counties — [MCA 2-6-1006](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0020/chapter_0060/part_0100/section_0060/0020-0060-0100-0060.html)
- **Wyoming — 2**: 16-4-202(d) favors alternative electronic formats, but custodians may charge "reasonable costs of producing and constructing" electronic copies — [Wyo. Code R. 010-2 § 2-4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/wyoming/agency-010/subagency-0009/chapter-2/010-2-Wyo-Code-R-SSSS-2-4)
- **Colorado — 1**: the statute provides a bulk subscription priced at full cost, and counties charge $500–$1,000/month — [Jeffco](https://www.jeffco.us/1599/Recording-Documents-and-Fees); [El Paso](https://clerkandrecorder.elpasoco.com/recording/recording-fees/)
- **Utah — 2**: GRAMA requires an electronic copy only if no "reformatting or conversion" is needed, county-set recorder fees apply, and counties sell subscriptions — [63G-2-201](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63G/Chapter2/C63G-2-S201_2026050620260701.pdf); [Wasatch](https://www.wasatchcounty.gov/remote-tax-system-subscription-service)

### Inferences
- The best tactic everywhere: request the recorder's *existing* daily or monthly index export (the file vendors or subscribers already get) for a narrow date range. Cite the electronic-format provisions (KS AG opinions, UT 63G-2-201, WY 16-4-202(d), NE 84-712.01). Ask for an itemized estimate before any work.
- In Colorado specifically, a CORA request may get routed to the paid bulk program. A small date range in small rural counties offers the best chance of a free answer.

### Gaps
- Scores are judgment calls based on statute excerpts, not on actual request outcomes. No MuckRock request histories for recorder bulk data in these states were reviewed.
