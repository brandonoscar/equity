## 1. Public records statute name and citation

### Takeaway
All nine states have a general public-records act that applies to county recorders/clerks (or the state recorder in AK and HI). Recorder-specific fee statutes sit beside each act.

### Cited Findings
- **Idaho**: Idaho Public Records Act, Idaho Code Title 74, Chapter 1 (§§ 74-101 et seq.) — [Idaho Legislature, Title 74 Ch. 1](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title74/t74ch1/); [RCFP Idaho guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/idaho/). The AG publishes an Idaho Public Records Law Manual (July 2026 edition at ag.idaho.gov) — [Idaho AG manual](https://www.ag.idaho.gov/content/uploads/2026/07/PublicRecordsLaw.pdf).
- **Nevada**: Nevada Public Records Act (NPRA), NRS Chapter 239; enforcement by writ under NRS 239.011 — [Justia, CIR v. LVMPD 2020](https://law.justia.com/cases/nevada/supreme-court/2020/77617.html); [RCFP Nevada guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/nevada/). Recorder fees: NRS 247.305 — [Justia NRS 247.305 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-247/statute-247-305/).
- **Arizona**: A.R.S. Title 39, Chapter 1, Article 2 (§§ 39-121 to 39-121.04); commercial purpose at § 39-121.03 — [FindLaw A.R.S. 39-121.03](https://codes.findlaw.com/az/title-39-public-records-printing-and-notices/az-rev-st-sect-39-121-03/). SB 1372 (2025, Laws 2025 ch. 102) amended § 39-121.01 — [azleg chaptered SB 1372](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/laws/0102.htm).
- **New Mexico**: Inspection of Public Records Act (IPRA), NMSA 1978 §§ 14-2-1 to 14-2-12; procedure and fees at § 14-2-9 — [Justia NMSA 14-2-9 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-14/article-2/section-14-2-9/); NM DOJ IPRA Guide, 9th ed. (2025) — [NMDOJ guide](https://nmdoj.gov/wp-content/uploads/NMDOJ-IPRA-Guide-Ninth-Edition-25.03.21.pdf).
- **California**: California Public Records Act (CPRA), recodified effective 2023 at Gov. Code §§ 7920.000 et seq.; electronic records at § 7922.575 (former § 6253.9), added by Stats. 2021, ch. 614 — [california.public.law § 7922.575](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_7922.575). Recorder copy fees: Gov. Code § 27366 — [california.public.law § 27366](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_27366).
- **Oregon**: Oregon Public Records Law, ORS 192.311 to 192.478; fees at ORS 192.324 — [Justia ORS 192.324 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/oregon/volume-05/chapter-192/section-192-324/).
- **Washington**: Public Records Act (PRA), Chapter 42.56 RCW — [RCW 42.56 full text](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.56&full=true); AG Model Rules, chapter 44-14 WAC — [WAC 44-14-05001](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/WAC/WAC%20%2044%20%20TITLE/WAC%20%2044%20-%2014%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20%2044%20-%2014%20-05001.htm).
- **Alaska**: Alaska Public Records Act, AS 40.25.100-.295 (search did not surface text). Recorder's Office (DNR, statewide) fees under AS 40.17 and 11 AAC 05.200 — [Cornell 11 AAC 05.200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/alaska/11-AAC-05.200); [DNR Recorder's Office fees](https://dnr.alaska.gov/ssd/recoff/fees).
- **Hawaii**: Uniform Information Practices Act (Modified) (UIPA), HRS Chapter 92F. Bureau of Conveyances: HRS Chapter 502 — [HRS 502 (2013 BOC copy)](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/boc/files/2014/04/HRS-502-May-2013-w-watermark.pdf&gsessionid=CTQJSxcNUuSCBQTYx3OyZm9OIKTawgRn_ctKldR0GAvJFA64cMJEqg).

### Inferences
- In AK and HI the custodian is a single state office (DNR Recorder's Office; DLNR Bureau of Conveyances), so one request covers the whole state. The other seven states need county-by-county requests.

### Gaps
- Alaska AS 40.25.110/.115/.120 and Hawaii HRS 92F-11 text were not surfaced by search. **[UNVERIFIED]** AS 40.25.110 (right to inspect, fees), 40.25.115 (electronic services and products), HRS 92F-11 (duty to disclose).

## 2. Fee rules (duplication only vs staff time; recorder-specific overrides)

### Takeaway
The general acts cap fees low (NV, NM, ID, CA most strictly), but recorder fee statutes and "sale of database" carve-outs (CA § 27366, NM §§ 14-3-15.1/14-3-18, AZ commercial-purpose fee, AK 11 AAC 05.200) are where counties ground paid bulk products. Washington and Oregon expressly allow staff-time/customized charges.

### Cited Findings
- **Idaho**: No fee for the first 2 hours of labor or first 100 pages, "except for fees that are authorized or prescribed under other provisions of Idaho law"; fees allowed only if >100 pages, redaction needed, or labor >2 person-hours; copy charge capped at actual cost "if another fee is not otherwise provided by law"; agencies may aggregate split requests — [Idaho Dept. of Finance fee schedule](https://www.finance.idaho.gov/legal/public-records-requests/public-records-request-fee-schedule/); [RCFP Idaho](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/idaho/); [Justia § 74-102 (2017)](https://law.justia.com/codes/idaho/2017/title-74/chapter-1/section-74-102/).
- **Nevada**: NRS 247.305 sets base recorder fees for recording, copying, certifying, and says that where another statute sets a fee, recorders charge only that fee — [Justia NRS 247.305](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-247/statute-247-305/). The former "extraordinary use" fee in NRS 239 was repealed in 2019 — [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/dozens-of-government-agencies-in-nevada-are-violating-the-states-public-records-law). LVMPD's continued "research fee" practice is being litigated (ACLU of Nevada v. LVMPD, 2026 filings) — [ACLU NV case page](https://www.aclunv.org/cases/aclu-of-nevada-v-lvmpd/). Clark County Recorder's fee schedule (2020) bills bulk media (FTP push/pull of images and index data) citing **NRS 239.052** (the public-records actual-cost provision), not 247.305; copies $1/page — [Clark County fee schedule 2020](https://files.clarkcountynv.gov/Recorder/FeeSchedule_2020.pdf) (via search snippet). Douglas County lists "Bulk Images/Data" at $300/month — [Douglas County fee sheet](https://communityservices.douglascountynv.gov/UserFiles/Servers/Server_12493019/File/Recorder/Fee%20Sheet%20EFFECTIVE%20Jan2020_rev02272024.pdf).
- **Arizona**: For commercial-purpose requests, the custodian may furnish reproductions at a charge that includes specified items (statute's fee subsection; full text not retrieved) — [FindLaw § 39-121.03](https://codes.findlaw.com/az/title-39-public-records-printing-and-notices/az-rev-st-sect-39-121-03/). Maricopa County Recorder requires a $300 minimum deposit to open a bulk account and a certified commercial-purpose statement on file — [Maricopa account application](https://legacy.recorder.maricopa.gov/pdf/AccountApp.pdf).
- **New Mexico**: § 14-2-9 lets custodians charge the actual cost of downloading to a disk/storage device (including the device) and of transmitting by mail/email/fax; subsection D says this does not limit sale of data under §§ 14-3-15.1 and 14-3-18, "including imposing reasonable restrictions on the use of the database and the payment of a royalty or other consideration" — [Justia § 14-2-9](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-14/article-2/section-14-2-9/); § 14-3-18 lets counties and municipalities charge fees for electronic copies of computer databases — [NMAG IPRA guide 2015](https://www.nmag.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Inspection-of-Public-Records-Compliance-Guide-2015-Edit.pdf). NM FOG argues personnel time can never be charged under IPRA (advocacy position) — [NMFOG](https://nmfog.org/2014/08/pay-electronic-records/). Eddy County example: $5 CD/DVD, $6.75 thumb drive — [Eddy County NextRequest FAQ](https://eddycountynm.nextrequest.com/faqs).
- **California**: § 7922.575 limits the cost of an electronic copy to the direct cost of producing it, but subdivision (b) shifts the cost of "data compilation, extraction, or programming" to the requester when needed to produce the record — [california.public.law § 7922.575](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_7922.575). *National Lawyers Guild v. City of Hayward* (Cal. 2020, S252445) read "extraction" narrowly (retrieving data for a particular use, e.g., pulling demographic data on all state employees), not redaction; ordinary searching is not chargeable — [Cal. Supreme Court opinion](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/archive/S252445.PDF); [CSBA summary](https://publications.csba.org/california-school-news/july-2020/supreme-court-clarifies-when-public-agencies-can-recover-cpra-costs/). Gov. Code § 27366: the fee for a copy of any other record or paper on file with the recorder, made by the recorder, is set by the board of supervisors at an amount to recover "direct and indirect costs" — [california.public.law § 27366](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_27366); *California Public Records Research, Inc. v. County of Alameda* (Cal. Ct. App. 2019) upheld Alameda's indirect-cost methodology under § 27366 — [Justia A152843](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a152843.html); [AK Land Law commentary](https://blog.aklandlaw.com/?p=834). County copy fees under § 27366: Sonoma $5/page, Marin $4 first page — [Sonoma fee schedule](https://sonomacounty.ca.gov/administrative-support-and-fiscal-services/clerk-recorder-assessor-registrar-of-voters/clerk-recorder/recorder-services/recorder-fee-schedule); [Marin fees](https://marincounty.org/depts/ar/recording-documents/recording-fees). LA County plain copy $5 first page, $3 each additional — [LA County via search](https://www.deeds.com/recorder/california/los-angeles/).
- **Oregon**: ORS 192.324(4)(a) allows fees "reasonably calculated to reimburse" actual cost, including summarizing, compiling, or tailoring records; (4)(b) allows attorney review/redaction time (not time deciding legal application); county clerk copy fees are those set under ORS 205.320; fee estimate notice required above $25 — [Justia ORS 192.324](https://law.justia.com/codes/oregon/volume-05/chapter-192/section-192-324/). Clackamas County charges $51/hour research and lists "electronic data requests, including an entire county data file" under ORS 192.324(4)(a) — [Clackamas fee appendix](https://www.clackamas.us/code/appendixa).
- **Washington**: RCW 42.56.120(3) permits a "customized service charge" on top of copy charges, e.g., for running a new customized report that extracts data from a database — [MRSC, Data as Records (Nov. 2022)](https://mrsc.org/stay-informed/mrsc-insight/november-2022/data-as-records-pra-disclosure-of-database-inform).
- **Alaska**: 11 AAC 05.200 sets Recorder's Office fees for products/services under AS 40.17, including CD-ROM or electronically transferred files with per-district charges for daily recorded document images and monthly plat images; tiered monthly daily-image fees (higher tier for 251+ documents/month) — [Cornell 11 AAC 05.200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/alaska/11-AAC-05.200); [Justia 11 AAC 05.200](https://regulations.justia.com/states/alaska/title-11/part-1/chapter-05/section-11-aac-05-200). Fees are not waived even for government agencies — [Justia 11 AAC 05 chapter](https://regulations.justia.com/states/alaska/title-11/part-1/chapter-05).
- **Hawaii**: BOC lists per-document subscription tiers, e.g., "Public, $50 per month and $3 per document" (via third-party summary of BOC site) — [incfacts Hawaii](https://www.incfacts.com/hawaii/) (secondary, weak). HRS 502 requires weekly index and image transmissions to a county clearinghouse at no charge (inter-governmental) — [HRS 502 (2013)](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/boc/files/2014/04/HRS-502-May-2013-w-watermark.pdf&gsessionid=CTQJSxcNUuSCBQTYx3OyZm9OIKTawgRn_ctKldR0GAvJFA64cMJEqg).

### Inferences
- California is the key conflict: a requester asking for the index as a CPRA electronic record would argue § 7922.575 direct cost; counties will argue § 27366 (direct + indirect, board-set) governs "copies of records on file with the recorder." § 27366's text speaks of copies "made by the recorder," and the case law that applied it involved copies of recorded documents; whether it reaches an electronic index export is untested in what I found.
- Nevada's general rule (actual cost, no extraordinary-use fee since 2019) is the most requester-friendly of the nine for a pure index export, but Clark's practice still bills bulk index data as a product.
- Arizona's fee is lawfully elevated if the requester's purpose is commercial (see Q5), and a judgment buyer's use is very likely commercial.

### Gaps
- Exact text of NRS 239.052 and the definition of "actual cost" (**[UNVERIFIED]** NRS 239.005 defines it as direct cost of reproduction excluding staff/operational costs).
- A.R.S. § 11-475 (recorder fees) text: not retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED]** whether it has a bulk/electronic-data provision.
- Washington RCW 36.18.010 auditor fees and RCW 42.56.120 default fee schedule (**[UNVERIFIED]** 15¢/page photocopy, 10¢ scanned page, 5¢ per four electronic files, 10¢/GB) — not confirmed.
- Oregon ORS 205.320 text — not retrieved.
- Hawaii HAR ch. 2-71 fees (**[UNVERIFIED]** $2.50 per 15 min search, $5 per 15 min review/segregation; $30 general waiver, $60 public-interest waiver) — not confirmed.
- Idaho recorder fee statute (**[UNVERIFIED]** Idaho Code § 31-3205) — not retrieved.
- Alaska AS 40.25.110(c) fee rule (**[UNVERIFIED]** standard unit cost; personnel costs only if >5 person-hours/month) and AS 40.25.115 — not retrieved.

## 3. Database extracts: existing record vs "no duty to create"

### Takeaway
Washington (Fisher Broadcasting) and California (Sierra Club / § 7922.575) are strongest that database output is producible; Nevada likely similar; Hawaii's UIPA expressly excuses compilations not "readily retrievable." Several states say provide electronic format if kept.

### Cited Findings
- **Washington**: *Fisher Broadcasting-Seattle TV LLC v. City of Seattle*, 180 Wn.2d 515, 326 P.3d 688 (2014): SPD had capacity to produce a partially responsive record from its database and violated the PRA by not producing it; agencies need not "create or produce a record that is nonexistent" — [midpage](https://app.midpage.ai/case/fisher-broadcasting-seattle-tv-llc-4710731); [Studicata](https://www.studicata.com/summaries/supreme-court-of-washington/fisher-broadcasting-seattle-tv-llc-v-city-of-seattle-2014-5ugm0i). MRSC: "not always a simple dichotomy" between existing and new records; database extraction can be charged via the customized service charge — [MRSC](https://mrsc.org/stay-informed/mrsc-insight/november-2022/data-as-records-pra-disclosure-of-database-inform). WAC 44-14-05001 cross-references Fisher for database discussion — [WAC 44-14-05001](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/WAC/WAC%20%2044%20%20TITLE/WAC%20%2044%20-%2014%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20%2044%20-%2014%20-05001.htm). Washington retention schedule says auditor recorded documents include "all indexes to recorded documents" — [WA county auditor retention schedule v6.0 (Aug 2025)](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/county-auditors-records-retention-schedule.pdf).
- **California**: § 7922.575(b) contemplates requests that "would require data compilation, extraction, or programming to produce the record" and allows them at requester cost, which presupposes the agency must do it — [california.public.law § 7922.575](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_7922.575); *Hayward* (2020) — [opinion](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/archive/S252445.PDF). Gov. Code § 27257 lets the recorder keep the grantor-grantee index in computerized or electronic format — [california.public.law § 27257](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_27257).
- **Oregon**: ORS 192.324(4)(a) fee language covers "summarizing, compiling, or tailoring" records, implying such work can be done (at a cost) — [Justia ORS 192.324](https://law.justia.com/codes/oregon/volume-05/chapter-192/section-192-324/); Clackamas prices "an entire county data file" — [Clackamas](https://www.clackamas.us/code/appendixa).
- **New Mexico**: § 14-2-9 contemplates electronic copies and database sales under §§ 14-3-15.1, 14-3-18 — [Justia § 14-2-9](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-14/article-2/section-14-2-9/).
- **Nevada**: *Las Vegas Metro. Police Dep't v. Center for Investigative Reporting*, 136 Nev. 122, 460 P.3d 952 (2020) exists and addressed NPRA obligations/fees — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/nevada/supreme-court/2020/77617.html); the search did not confirm its database-search holding.
- **Hawaii**: BOC's own search site asks those with "a legitimate need for bulk data" to contact staff (dlnr.bc.data@hawaii.gov) rather than scrape — [BOC document search](https://bocdataext.hi.wcicloud.com/).

### Inferences
- In WA a recorder index export is likely a producible record, but the customized service charge can make it non-free, and the commercial "lists of individuals" bar (Q5) is the bigger obstacle.
- In CA, an index export from the recorder's system is probably "extraction" under *Hayward*'s reading (retrieving data for a use), so the requester may bear programming cost unless the county already produces the file for others (e.g., title companies), which then counts as an existing format.

### Gaps
- **[UNVERIFIED]** *Sierra Club v. Superior Court* (2013) 57 Cal.4th 157 held Orange County's GIS parcel basemap (OC Landbase) is a public record not excluded as "computer mapping system" software, producible at direct duplication cost, not license fee. Not confirmed this session but a well-known holding; verify before citing.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Cal. Gov. Code § 7922.570 (former 6253.9(a)): agency must provide in any electronic format in which it holds the record, and in a format it uses for itself or other agencies.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** *CIR v. LVMPD* (2020) held a database search is not creation of a new record; and NRS 239.010 bars refusing a copy in a readily available medium because the agency prefers another medium.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** ORS 192.324(3): if record is kept in electronic form, provide it in the form requested if available.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** NMSA § 14-2-9: custodian shall provide electronic copy if available electronically and specifically requested.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** HRS § 92F-11(c): agency not required to prepare a compilation or summary unless readily retrievable in the form requested.
- Idaho and Arizona: no source found on database extracts. **[UNVERIFIED]** Arizona case law (*Lake v. City of Phoenix*, 2009, metadata is a public record) and *Primary Consultants v. Maricopa County Recorder* (Ariz. App. 2005, recorder bulk images/commercial fee) — verify.
- Alaska: no source found.

## 5. Commercial-purpose restriction, certification, or surcharge

### Takeaway
Arizona (commercial-purpose statement and value-based fee; since 2025 every requester must affirm non-commercial or state the use) and Washington (statutory ban on providing lists of individuals for commercial purposes) are the hard cases. Idaho bars agency distribution of mailing/telephone lists. New Mexico allows database-use restrictions and royalties. No commercial bar found for NV, CA, OR, AK, HI.

### Cited Findings
- **Arizona**: Requester for a commercial purpose must give a statement setting forth the commercial purpose; custodian may then charge a fee including specified items; undisclosed commercial use carries liability for damages, and the custodian may ask the governor to bar an abusive commercial purpose — [FindLaw § 39-121.03](https://codes.findlaw.com/az/title-39-public-records-printing-and-notices/az-rev-st-sect-39-121-03/); [Maricopa commercial account form](https://legacy.recorder.maricopa.gov/pdf/AccountApp_CommPurp.pdf). SB 1372 (signed May 2, 2025, ch. 102) amends § 39-121.01 to require every requester to affirm the record is not for a commercial purpose or, if it is, give a statement explaining the intended use; commercial purpose covers sale/resale, producing a document containing the record for sale, or obtaining names for solicitation; fact sheet describes treble damages plus fees if the record would not have been provided had the purpose been stated — [House summary, signed](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/summary/H.SB1372_051525_SIGNED.DOCX.htm); [chaptered text](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/laws/0102.htm).
- **Washington**: RCW 42.56.070(8) — the PRA does not authorize agencies to give, sell, or provide access to lists of individuals requested for commercial purposes (renumbered from (9)) — [RCW 42.56.070](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCW/RCW%20%2042%20%20TITLE/RCW%20%2042%20.%2056%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2042%20.%2056%20.070.htm). AGO 2019 No. 3: access to sortable/queryable electronic records that can display lists of individuals is also barred when the request is commercial; an alphabetical list of property owners is a "list of individuals"; RCW 42.56.080 lets the agency inquire whether the purpose is commercial; recommends redacting names where possible — [AGO 2019 No. 3](https://www.atg.wa.gov/ago-opinions/listings-taxable-property-lists-names-individuals-requested-commercial-purpose).
- **Idaho**: Idaho Code § 74-120 bars agencies from distributing or selling a list of persons for use as a mailing or telephone number list without the listed persons' permission; individuals may still compile lists from open records; civil penalty up to $1,000; AG says it reaches only lists as compiled by the agency — [Idaho Code § 74-120](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title74/T74CH1/SECT74-120/); [Idaho Dept. of Lands](https://www.idl.idaho.gov/public-records-request/); [Harvard CAP Idaho note](https://copyright.lib.harvard.edu/states/idaho/).
- **New Mexico**: custodian selling databases under §§ 14-3-15.1/14-3-18 may impose reasonable use restrictions and charge royalty or other consideration — [Justia § 14-2-9](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-14/article-2/section-14-2-9/).

### Inferences
- A judgment-buying business building a land-records graph and contacting owners/debtors is plainly "commercial" under AZ's definition (names obtained for solicitation, or business use). An honest request in AZ must state the commercial use, which unlocks the commercial fee and Maricopa's paid bulk program; non-commercial affirmation would be false and expose treble damages.
- In WA, a grantor/grantee index is a list of individuals; AGO 2019-3 reasoning would let an auditor deny a commercial requester outright. Business-entity rows or name-redacted exports (instrument number, date, doc type, consideration, parcel) might still be releasable, but these lose most of the graph value.
- Idaho § 74-120 is narrower (mailing/telephone lists); a recorder index used for records research rather than mailing likely falls outside it, but a requester planning outreach should not certify otherwise.

### Gaps
- Whether WA's "authorized by law" exception covers recorder indexes (e.g., via RCW 65.04 or 36.22 public-index duties) — no source found.
- *SEIU 775 v. DSHS* (Wash. App. 2016) definition of "commercial purposes" — **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** NMSA § 14-3-15.1 may require a database requester to agree not to use data for commercial/political solicitation; not confirmed.
- NV, CA, OR, AK, HI: no commercial bar found; **[UNVERIFIED]** CA Gov. Code § 7921.300 (purpose of request irrelevant). CA does protect elected/appointed officials' addresses (**[UNVERIFIED]** § 7928.205, former 6254.21).

## 6. Response deadline

### Takeaway
Ranges from "promptly" (AZ) to 15 calendar days (NM); most are 5-10 business days with extensions.

### Cited Findings
- **Oregon**: ORS 192.324 includes procedure for records requests (deadline text not in snippet) — [Justia ORS 192.324](https://law.justia.com/codes/oregon/volume-05/chapter-192/section-192-324/).
- **Nevada**: NPRA timing not retrieved; RCFP Nevada guide covers it — [RCFP Nevada](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/nevada/).

### Gaps (all **[UNVERIFIED]**, not confirmed this session)
- Idaho § 74-103: 3 working days; extension up to 10 working days.
- Nevada NRS 239.0107: 5 business days to provide, deny, or give a date.
- Arizona § 39-121.01(D)(1): "promptly"; no fixed day count.
- New Mexico § 14-2-8(D): as soon as practicable, no later than 15 calendar days; 3-business-day written acknowledgment if not immediate.
- California § 7922.535: 10 days to determine, extendable 14 days.
- Oregon ORS 192.329: acknowledge within 5 business days; complete within 10 business days after acknowledgment, if practicable.
- Washington RCW 42.56.520: 5 business days to respond (provide, acknowledge with estimate, or deny).
- Alaska 2 AAC 96.325: 10 working days, extension 10 more.
- Hawaii HAR § 2-71-13: 10 business days.

## 7. Recorder-specific bulk/subscription sale statutes and prices

### Takeaway
Paid bulk index/image programs exist in AZ (Maricopa, Pima), NV (Clark, Douglas), AK (statewide, 11 AAC 05.200), and NM by statute. California counties sell under § 27366-type ordinances but no price page surfaced. HI BOC invites bulk-data contact.

### Cited Findings

## 8. Known free statewide or county bulk recorder/land-records data

### Takeaway
No free bulk index downloads were confirmed in any of the nine states. Free online per-document search exists widely (AK statewide, HI BOC, King County WA, San Diego and Orange CA), which helps verification but not bulk ingestion.

## 9. Score (1-5): likelihood of free/near-free machine-readable recorder index via a records request

### Takeaway
Best odds: Nevada and Alaska (low statutory fee caps / single statewide office) and Oregon/Idaho for smaller counties; worst: Arizona and Washington because of commercial-purpose rules.

### Cited Findings (basis per score; see sections above for sources)
- **Nevada – 3**: Actual-cost rule and repeal of extraordinary-use fee favor requesters ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/dozens-of-government-agencies-in-nevada-are-violating-the-states-public-records-law)), but Clark and Douglas already sell bulk index data ($300/mo Douglas) ([Douglas](https://communityservices.douglascountynv.gov/UserFiles/Servers/Server_12493019/File/Recorder/Fee%20Sheet%20EFFECTIVE%20Jan2020_rev02272024.pdf)).
- **Idaho – 3**: First 2 hours labor free and actual-cost cap ([Idaho DOF](https://www.finance.idaho.gov/legal/public-records-requests/public-records-request-fee-schedule/)), but "fees prescribed by other law" exception and § 74-120 list rule add friction; no case law on extracts found.
- **Oregon – 3**: No commercial bar; staff time and compiling chargeable ([ORS 192.324](https://law.justia.com/codes/oregon/volume-05/chapter-192/section-192-324/)); small counties may export cheaply, larger ones will quote hourly ($51/hr Clackamas).
- **Alaska – 3**: One statewide office and free online search, but 11 AAC 05.200 sets paid electronic products and fees are not waived ([Cornell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/alaska/11-AAC-05.200)).
- **New Mexico – 2**: IPRA actual-cost download fee is favorable, but §§ 14-3-15.1/14-3-18 let counties sell databases with royalties and use restrictions ([Justia § 14-2-9](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-14/article-2/section-14-2-9/)).
- **California – 2**: Strong CPRA electronic-record law, but § 7922.575(b) shifts extraction/programming costs and § 27366 lets boards set cost-recovery fees including indirect costs ([§ 27366](https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_27366); [CPRR v. Alameda](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a152843.html)).
- **Hawaii – 2**: Single statewide BOC with a bulk-data contact, but UIPA likely excuses non-readily-retrievable compilations and BOC monetizes per-document access ([BOC](https://bocdataext.hi.wcicloud.com/)).
- **Arizona – 1**: Honest statement of commercial use (mandatory since SB 1372, 2025) triggers § 39-121.03 commercial pricing and paid recorder bulk programs (Pima $500/yr + per-line; Maricopa $300 deposit) ([SB 1372 summary](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/summary/H.SB1372_051525_SIGNED.DOCX.htm); [Pima](https://www.recorder.pima.gov/SubscriptionFees)).
- **Washington – 1**: RCW 42.56.070(8) and AGO 2019-3 let auditors deny lists of individuals (including queryable databases) to commercial requesters; customized service charge applies otherwise ([AGO 2019-3](https://www.atg.wa.gov/ago-opinions/listings-taxable-property-lists-names-individuals-requested-commercial-purpose)).

### Inferences
- "Free or near-free" in practice will most likely come from small rural counties (NV, ID, OR, NM) whose vendor systems (Tyler/Eagle, Fidlar, Landmark) can export a CSV in minutes, not from metro counties with existing revenue products.
- Paid entry points worth owner approval: Pima (~$550 first year plus per-line), Douglas NV ($300/mo).

### Gaps
- Scores are judgment calls built on partly unverified statute details; adjust after reading the full NRS 239, ORS 192.324, HRS 92F-11, AS 40.25 texts and actual county price pages.
