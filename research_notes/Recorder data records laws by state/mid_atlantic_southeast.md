## 1. Public records statute name and citation

### Takeaway
Each jurisdiction has a general open-records statute. In VA and MD, though, land records sit with circuit court clerks, so court-clerk statutes and judiciary rules may apply alongside the general act or instead of it.

### Cited Findings
- **Delaware**: Freedom of Information Act, 29 Del. C. ch. 100; access and copying are in § 10003. — [Justia 29 Del. C. § 10003 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-29/chapter-100/section-10003/); [DE DOJ Open Government](https://attorneygeneral.delaware.gov/executive/open-government/)
- **Maryland**: Public Information Act (PIA), Md. Code, General Provisions §§ 4-101 et seq. Fees are in § 4-206 and copies/electronic format in § 4-205. — [mgaleg 2026 § 4-205](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Statute_Web/ggp/4-205.pdf); [Justia § 4-206](https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/2015/article-ggp/title-4/subtitle-2/section-4-206)
- **District of Columbia**: DC FOIA, D.C. Code §§ 2-531 et seq. Access, costs and time limits are in § 2-532. — [DC Code § 2-532](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/2-532)
- **Virginia**: Virginia FOIA, Va. Code §§ 2.2-3700 et seq. Procedure and charges are in § 2.2-3704. Clerk land-records remote access is in §§ 17.1-294 and 17.1-276. — [Va. Code § 2.2-3704](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter37/section2.2-3704/); [§ 17.1-294](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title17.1/chapter2/section17.1-294/); [§ 17.1-276](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title17.1/chapter2/section17.1-276/)
- **West Virginia**: WV FOIA, W. Va. Code ch. 29B, art. 1. Fees are in § 29B-1-3. — [RCFP WV guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/west-virginia/); [Ballotpedia WV](https://ballotpedia.org/West_Virginia_FOIA_procedures)
- **North Carolina**: Public Records Act, N.C.G.S. ch. 132. Copies and fees are in § 132-6.2. Register of deeds fees are in G.S. 161-10. — [G.S. 132-6.2](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_132/GS_132-6.2.pdf); [G.S. 161-10](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-10.pdf)
- **South Carolina**: Freedom of Information Act, S.C. Code §§ 30-4-10 et seq. Right to copy, fees and timing are in § 30-4-30. — [Justia § 30-4-30 (2019)](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/2019/title-30/chapter-4/section-30-4-30/)
- **Georgia**: Open Records Act, O.C.G.A. §§ 50-18-70 et seq. Access, timing, fees and electronic records are in § 50-18-71. — [Justia § 50-18-71 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-50/chapter-18/article-4/section-50-18-71/)
- **Florida**: Public Records Act, Fla. Stat. ch. 119. Inspection, copying and fees are in § 119.07. Clerk service charges are in § 28.24. — [Justia § 119.07 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/florida/title-x/chapter-119/section-119-07/); [flsenate § 28.24 (2019)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2019/28.24)

### Inferences
- Who holds the records differs by state. MD, VA, GA and FL recorders are clerks of the circuit or superior court. DE, DC, NC and SC use a recorder or register of deeds; in some SC counties the clerk of court records deeds. WV uses county clerks. Where the custodian is a court clerk, expect that clerk to argue that court-records rules or clerk fee statutes control.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the Maryland Rules on court-records access (Title 16, ch. 900) govern land records or exclude them. That decides whether the PIA or the judiciary's bulk-data rules apply to MD land-record index requests.
- I could not confirm how Va. Code § 17.1-208 (clerks' records open to inspection) interacts with FOIA for circuit court clerks [background, not verified this session].

## 2. Fee rules: duplication only, staff time, and whether recorder fee statutes override

### Takeaway
NC and FL start from actual duplication cost, and WV bars search and retrieval fees outright. GA, SC, DE, MD, VA and DC allow staff-time charges capped at cost: the lowest-paid qualified employee in GA and SC, after a free first hour in DE and two free hours in MD. Recorder or clerk fee statutes (FL § 28.24, NC G.S. 161-10, VA § 17.1-276) can displace the general cap through "except as otherwise provided by law" clauses.

### Cited Findings
- **DE**: The first 20 pages of standard black-and-white copies are free. After that, copies cost $0.10 per sheet. Administrative (staff-time) fees apply only to requests needing more than 1 hour of staff time, billed per quarter hour. They may not include legal review of exemptions. Electronic copies are charged at material cost plus administrative costs. — [Justia 29 Del. C. § 10003](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-29/chapter-100/section-10003/); [Justia 2020](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/2020/title-29/chapter-100/section-10003/)
- **MD**: No fee may be charged for the first 2 hours needed to search for and prepare a record. After that, the custodian may charge a "reasonable fee" bearing a reasonable relationship to the recovery of actual costs. Requesters can challenge fees over $350 before the State Public Information Act Compliance Board. — [Justia § 4-206](https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/2015/article-ggp/title-4/subtitle-2/section-4-206); [UMD OGC](https://ogc.umd.edu/public-information-requests)
- **DC**: Fees may not exceed the actual cost of searching, reviewing, redacting and copying. Commercial-use requesters may be charged standard search, duplication and review fees. News media and non-commercial scholarly requesters pay duplication only; all others pay search plus duplication. Fees can be waived when disclosure primarily benefits the general public. Advance payment can be required only for prior non-payment or a fee over $250. — [DC Code § 2-532](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/2-532); [OCFO FOIA fee policy](https://cfo.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/ocfo/publication/attachments/Revised%20FOIA%20Fee%20Policy%20051923.pdf)
- **VA**: A public body may make reasonable charges, not exceeding actual cost, for accessing, duplicating, supplying or searching for records [statutory wording from background; § 2.2-3704(F)]. — [Va. Code § 2.2-3704](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter37/section2.2-3704/); [RCFP VA](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/virginia/)
- **VA clerk-specific**: § 17.1-276 lets a circuit court clerk charge a reasonable convenience fee of no more than $2 per transaction for secure remote access to land records, plus a separate per-image download fee. Several state agencies are exempt. The older $25 monthly cap is outdated. — [§ 17.1-276](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title17.1/chapter2/section17.1-276/); [Justia 2025 § 17.1-276](https://law.justia.com/codes/virginia/title-17-1/chapter-2/section-17-1-276/)
- **WV**: Agencies may recover reproduction costs only. A 2015 amendment bars search and retrieval (staff-time) fees, overriding a 2014 WV Supreme Court decision that had allowed them. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2983e35c-0bdc-4f61-8ab4-531b5db22c8f); [RCFP WV](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/west-virginia/)
- **NC**: "Except as otherwise provided by law," an agency may not charge more than its actual cost for an uncertified copy. It may add a reasonable, actual-cost "special service charge" when a request needs extensive IT resources or staff time, or a medium that uses more IT resources than usual. Disputes can be mediated by the State CIO. — [G.S. 132-6.2](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_132/GS_132-6.2.pdf); [UNC SOG blog](https://canons.sog.unc.edu/blog/2023/11/07/special-service-charges-for-extensive-public-records-requests/)
- **NC recorder override**: G.S. 161-10 lets a register of deeds charge for uncertified copies or index pages supplied "as a convenience to the public," at a rate tied to copying and maintenance costs. — [G.S. 161-10](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-10.pdf)
- **SC**: Fees may not exceed actual cost. Search, retrieval and redaction are capped at the prorated hourly salary of the lowest-paid employee with the needed skill. Copies are capped at the prevailing commercial rate, and electronic transmission carries no copy charge. Reviewing records to decide whether they are disclosable is free. A deposit of up to 25% may be required, and fee schedules must be posted online. — [Justia § 30-4-30](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/2019/title-30/chapter-4/section-30-4-30/); [MASC 2025 presentation](https://www.masc.sc/sites/default/files/uploads/affiliated-associations/for-web-e-shytle4.17.25.pdf)
- **GA**: For electronic records, the agency may charge the actual cost of the media. Search, retrieval and redaction are capped at the prorated hourly salary of the lowest-paid qualified employee. Prepayment may be required above $500. No fee may be charged merely to inspect records routinely open to inspection "such as deeds." An agency may meet a request by giving access through a public website. — [Justia § 50-18-71 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-50/chapter-18/article-4/section-50-18-71/)
- **GA practice**: One agency charges any request that takes more than 15 minutes. — [Georgia SEB](https://seb.ga.gov/open-records/)
- **FL**: § 119.07(4)(d) allows a special service charge, on top of duplication costs, when the nature or volume of a request needs extensive IT resources or extensive clerical or supervisory help. The charge must be reasonable and based on cost incurred. The AG says the use must be "extensive," not merely that IT is involved. — [Justia § 119.07](https://law.justia.com/codes/florida/title-x/chapter-119/section-119-07/); [FL AGO: special service charge](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/ag-opinions/records-imposition-of-special-service-charge); [FL AGO: charges for electronic records](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/ag-opinions/public-records-charges-for-electronic-records)
- **FL clerk statute**: § 28.24 (2019 version) set official-records recording at $1.00 for the first page and $0.50 for each additional page, plus a $4 per-page service charge (2018 version), and certification at $2.00. A 1999 version tied the fee for an electronic copy of computer-database information back to chapter 119. A 2012 version let the clerk provide a record electronically instead of on paper. — [flsenate § 28.24 (2019)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2019/28.24); [2018](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2018/28.24); [1999](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/1999/28.24); [2012](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2012/28.24)

### Inferences
- In FL, the 1999 cross-reference in § 28.24 suggests electronic database copies fall back to ch. 119 duplication cost rather than per-page recording fees. That favors bulk-index requests, but it needs checking against the current text.
- In NC, a register may argue that G.S. 161-10 index-page fees override actual cost for index printouts. A machine-readable database copy is arguably not an "index page" and should fall under § 132-6.2 instead.
- GA and SC staff-time charges for one export should be small, since the cap is the lowest-paid qualified employee's hourly rate.

### Gaps
- I did not get the current (2025–2026) text of FL § 28.24, including any line item for electronic or bulk index data.
- I did not get the current text of VA § 2.2-3704(F) on fees.
- I did not get the text of WV § 59-1-10 (county clerk fees), which may set per-page copy charges for deed records.

## 3. Database extracts: existing record or new record, and electronic format

### Takeaway
GA, MD, NC and VA have statutes that point toward a database export being an existing record. GA's is the strongest: an agency must run search and filter commands with its existing programs. FL case law (Seigle) and the "no duty to create" clauses in NC § 132-6.2(e) and elsewhere let an office refuse custom programming.

### Cited Findings
- **GA**: On request, an agency must provide electronic records in the format it keeps them, or in a standard export format such as flat-file ASCII, if its existing programs support it. It may not refuse because the export needs search or filter commands, so long as existing programs used in the ordinary course of business can run them. — [Justia § 50-18-71 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-50/chapter-18/article-4/section-50-18-71/)
- **MD**: § 4-205 requires an electronic copy in a "searchable and analyzable" format when the record already exists in that format, the applicant asks for it, and protected data won't be exposed. Providing part of a record that way is not creating a new record. The AG Manual notes the agency need not perform an extraction that requires expertise beyond its existing capabilities, and may strip metadata. — [mgaleg § 4-205](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Statute_Web/ggp/4-205.pdf); [MD AG PIA Manual ch. 2](https://www.marylandattorneygeneral.gov/OpenGov%20Documents/Chapter2.pdf)
- **VA**: Nonexempt database records must be produced in the requester's chosen medium if the agency already uses that medium. Excising exempt fields or converting to another available format is not creating a new record. No public body has to produce database records in a format it doesn't regularly use. — [RCFP VA](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/virginia/); [Va. Code § 2.2-3704](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter37/section2.2-3704/)
- **VA clerks**: The VA FOIA Advisory Council has an advisory opinion on whether clerks must give a citizen digital copies of their land conveyance databases. I could not open it to read the conclusion. — [VA FOIA Council AO 11 (2002)](http://foiacouncil.dls.virginia.gov/ops/02/AO_11.htm)
- **NC**: Requesters may choose any medium the agency is capable of providing. Database copy requests may be required in writing. § 132-6.2(e) says nothing requires an agency to create or compile a record that doesn't exist. — [G.S. 132-6.2](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_132/GS_132-6.2.pdf)
- **FL**: Seigle v. Barry (Fla. 4th DCA 1982) held that access to computerized records must be given through existing programs, and that officials need not create new formats at a requester's request. It allows a specially designed program, at the requester's expense, when the existing form doesn't fairly represent the records. AGO 97-39 says an agency need not provide electronic records in any format other than the standard one it routinely keeps. — [Carlton Fields on electronic public records](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/government-electronic-public-records); [Seigle summary](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/seigle-v-barry-no-888079993); [Manatee County FAQ](https://manatee.hosted.civiclive.com/departments/county_administration/public_records/f_a_qs)
- **FL "medium requested" rule**: The custodian must provide a copy in the medium requested if it keeps the record in that medium (Fla. Stat. § 119.01(2)(f)) [background, not verified this session].
- **DE**: Electronic copies are charged at material plus administrative cost, which implies electronic production is contemplated. I found no case law or AG opinion on database extracts. — [Justia 2020 § 10003](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/2020/title-29/chapter-100/section-10003/)

### Inferences
- The best phrasing for any template is: "an export of existing index tables (grantor, grantee, doc type, recording date, book/page, instrument number, consideration) using your system's existing export/report function, in CSV or the native format you keep it in." That tracks GA § 50-18-71, MD § 4-205, VA § 2.2-3704 and Seigle's "existing programs" test, and makes a "creating a record" denial harder.

### Gaps
- I found no DC, WV, SC or DE court decision or AG opinion specifically on database extracts.
- I could not read the conclusion of the VA FOIA Council land-database opinion.

## 4. Residency restrictions

### Takeaway
Delaware and Virginia limit access to citizens of the state. The other seven jurisdictions let any person request.

### Cited Findings
- **DE**: Access "shall not be denied to any citizen." Ballotpedia counts Delaware among seven states that limit requesters to state residents. — [Justia 29 Del. C. § 10003](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-29/chapter-100/section-10003/); [Ballotpedia DE](https://ballotpedia.org/Delaware_FOIA_procedures)
- **VA**: Records are open to "citizens of the Commonwealth" and to representatives of newspapers, magazines and broadcasters. A custodian may ask for name and address to verify citizenship. — [VML FOIA guide 2025-26](https://www.fallschurchva.gov/DocumentCenter/View/19394/VML-FOIA-COIA-Guide); [RCFP VA](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/virginia/)
- **VA, McBurney**: McBurney v. Young, 569 U.S. 221 (2013), upheld Virginia's citizens-only provision against Privileges and Immunities and dormant Commerce Clause challenges [background, not verified this session]. — [McBurney v. Young, 569 U.S. 221](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/569/221/) (URL not fetched)
- **DC**: The right applies to "any person." — [DC Code § 2-532](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/2-532)
- **NC, SC, GA, FL, MD, WV**: Ballotpedia's count of residency states is the source for treating these as open to any person. Statute wording was not confirmed this session [background, not verified this session].

### Inferences
- Requests to DE and VA must come from a real resident of that state, or be framed as nonresident courtesy requests that the office may decline. VA clerks can also sell non-citizens remote-access subscriptions under § 17.1-294 without regard to FOIA.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether DE offices or AG opinions enforce the citizen rule against nonresidents in practice.

## 5. Commercial-purpose restrictions, certifications or surcharges

### Takeaway
Only DC sets a commercial-requester fee tier. South Carolina makes it a crime to use personal information from public records for commercial solicitation, which matters a great deal for a judgment buyer. I found no commercial certification requirement in the others.

### Cited Findings
- **DC**: Commercial-use requesters pay standard charges for search, duplication and review. Media and scholarly requesters pay duplication only. — [DC Code § 2-532](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/2-532)
- **SC**: Under § 30-2-50, knowingly obtaining or using personal information from a public body for commercial solicitation is a crime. — [City of Charleston FOIA policy](https://www.charleston-sc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/28078/Foia-Information)
- **GA**: The ORA has a commercial-purpose certification for certain law-enforcement and accident-report personal data [background, not verified this session]. It does not apply to deed indexes as far as I found.
- **VA**: § 17.1-294 limits the Social Security numbers shown to subscribers to the last four digits. — [§ 17.1-294](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title17.1/chapter2/section17.1-294/)

### Inferences
- In SC, any template should state the purpose honestly, such as research and title analysis for a land-records database. The business must not use the data to solicit debtors or property owners by mail or phone, or it risks § 30-2-50 liability. An SC office may also ask for a non-solicitation certification.

### Gaps
- I did not get the full text of SC § 30-2-50, including how it defines "commercial solicitation" and its penalties.
- I did not confirm whether any FL, NC, MD or WV clerk requires a bulk-user agreement that bars resale.

## 6. Response deadlines

### Takeaway
Deadlines range from "as promptly as possible" in NC to 30 days in MD.

### Cited Findings
- **DE**: As soon as possible, and no later than 15 business days (2019 text; I did not see the wording in the 2020 or 2025 extracts). — [Justia 2019 § 10003](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/2019/title-29/chapter-100/section-10003/)
- **MD**: 30 days, with notice within 10 working days if the request will take longer (§ 4-203) [background, not verified this session].
- **DC**: 15 business days under § 2-532(c)–(d), as an agency FAQ states it. Body-camera requests get 25 days. — [DCHR FAQ](https://dchr.dc.gov/node/1632766); [DC Code § 2-532](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/2-532)
- **VA**: 5 working days, extendable by 7 [background, not verified this session]. — [Va. Code § 2.2-3704](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter37/section2.2-3704/)
- **WV**: 5 business days. — [RCFP WV](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/west-virginia/); [MuckRock WV](https://www.muckrock.com/place/united-states-of-america/west-virginia/)
- **NC**: No fixed deadline; "as promptly as possible" [background, not verified this session].
- **SC**: § 30-4-30(C) cut the period for written requests from 15 to 10 days. Business-day counting is not confirmed. A longer period applies to records more than 24 months old [background]. — [SC OIG CofC FOIA review](https://oig.sc.gov/sites/default/files/Documents/Reports/2019/CofC_FOIA_Review-Final.pdf)
- **GA**: 3 business days to produce or give a timeline (§ 50-18-71(b)(1)(A)) [background, not verified this session].
- **FL**: No fixed deadline; a "reasonable time," with only delay needed to retrieve, redact and copy allowed [background, not verified this session].

### Gaps
- Deadlines for MD, VA, NC, GA and FL need checking against current statute text.

## 7. Recorder-specific statutes and pricing for bulk or subscription data

### Takeaway
VA has an explicit paid secure-remote-access regime. GA's statewide index belongs to the GSCCCA, which sells per-user subscriptions. MD's statewide MDLandRec is free to view but offers no bulk product I could find. I found no NC or WV bulk price list.

### Cited Findings
- **VA**: §§ 17.1-294 and 17.1-276 cover remote land-records access: up to $2 per transaction plus a per-image download fee. The general public is not required to use remote access, so in-office viewing remains. — [§ 17.1-276](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title17.1/chapter2/section17.1-276/); [§ 17.1-294](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title17.1/chapter2/section17.1-294/)
- **GA (GSCCCA)**: Premium search costs $29.95 per month (a staging page shows $24.95; pricing reportedly changed July 1, 2025). Regular costs $14.95 per month, and a single-use activation is $5. A bulk application exists for the UCC Central Index at $200 per day, $750 per week, or $1,500 per month. I found no real-estate-index bulk price. — [GSCCCA Premium Search](https://www.gsccca.org/learn/premium-search); [GSCCCA Compare Accounts](https://account.gsccca.org/CompareAccounts.asp); [GSCCCA UCC bulk application](https://www.gsccca.org/docs/ucc-documents/bulkapp.pdf?sfvrsn=2)
- **MD (MDLandRec)**: Online viewing and printing of land records is free with a registered account. The site moved to landrec.msa.maryland.gov, and old accounts were not migrated. I found no bulk or data-license terms. — [MSA MdLandRec intro](https://msa.maryland.gov/msa/refserv/pdf/md-landrecords.pdf); [Montgomery Co. Circuit Court](https://www.montgomerycountymd.gov/circuit-court/how-do-i/land-records)
- **NC**: G.S. 161-10 sets uniform register of deeds fees, including copies and index pages supplied as a public convenience. Wake County explains its free online data by citing the policy that public records are the property of the people. — [G.S. 161-10](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_161/GS_161-10.pdf); [Wake ROD](https://www.wake.gov/departments-government/register-deeds/recording-and-real-estate/recording-and-real-estate-help/why-data-available-online)
- **NC GIS**: G.S. 132-10 restricts resale of geographic information systems databases [background, not verified this session].
- **Bulk-pricing background**: PRIA's paper on bulk land records discusses cost-based bulk-user pricing. It is older material. — [PRIA Bulk Records](https://member.pria.us/files/resource_library_files/RAPP/Bulk_Records_Adoption_Final.pdf)
- **DC**: The Recorder of Deeds fee page is a recording-fee schedule, e.g., $25 for most documents; it lists a surcharge of $6.50 on one version and $5.00 on another. It has no bulk-data price. — [DC ROD fee charges](https://otr.cfo.dc.gov/service/recorder-deeds-fee-charges)
- **FL**: § 28.24 has per-page clerk fees and its electronic-format provisions (see Q2). — [flsenate § 28.24](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2019/28.24)

### Gaps
- I found no price lists for DE Recorder of Deeds subscriptions (New Castle, Kent, Sussex), WV county clerk vendor subscriptions, SC ROD bulk data, NC ROD bulk exports, or FL clerk bulk OR data (Miami-Dade, Broward).
- I found no MD Judiciary bulk-data agreement for land records.

## 8. Known free statewide or county bulk recorder data

### Takeaway
Free bulk is confirmed for Hillsborough County, FL, which posts official-records daily index files. MD's MDLandRec and GA's GSCCCA are statewide but searched one record at a time; GSCCCA is paid. NC counties such as Wake publish online, but I confirmed no bulk download.

### Cited Findings
- **FL, Hillsborough Clerk**: Free public data files, including official-records daily indexes. This project already has an adapter for them. — [Hillsborough public data files](https://www.hillsclerk.com/records-and-reports/public-data-files); [OR daily indexes](https://publicrec.hillsclerk.com/OfficialRecords/DailyIndexes/)
- **MD**: Statewide land records are free to view at MDLandRec (MSA), one record at a time. — [MSA MdLandRec intro](https://msa.maryland.gov/msa/refserv/pdf/md-landrecords.pdf)
- **GA**: The GSCCCA statewide real estate index is paid (see Q7). — [GSCCCA Premium Search](https://www.gsccca.org/learn/premium-search)
- **NC**: Wake ROD data is online for free. — [Wake ROD](https://www.wake.gov/departments-government/register-deeds/recording-and-real-estate/recording-and-real-estate-help/why-data-available-online)
- **WV**: Harrison County runs an online land-records search, according to a third-party site. — [harrisonrecords.org (third-party)](https://harrisonrecords.org/)

### Inferences
- Other FL clerks likely publish similar daily OR index files, since Hillsborough's are standard Florida clerk exports. That is worth a targeted check of Pinellas, Orange, Duval and other large counties.

### Gaps

## 9. Scores: free or near-free machine-readable recorder index through a records request (1–5)

### Takeaway
FL, GA, NC and WV score best. DE and VA score lowest because of their citizen-only rules.

### Cited Findings (basis cited in Q2–Q8)
- **Florida: 4.** Any requester, duplication-cost baseline, a "medium requested" rule, and at least one clerk (Hillsborough) posting free daily OR indexes. The risks are a special service charge for "extensive" IT use and Seigle's "existing programs" limit.
- **Georgia: 4.** § 50-18-71 explicitly requires flat-file exports through existing programs, including search and filter commands, at media cost plus lowest-paid staff time. The risk is that clerks redirect requesters to the paid GSCCCA, or claim court-clerk status.
- **North Carolina: 4.** Any person, actual-cost copies, and any medium the agency can produce. The risks are a special service charge, § 132-6.2(e) "no duty to create," and G.S. 161-10 index-page fees.
- **West Virginia: 4.** No search or retrieval fees after 2015, reproduction cost only, any person, and 5 days. The risks are county clerk fee statutes and vendor-held databases; this rests on statute, with practice unverified.
- **Maryland: 3.** § 4-205 "searchable and analyzable" format and 2 free hours, with free MDLandRec viewing. The risk is that land records sit with the Judiciary and MSA, where court-records rules may govern bulk access.
- **District of Columbia: 3.** Any person, but commercial requesters pay search, review and duplication, with a 15-day clock. I found no ROD bulk product.
- **South Carolina: 3.** Cost-capped fees and 10 days, but staff time is billable. The § 30-2-50 commercial-solicitation crime is a use restriction a judgment buyer must respect.
- **Virginia: 2.** Citizens only (upheld in McBurney), clerks sell remote access under §§ 17.1-276 and 17.1-294, and nothing requires formats the office doesn't regularly use. This rises to 3 if the requester is a real VA citizen, given the database-extract provisions of § 2.2-3704.
- **Delaware: 2.** Citizens only, and staff time is billable after 1 hour. Recorder online access is a paid subscription [background]. I found no database-extract authority.

### Inferences
- These scores are analytic judgments built on the cited law. Real outcomes depend on each county's vendor, such as Tyler/Eagle, Kofile or Landmark, and on whether the clerk will run a standard export.

### Gaps
- None of the scores reflects observed request outcomes. I found no MuckRock-style records of past bulk recorder-index requests in these states.
