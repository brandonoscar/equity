## 1. 2026 homestead amounts against judgment creditors, by state, with declaration and tenancy-by-the-entirety (TBE) rules

### Takeaway
Seven states have no dollar cap, only acreage limits: AR, FL, IA, KS, OK, SD and TX, plus DC and PR. Florida and Texas are the strongest, because their exemptions are constitutional. NJ and PA give no homestead protection; PA has only a $300 wildcard. DE and MD protect a home only in bankruptcy. Since the NCLC table (Dec 2025), at least seven states have changed or indexed their amounts: MN, NH, OR, SC, UT, MT, and GA (bankruptcy only). MO has a $40,000 increase reported for 2027 but not confirmed. A recorded declaration is a precondition in NV, MT, UT and VA, and is needed in MA for the full amount.

- For equity screening, "unlimited" states (FL, TX, KS, IA, OK, SD, AR, DC) put a judgment creditor out of reach of an owner-occupied homestead, except for the lien types those constitutions allow. Declaration states (NV, MT, UT, VA, and MA above $125k) can be tested from recorder indexes for a recorded "Declaration of Homestead" document type. Where no declaration is recorded, the home is exposed in NV and MT.
- TBE "Shield" states (FL, PA, MD, VA, DC, HI, IN, MI, MO, VT, WY, DE, NC, IL homestead, MA residence) need a flag for married co-owner deeds. A judgment against one spouse cannot reach those homes, even in states with a low homestead cap (for example PA, MD, VA).

## 2. Is there a maintained authoritative compilation, and how current is it?

### Takeaway
NCLC's *No Fresh Start* is the best nonprofit compilation of non-bankruptcy judgment-creditor exemptions. Its 2025 edition (Table 2, Dec 2025; State Summaries, Jan 2026) predates the mid-2026 changes. The Alper Law charts (Aug 2026) are more current but are a private law firm's. Federal amounts are authoritative only in the Federal Register.

## 3. Free, lawful structured sources of imminent sale data (tax lien and deed, sheriff, foreclosure, land bank and forfeiture)

### Takeaway
No national or statewide machine-readable feed exists. The auction platforms (RealAuction/realtaxdeed/realforeclose, LienHub, GovEase, Bid4Assets, SRI/Zeus) are login-gated, block bots, or are search-only. ## 4. Surplus and excess proceeds lists, and whether judgment creditors can claim them

### Takeaway
After *Tyler v. Hennepin County* (2023), states must return surplus equity after a tax sale. Many statutes pay **recorded lienholders before the owner**: FL (tax deed and judicial foreclosure), CA (tax-defaulted sale), NV, and GA "in order of priority". So a judgment creditor whose lien was recorded before the sale can often claim surplus. In GA a judgment lien alone has been held insufficient, and MI limits claims to holders of a "legal interest". Lists are published county by county, mostly as PDFs. A few are XLSX or ArcGIS layers, and GA DeKalb and NV Washoe are already in the repo.

### Cited Findings
**Creditor-priority rules**
- **Florida tax deed (F.S. 197.582)**: surplus goes first to governmental units for liens of record. The balance is held for persons in 197.522(1)(a), "as their interests may appear". The statutory notice says funds "will be used to satisfy in full each claimant with a senior mortgage or lien … before distribution … to any junior mortgage or lien claimant or to the former property owner". Claims are due within 120 days of the notice. — [F.S. 197.582 (2025)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/197.582) [PRIMARY]. Per AG Op. 2020-01, a non-governmental lienholder that misses the deadline is barred. — [FL AG opinion](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/node/1468) [SEARCH]
- **Florida judicial foreclosure (F.S. 45.032)**: a "subordinate lienholder" expressly "includes … a subordinate mortgage, judgment, tax warrant, assessment lien, or construction lien" shown on the pleadings. The rebuttable presumption is that the owner of record at lis pendens is entitled to surplus "after payment of subordinate lienholders who have timely filed a claim". — [F.S. 45.032](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/45.032) [PRIMARY]
- **California tax-defaulted sale (R&T 4675)**: excess proceeds go "First, lienholders of record prior to the recordation of the tax deed … in the order of their priority", then title holders. Claims are due within 1 year of recording the tax deed. — [R&T 4675](https://california.public.law/codes/revenue_and_taxation_code_section_4675); [SCO Excess Proceeds Guide](https://sco.ca.gov/Files-ARD-Tax-Info/Tax-Collector-Ref-Man/SCO_ExcessProceedsGuide.pdf) [SEARCH]
- **Nevada (NRS 361.610)**: the county keeps the first $300 and 10% of the next $10,000. Claims are due within 1 year after the treasurer's deed is recorded. They are paid "in the order of priority of the liens recorded or perfected before the sale" to persons in NRS 361.585(4)(b),(c),(d),(g),(h),(i) and certain HOAs, and then to persons in 361.585(4)(a),(e),(f). Unclaimed funds go to the county and "must not thereafter be refunded". — [NRS 361](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-361.html) [PRIMARY]. Which 361.585(4) paragraph covers judgment creditors was not read [UNVERIFIED]. The 1-year forfeiture may be open to challenge after *Tyler* [inference].
- **Georgia (O.C.G.A. 48-4-5)**: excess goes "to the owner or owners as their interests appear in the order of priority". Notice goes to parties with a recorded equity interest or claim at the time of sale. *DLT List* (Ga.) overruled the rule giving redeeming creditors first priority. In *Denhardt*, "a judgment lien, without more, does not entitle him to a distribution". Unclaimed funds go to DOR after 5 years. — [Justia 48-4-5](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2020/title-48/chapter-4/article-1/section-48-4-5); [DLT List v. M7VEN](https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2015/a15a1485.html); [Fulton excess funds application](https://www.fultoncountyga.gov/-/media/Departments/Sheriff/Tax-Sales-Excess-Funds-Application_Updated1.pdf) [SEARCH; Denhardt citation not retrieved]
- **Michigan (MCL 211.78t, post-*Rafaeli*)**: a "claimant" is "a person with a legal interest in property immediately before the effectiveness of a judgment of foreclosure". A notice of intention is due by July 1 after foreclosure. — [MCL 211.78t](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-78t) [PRIMARY]. Whether a judgment lienholder counts as holding a "legal interest" is UNVERIFIED.
- **Minnesota post-*Tyler***: 2024 legislation created a surplus-claim process for interested parties, with unclaimed surplus going to the county forfeited-tax-sale fund. — [MN House 2024 summary](https://www.house.mn.gov/NewLaws/story/2024/5584) [SEARCH; enacted text not read]
- **Tyler v. Hennepin County**, 598 U.S. 631 (2023): keeping surplus beyond the tax debt is a taking. — [Opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/22-166_3e04.pdf) [UNVERIFIED URL from memory; holding confirmed by [Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2023/sep/surplus-from-homes-tax-forfeiture-sale-must-be-returned-to-owner-supreme-court-holds.html)]

### Inferences
- Judgment-creditor surplus claims are strongest in FL (45.032 names judgments; 197.582 pays senior liens first), CA (4675 lienholders first) and NV (recorded-lien priority). In each case the lien must be recorded before the sale or deed, and the claim filed within the window: FL 120 days for tax deed, CA 1 year, NV 1 year. GA needs more than a bare judgment lien.
- Surplus lists are almost never structured data. A repo adapter would mostly parse PDFs, which carry APN, former owner, sale date, excess amount and claim deadline. Each item should carry its claim deadline so items can be ranked before they expire.
