# Verification 1: voter-file verdicts for DC, NJ, CT, RI, FL, SC, NC, AR, MS and AL, checked against primary text

Use case: a private company's internal records database that locates people and links them to property and debt records. No redistribution, no solicitation, no mailing. Law is current as of 2026-11-25. This is legal research for internal planning, not legal advice. Every source below was read on **2026-11-25** with curl from this container, unless a line says it came from a search snippet.

**Bottom line.** Five verdicts changed after reading the primary text:

- **Florida** moves to ALLOWED. The old § 98.095 use limit was repealed around 2005.
- **Rhode Island** moves to ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. The political-use limit in § 17-6-5 binds only lists sold to party chairs and candidates. The public-records route is purpose-blind by statute.
- **New Jersey** keeps its verdict, but a statutory commercial-solicitation ban was missed earlier. The statute also lets only a "voter" request the list.
- **Connecticut** is confirmed NOT ALLOWED, but the vehicle is **PA 26-1, § 96**, not SB 1516. SB 1516 died on the Senate calendar.
- **Alabama** is confirmed NOT ALLOWED as **Act 2026-570**, effective June 1, 2026. The enrolled text does **not** withhold birth month and day, and it has no penalty clause.

## District of Columbia: governing section, use restriction, BOE request terms

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED (tentative). Verified: ALLOWED.** The D.C. Code (§§ 1-1001.05 and 1-1001.07) requires a public voter list and has no use restriction. The BOE gives the full list free through a "secure link", using a request form under the D.C. FOIA timeline. The form asks for no statement of purpose and carries no use terms.

### Cited Findings
- § 1-1001.05(a)(1): the Board shall "Accurately maintain a uniform, interactive computerized voter registration list which shall serve as the official voter registration list for all elections in the District, and shall contain the name, registration information, and a unique identifier assigned for every registered voter." — [D.C. Code § 1-1001.05](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-1001.05)
- § 1-1001.07(h)(1): "No later than 45 days preceding any election ... the Board shall cause a District-wide alphabetical list of qualified electors registered to vote in the District to be placed in the main public library and shall cause an alphabetical ward list ... to be placed in each branch library." Section (h)(2) adds a copy of the list as of the date the registry closed, posted in public buildings for at least 14 days before each election. — [D.C. Code § 1-1001.07](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-1001.07)
- The confidentiality provisions in § 1-1001.07 cover only (1) the fact that someone declined to register and (2) the voter registration agency used: "will remain confidential and will be used only for voter registration purposes" (§ 1-1001.07(c)), and "the Board shall ensure that the identity of the voter registration agency through which any particular individual is registered to vote is not disclosed to the public" (§ 1-1001.07(d)(10)). I found no clause limiting how the voter list itself may be used. A text search of the section for "commercial", "sale" and "purchase" turned up nothing relevant. — [D.C. Code § 1-1001.07](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-1001.07); [§ 1-1001.05](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-1001.05)
- BOE request form ("Rev. 3/7/23"): "E-mail or Secure Link of Voter Registration Data – Microsoft Excel (No Charge) (The entire voter registration list can only be obtained through a secure link.)" The options are a list with or without voter history. The requester gives a name or group, address, phone, contact person, email, signature and initials. The form cites "the District of Columbia Freedom of Information Act (D.C. Official Code § 2-532(c)) permits up to 15 business days to fill a request." It has no purpose field and no use-restriction affirmation. — [DCBOE Data Request Form 2023 (PDF)](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf)
- The online version of the same form, "Data Request Form 02062023", has the same fields and terms and adds a "Voter Registration List With Voter History? No / Yes" choice. — [vr.dcboe.org online form](https://vr.dcboe.org/230363516728053)
- Context: a December 2025 U.S. DOJ complaint says DCBOE refused to give DOJ the *full* (unredacted) list. That dispute is about confidential fields, not the public list. (Search snippet only; not read.) — [DOJ complaint](https://www.justice.gov/crt/media/1421476/dl)

### Inferences
- This confirms the earlier tentative verdict. Nothing restricts use, the list is free, and no affidavit is required. For our use, DC is the most permissive jurisdiction in this batch.
- What changed: the governing provisions are now identified and quoted. They are §§ 1-1001.05(a)(1) and 1-1001.07(h), with access through D.C. FOIA (§ 2-532). DOB was not checked against the actual file. § 1-1001.07(c)(2)(A) shows DOB is collected, but the form does not list fields.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a DCMR (Title 3, ch. 5) section on voter-list sales or copies. A search found none, and dcregs.dc.gov was not reached. If such a section exists, it would be the only likely place for a use term.
- The fields in the file (DOB, phone, email) were not confirmed from a layout or sample.
- I did not search the DC Council LIMS for pending 2026 bills; a general web search found none.

## New Jersey: N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1 text, use restriction, fee, DOB, Daniel's Law

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. Verified: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS, with new conditions.** The current text (as amended by P.L. 2021, c. 371) **does** contain a use restriction that the earlier notes missed. It bans using the lists "as a basis for commercial or charitable solicitation" and makes that a disorderly-persons offense with a fine of up to $500. That ban does not reach an internal locate-and-link database that sends no solicitations. The statute provides copies to "any voter". It requires Daniel's Law covered-person addresses to be left out, and caps county electronic copies at $375.

### Cited Findings
- **19:31-18.1(a)** (as amended by P.L. 2021, c. 371, § 6): "The county clerk ... shall furnish to any voter applying for the same such copies, charging therefor $0.25 per copy of the list of voters of each election district... In no case shall a list of registered voters furnished pursuant to this section include voter signatures or, except as otherwise provided in section 3 of P.L.2021, c.371 (C.47:1B-3), the home address of a covered person, as defined in section 1 of P.L.2021, c.371 (C.47:1B-1), who has received approval from the Office of Information Privacy for the redaction or nondisclosure of the covered person's address. The county clerk shall satisfy the request by delivery of a computer-generated or electronic copy of the list for the county from the Statewide voter registration system." — [P.L. 2021, c. 371 (PDF), § 6](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF)
- **19:31-18.1(b)**: "The commissioner of registration shall furnish a computer-generated or electronic copy of a list of registered voters in any or all election districts in the county to any voter requesting it, for which copy such commissioner shall make a charge which shall be uniform in any calendar year and which shall reflect only the cost of reproducing the list, but which in any case shall not exceed $375." — [P.L. 2021, c. 371, § 6](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF)
- **19:31-18.1(c) (use restriction)**: "No person shall use voter registration lists or copies thereof prepared pursuant to this section as a basis for commercial or charitable solicitation of the voters listed thereon. Any person making such use of such lists or copies thereof shall be a disorderly person, and shall be punished by a fine not exceeding $500.00." — [P.L. 2021, c. 371, § 6](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF)
- Daniel's Law (P.L. 2021, c. 371) lets "approved covered persons" have their addresses redacted. It also lists categories that receive *unredacted* recorded real-property documents "in such person's ordinary course of business": title insurers and agents, mortgage guarantee insurers, mortgage loan originators, registered title search businesses, real estate licensees, and parties to a purchase offer. — [P.L. 2021, c. 371, § 3](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF)
- OPRA as amended by P.L. 2024, c. 16, matters if the statewide file is requested from the state under OPRA. It defines "Commercial purpose" as "the direct or indirect use of any part of a government record for sale, resale, solicitation, rent, or lease of a service or any use by which the user expects a profit either through commission, salary, or fee." The request form must let the requester "certify whether the government record will be used by that requestor or another person for a commercial purpose, and the requestor shall be required to provide this information for the request to be fulfilled." Commercial requests get 14 business days instead of 7. — [GRC readable OPRA (Sept 2024)](https://nj.gov/grc/laws/act/Readable%20Version%20of%20OPRA%20(Sept%202024)(P.L.%202024,%20c.16).pdf)
- Pending, not enacted: A4436 and S3605 (both introduced Feb 19, 2026) "Expands 'Daniel's Law' protections; requires Office of Information Privacy to establish portal for prohibiting disclosure of personal information by private entities and establishes penalties for failure to comply." Both are "As introduced." — [A4436](https://pub.njleg.gov/Bills/2026/A4500/4436_I1.HTM); [S3605](https://pub.njleg.gov/Bills/2026/S4000/3605_I1.HTM)
- DOB (secondary source): a PILF data review says 15,655 NJ registrants "are flagged for having no dates of birth in the public record". That implies DOB is normally in the public file. (Search snippet; not read.) — [PILF 2025 NJ overview](https://publicinterestlegal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/7.16.2025-NJ-Data-Findings-Overview.pdf)

### Inferences
- What changed: the statute has an express solicitation ban, § 19:31-18.1(c), which is a disorderly-persons offense. It does not cover internal locate and link with no solicitation, but it rules out any later marketing or outreach use. A debt-collection contact program built on the file is a grey area under "commercial ... solicitation". Keep the file out of any contact workflow.
- Requester eligibility: (a) and (b) supply lists to "any voter". A company may have to request through an NJ-registered individual. Alternatively it can use OPRA at the state level, which requires a commercial-purpose certification but no purpose limit. Whether a non-voter or corporate OPRA requester can get the statewide SVRS extract was not confirmed.
- Daniel's Law is now built into the list: covered persons' addresses are left out at the source. The risk to us is re-linking covered persons' addresses from other sources (such as property records) and then disclosing them. Under the 2021 Act, recorded property documents can be redacted except for the listed business categories, and we are not one of them.
- The fee is ≤ $375 per county electronic copy, or $0.25 per election-district printed list. No statewide fee was found.

### Gaps
- No post-2021 amendment to § 19:31-18.1 was found, but I could not open the official NJ statutes database (lis.njleg.state.nj.us) to confirm the current compiled text. The text above is the 2021 chapter-law version.
- I did not confirm the Division of Elections statewide request process or fee, or whether DOB is in the public extract (only a secondary source was found).
- I did not read the private-right-of-action parts of Daniel's Law (C.56:8-166.1); details remain from background knowledge.

### Inferences
- What changed: the enacting vehicle is PA 26-1 § 96 (SB 298, 2026), not SB 1516. The codified section is § 9-50d, not § 9-50a. The effective date is passage, with the governor's signature on March 3, 2026. The penalty is a civil fine of up to $2,000 per offense from SEEC.
- Our use is a "private or commercial purpose". Clause (III) separately bars "display in any other format", which an internal lookup screen would plausibly violate. CT is closed for this use. Any CT records bought before March 3, 2026 are still subject to the *use* ban going forward, because the statute regulates use, not just acquisition.
- The public file now carries birth **year** only. Under prior law it carried month and year.

## Rhode Island: § 17-6-5 and related sections; is use limited to political purposes?

### Takeaway
**Prior: NOT ALLOWED (lean; sources conflicted). Verified: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS.** The political-use limit in § 17-6-5(b) applies only to lists "so furnished" under § 17-6-5, which means SoS sales to state party chairs and candidates, "and to no one else." Separately, registration lists are public records (§ 17-9.1-15). § 17-9.1-21 says nothing prevents "any member of the general public from obtaining the same information under the access to Public Records Acts". APRA § 38-2-3(j) bars withholding records "based on the purpose for which the records are sought". The Secretary of State's office says "the full voter file is now available upon request", including full DOB (Aug 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **§ 17-6-5(a)–(b)**: the SoS shall furnish lists "to the state chairperson of each political party and to each person proposing to be a duly qualified candidate for state or congressional office and to no one else except as provided in this chapter." "(b) These lists, so furnished, shall be used by the chairpersons for political purposes, or by the candidates only in the furtherance of candidacy for political office in the ensuing primary and/or general election and for no other purpose. The information available for each registrant shall consist only of the name, designation of party affiliation, street address, city or town, congressional district, senatorial district, representative district, ward, and/or precinct and nothing else." (History: P.L. 1976, ch. 207, § 1; unamended.) — [R.I. Gen. Laws § 17-6-5](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-6/17-6-5.htm)
- **§ 17-9.1-15**: "(a) ... the original and any duplicate registration cards shall be public records and shall be open to public inspection and copying at all reasonable times. (b) ... the registration lists prepared pursuant to § 17-9.1-21, shall also be deemed public records." — [§ 17-9.1-15](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-9.1/17-9.1-15.htm)
- **§ 17-9.1-21** (lists to parties and candidates) ends: "Nothing in this section shall be construed to prevent any member of the general public from obtaining the same information under the access to Public Records Acts, chapter 2 of title 38." — [§ 17-9.1-21](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-9.1/17-9.1-21.htm)
- **§ 17-10-14**: "The voting list shall be deemed a public record; and a copy of the list shall be made available, upon request, to any elector within the city or town upon payment of no more than the actual cost of reproduction." — [§ 17-10-14](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-10/17-10-14.htm)
- **§ 17-6-9**: "All requests for registration lists, and the action taken on those requests by the secretary of state, shall be deemed to be public records." — [§ 17-6-9](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-6/17-6-9.htm)
- **APRA § 38-2-3(j)**: "No public records shall be withheld based on the purpose for which the records are sought, nor shall a public body require, as a condition of fulfilling a public records request, that a person or entity provide a reason for the request or provide personally identifiable information about him/herself." — [R.I. Gen. Laws § 38-2-3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE38/38-2/38-2-3.htm)
- SoS practice (Providence Journal, Aug 1, 2024): Secretary Amore "canceled a policy set by his predecessor that blocked people from getting databases listing registered voters with their full birthdates". His spokeswoman said: "the full voter file is now available upon request ... Dates of birth in the voter file are public information." — [Providence Journal via AOL](https://aol.com/full-transparency-restored-ri-secretary-085927992.html)
- 2026 session: the official 2026 public-laws list shows election measures (primary date, mail-ballot processing, write-ins, party-voter lists for vacancies). None amends §§ 17-6-5, 17-9.1-15 or 17-10-14, judging by titles. — [RI 2026 Public Laws list](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Lawrevision/plshort/pl2026bil.htm)

### Inferences
- What changed: the earlier "political purposes only" reading (from NCSL) describes § 17-6-5, the party and candidate sales channel. It does not describe the public-records channel. A records request for the voter file under APRA and §§ 17-9.1-15 and 17-9.1-21 carries no statutory use restriction, and the agency may not ask why we want it. The 2018 CT OLR view ("no restrictions on access to or use") fits the statutes better than NCSL does.
- Conditions: (1) request under APRA and the public-records sections, not § 17-6-5, and do not represent yourself as a candidate or party; (2) treat confidential fields (DL, partial SSN, address-confidentiality participants) as unavailable; (3) recheck SoS terms, since the office could attach a use statement by policy, and the request form was not reviewed. There is residual tension: § 17-6-5(a)'s "to no one else" could be read as limiting SoS *list sales* generally. But § 17-9.1-21 and § 38-2-3(j) expressly preserve public access.

### Gaps
- I did not find or read the current SoS voter-file request form, fee or delivery terms.
- I did not verify whether the SoS or DOJ litigation (2025–2026) changed public-file contents.

## Florida: current Fla. Stat. § 98.095 / § 97.0585; is use of the extract limited?

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS (unverified). Verified: ALLOWED.** § 98.095, with its 2004 "elections, political or governmental activities, voter registration, or law enforcement" limit and oath, **does not exist** in the official Florida Statutes for 2005 or any later year, including 2025 and 2026. Chapters 97 and 98 (2026) contain no "commercial" use limit. § 97.0585 makes only specific items confidential (declinations, place of registration, SSN, DL and FL ID numbers, 16–17-year-old preregistrants, signatures). The DOS extract is "public record" and includes full birth date, phone and email.

### Cited Findings
- 2004 § 98.095 required an oath that "the information acquired will be used only for the purposes prescribed in that section and for no other purpose; and that I will not permit the use or copying of such information by persons not authorized by the Election Code". — [Fla. Stat. § 98.095 (2004)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2004/98.095)
- Requests for `/Laws/Statutes/2005/98.095`, `/2006/98.095`, `/2024/98.095` and `/2026/98.095` on the Senate statutes site all redirect to the year's index page, because the section does not exist. The 2025 Chapter 98 full text lists only §§ 98.015, .035, .045, .065, .0655, .075, .0751, .0755, .077, .081, .093, .0981, .161, .212, .255, .301 and .461. There is no § 98.095. — [Fla. Stat. ch. 98 (2025), all sections](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter98/All); [2026 ch. 98](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter98/All)
- A text search of the 2026 Chapters 97 and 98 found zero occurrences of "commercial". The only "may be used only" language covers declinations and § 97.0585 data ("may be used only for voter registration purposes"). — [ch. 97 (2026)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter97/All); [ch. 98 (2026)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter98/All)
- **§ 97.0585(1)**: "The following information held by an agency ... and obtained for the purpose of voter registration is confidential and exempt from s. 119.07(1) ... and may be used only for purposes of voter registration: (a) All declinations to register to vote ... (b) Information relating to the place where a person registered to vote or where a person updated a voter registration. (c) The social security number, driver license number, and Florida identification number of a voter registration applicant or voter. (d) All information concerning preregistered voter registration applicants who are 16 or 17 years of age. (2) The signature of a voter registration applicant or a voter is exempt from the copying requirements..." (History ends "s. 1, ch. 2024-39", unchanged in 2026.) — [Fla. Stat. § 97.0585 (2025)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/97.0585); [2026](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/97.0585)

- DOS extract page (last updated Nov 4, 2026): "Voter registration and voting history information is public record under Florida law. However, some information is exempt from disclosure based on applicable law." Requesters provide "contact information and either a phone number or an email address, and a mailing address"; the extract "IS NOT available by email or online." "A one-time request may be made each year to receive all monthly extract disks." ## South Carolina: § 7-3-20(D)(13) and § 30-2-50, scope of the ban, who may buy

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS (high ambiguity). Verified: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. The use question is now clear; the buyer gate is the obstacle.** § 30-2-50 bans only obtaining or using personal information "for commercial solicitation". § 30-2-30(3) defines that narrowly as "contact by telephone, mail, or electronic mail for the purpose of selling or marketing a consumer product or service." It is **not** a general commercial-use ban, so an internal database with no outbound marketing falls outside it. But § 7-3-20(D)(13) lets the SEC furnish lists only "to a qualified elector requesting them". The SEC says "You must be a registered South Carolina voter to purchase a list."

### Cited Findings
- **§ 7-3-20(D)(13)**: the executive director shall "furnish at reasonable price any precinct lists to a qualified elector requesting them". — [S.C. Code Title 7, Ch. 3](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t07c003.php)
- **§ 30-2-50**: "(A) A person or private entity shall not knowingly obtain or use personal information obtained from a state agency, a local government, or other political subdivision of the State for commercial solicitation directed to any person in this State. ... (C) All state agencies ... shall take reasonable measures to ensure that no person or private entity obtains or distributes personal information obtained from a public record for commercial solicitation. (D) A person knowingly violating the provisions of subsection (A) is guilty of a misdemeanor and, upon conviction, must be fined an amount not to exceed five hundred dollars or imprisoned for a term not to exceed one year, or both." (History: 2002 Act No. 225; 2003 Act No. 20; 2017 Act No. 67.) — [S.C. Code Title 30, Ch. 2](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t30c002.php)
- **§ 30-2-30(3)**: "'Commercial solicitation' means contact by telephone, mail, or electronic mail for the purpose of selling or marketing a consumer product or service." It excludes credit-union membership offers, continuing-education notices, GLBA-regulated financial or insurance marketing, and "(d) contacting persons for political purposes using information on file with state or local voter registration offices." § 30-2-30(1) defines "personal information" broadly (name, home address, DOB and more). — [S.C. Code Title 30, Ch. 2](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t30c002.php)
- SEC list-sales page: "You must be a registered South Carolina voter to purchase a list (S.C. Code 7-3-20(D)(13))." The electronic file "Includes: Name, Address, Race, Gender, Date of Birth, Voter Registration Number and Date Registered, County, Precinct, Voting Districts, and Voter Participation History (past two statewide primaries and general elections." Fees run from $25 to a $2,500 maximum for electronic lists. The page repeats the § 30-2-50 notice and penalty. — [SC SEC Sale of Voter Registration Lists](https://scvotes.gov/resources/sale-of-voter-registration-lists/)
- Related, but not about voter files: § 30-2-60 (2026 Act No. 214, effective Jan 1, 2027) requires eviction filings and records to be removed from public indexes seven years after final disposition or filing if nothing further is filed. This matters for our debt-record sources. — [S.C. Code Title 30, Ch. 2](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t30c002.php)

### Inferences
- What changed: the ambiguity about scope is resolved in our favor. The 2018 W&M summary ("cannot use it for commercial purposes") overstates the statute. The ban needs outbound phone, mail or email contact to sell or market a consumer product or service.
- The remaining conditions are real. (1) Only a "qualified elector" (an SC-registered voter) may buy, so a company would need an SC-registered employee or agent as purchaser, and the SEC may treat a purchase for a corporate principal as outside § 7-3-20(D)(13). (2) Never feed SC voter data into any marketing contact. Debt-collection contact is not "selling or marketing a consumer product", but avoid that use without counsel review. (3) Full DOB is included, which makes the file valuable for linking.

### Gaps
- I did not determine whether the SEC verifies that the buyer is buying in a personal capacity, or what attestation the online portal shows at checkout (the portal was not opened).

## Alabama: 2026 HB67, enactment, Act number, effective date, language, penalty

### Takeaway
**Prior: NOT ALLOWED (from 2026). Verified: NOT ALLOWED (confirmed).** HB67 is **Act 2026-570**. It was enacted April 17, 2026 and took effect **June 1, 2026** (ALISON).
