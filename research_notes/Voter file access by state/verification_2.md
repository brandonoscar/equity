# Voter file verification, round 2: AK, CO, ID, NV, TX, UT, WA, AZ, IN (primary-text check)

Use case tested: a private company's internal records database used to locate people and link them to property and debt records. No redistribution, no solicitation, no mailing.

Method: Every quote below was read on 2026-11-25 from the official statute, rule or form page, using curl from this container, unless marked otherwise. Where an official page could not be fetched (Nevada SOS behind an Incapsula bot wall, the Washington SOS download page behind a Cloudflare challenge, iga.in.gov rendered only by JavaScript), this is said, and the closest official substitute is cited. This is legal research for internal planning, not legal advice.

## Summary of verified verdicts

### Takeaway
Two prior verdicts change. **Colorado** moves from "probably allowed" to **ALLOWED**: no statute restricts use, the SOS request form has no use certification, and the SOS says it releases the data "without regard to how these people might use the information." **Indiana county-level** moves from "ambiguous" to **ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS**: IC 3-7-27-6(c) applies "notwithstanding any other statute," and VRG-24 bars only solicitation and transfer. **Washington** stays **NOT ALLOWED**, but on a firmer basis, WAC 434-324-130(2). The other six verdicts stand, with sharper text: AK ALLOWED; ID ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS; TX ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS; NV, UT and AZ NOT ALLOWED.

## Alaska — AS 15.07.127 and 15.07.195

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED (verify). Verified: ALLOWED.** The current statute lets "any person" buy the list, subject only to the confidentiality rules in AS 15.07.195. There is no use, purpose or commercial restriction. The fee is set by the director. Released fields are name, residence and mailing addresses and party. DOB/age, voter ID number, SSN, DL number, place of birth and signature are confidential. **What changed:** nothing substantive. Primary text now confirms the snippet-based verdict.

### Cited Findings
- AS 15.07.127 (Alaska Statutes 2025, akleg.gov): "The director shall prepare both a statewide list and a list by precinct of the names and addresses of all persons whose names appear on the master register and their political party affiliation. Subject to the limitations of AS 15.07.195, any person may obtain a copy of the list, or a part of the list, or an electronic format containing both residence and mailing addresses of voters, by applying to the director and paying to the state treasury a fee as determined by the director." — [akleg.gov AS 15.07.127](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp?media=print&secStart=15.07.127&secEnd=15.07.127) (read 2026-11-25)
- AS 15.07.195(a): confidential and "not open to public inspection": "(1) the voter's age or date of birth; (2) the voter's social security number, or any part of that number; (3) the voter's driver's license number; (4) the voter's voter identification number; (5) the voter's place of birth; (6) the voter's signature." Under (b), a voter may elect in writing to keep the residential address confidential "if the voter provides a separate mailing address." — [akleg.gov AS 15.07.195](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp?media=print&secStart=15.07.195&secEnd=15.07.195) (read 2026-11-25)
- AS 15.07.195(c)(3): confidential information "may be released by the division … to a person holding a writ of execution against the person or property of the voter." — same URL
- The Division FAQ says "Alaska law allows for any person to request a statewide or house district voting list." — [Alaska DOE PFD AVR FAQ (rev. 12/11/2023)](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/info/PFD%20AVR%20FAQ.pdf) (cited by the prior researcher; not re-read)

### Inferences
- No use clause exists, so an internal locate/link database is lawful under the statute. Ordinary privacy and FCRA/FDCPA rules still apply to downstream debt-collection use. Those are outside this scope.
- (c)(3) matters for the debt use case: a judgment creditor holding a writ of execution can get otherwise confidential fields (such as DOB) for that specific debtor from the Division. This is a narrow, per-debtor route, not a bulk one.
- Without DOB, matching must rely on name and address.

### Gaps
- I could not find the current request form or fee. The old Division page (/research/voter-lists/) returns 404, and form B06 is the registrar handbook. I could not confirm whether the request form adds a use certification. Since the statute has none, any such certification would be administrative only.
- The akleg page is labeled "Alaska Statutes 2025." I did not check for any 2026 session amendment to these sections.

## Idaho — Idaho Code §§ 34-437, 34-437A; penalty; SOS acknowledgement

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. Verified: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS.** Both sections let "any person" buy the list at reproduction cost. They bar only using the information "for the purpose of mailing or delivering" advertisements, offers or solicitations, with a political-purpose exception. There is no general commercial-use bar. Neither section has a specific penalty, so the default election-offense penalty in § 18-2315 applies to willful violations: a fine up to $1,000, up to 5 years in state prison, or both. **What changed:** the penalty is now identified. The statutes were last amended in 2003.

### Cited Findings
- § 34-437A(3): "Any person desiring a copy of the statewide list of registered electors shall be furnished the same, and the secretary of state shall assess the individual an amount which will compensate the state for the cost of reproducing such copy. No person to whom a list of statewide electors is furnished and no person who acquires a list of statewide electors prepared from such list shall use any information contained therein for the purpose of mailing or delivering any advertisement or offer for any property, establishment, organization, product, or service or for the purpose of mailing or delivering any solicitation for money, services, or anything of value. Provided however, that any such list and label may be used for any political purpose." History: "added 1976 … am. 2003, ch. 48, sec. 8." — [legislature.idaho.gov § 34-437A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title34/T34CH4/SECT34-437A/) (read 2026-11-25)
- § 34-437(1)-(2) (county lists): the county clerk "shall supply to any individual, a current list of the registered electors of the county and their addresses." (2) repeats the same mailing/delivery ban and political-purpose proviso. Last amended in 2003 (ch. 48, sec. 7). — [legislature.idaho.gov § 34-437](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title34/T34CH4/SECT34-437/) (read 2026-11-25)
- § 18-2315: "Unless a different punishment is otherwise prescribed by law, every person who willfully violates any of the provisions of the laws of this state relating to elections is punishable by fine not exceeding $1,000, or by imprisonment in the state prison not exceeding five (5) years, or by both." — [legislature.idaho.gov § 18-2315](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title18/T18CH23/SECT18-2315/) (read 2026-11-25)
- The SOS order form and acknowledgement cite § 34-437A(3), and the signer acknowledges the restriction. This is from search extracts only: both SOS PDF URLs now return an HTML page instead of the PDF. — [Idaho SOS order form](https://sos.idaho.gov/downloads/PRR_Absentee_VoterReg_Report.pdf); [Idaho SOS acknowledgement](https://sos.idaho.gov/PressRelease/CommissionDetails/Voter%20Registration%20-%20Acknowledgement.pdf) (not readable 2026-11-25)

### Inferences
- An internal locate/link database with no mailing or delivery is outside the prohibited act. Using voter-derived addresses to mail or deliver collection letters ("solicitation for money") or property-purchase offers ("offer for any property") would fall inside it and could be charged under § 18-2315. A hard internal control is needed: voter-derived addresses must never be used for mail or delivered outreach, and should be flagged in the database.
- Redistribution is not barred by the text, but a person who "acquires a list … prepared from such list" is also bound. Keep the data internal.

### Gaps
- I could not read the current SOS acknowledgement or order-form text (the PDFs were moved) or confirm the current fee ($20 per prior notes) or the released fields (DOB vs. age).

## Nevada — NRS 293.440, 293.558; SOS administration

### Takeaway
**Prior: NOT ALLOWED (ambiguous). Verified: NOT ALLOWED as administered.** The statutory text is now confirmed as narrow. NRS 293.440(6)'s ban on non-election use and on sale binds only party committees, or their members and representatives, "who receive[] without charge" a list. Paying purchasers under 293.440(1) ("Any person … may obtain a copy … paying … 1 cent per name") are not named. The SOS statewide route (NevVoter) applies the election-only rule to everyone. Its 2026 Elections Procedures Manual (accepted by the Legislative Commission on 2025-12-17) states the rule for all recipients and cites NRS 293.440(6)(b), and the SOS verifies each applicant's "qualification." **What changed:** the ambiguity is resolved in favor of "the statute is narrow." The practical verdict does not change, because both the SOS (statewide) and county clerks, who follow the Procedures Manual, administer an election-only rule. A county-level purchase under 293.440(1) has a textual argument but high risk.

### Cited Findings
- NRS 293.440(1): "Any person who desires a copy of any list of the persons who are registered to vote in any precinct, district or county may obtain a copy by applying at the office of the county clerk and paying therefor a sum of money equal to 1 cent per name on the list…" (2): "Except as otherwise provided in NRS 293.5002 and 293.558, the copy of the list provided pursuant to this section must indicate the address, date of birth, telephone number and the serial number on each application to register to vote." — [NRS ch. 293 (Rev. 9/9/2026)](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec440) (read 2026-11-25)
- NRS 293.440(6): "Any state or county central committee of a major political party, any executive committee of a minor political party or any member or representative of such a central committee or executive committee who receives without charge a list of the persons who are registered to vote in any precinct, district or county pursuant to this section shall not: (a) Use the list for any purpose that is not related to an election; or (b) Sell the list for compensation or other valuable consideration." History ends with "2007, 2600": no amendment since 2007. — same URL
- NRS 293.558(2)-(4): clerks shall not disclose SSN, DL or ID card number, or the voter's email (the email "may not [be] release[d] … to a third party"). A voter may have address and phone withheld on written request. (4): "No information relating to a registered voter may be withheld from the public other than" those items and any other information that state or federal law makes confidential. — [NRS ch. 293](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-293.html#NRS293Sec558) (read 2026-11-25)
- 2026 Elections Procedures Manual (v3.2, updated 23-Mar-26), "Voters," p. 16: "Download, complete, and submit a Request for Access to the Statewide Voter Registration List… The applicant's qualification to access statewide voter data will be verified… No person, candidate, party, or organization who receives voter data can use the information for purposes unrelated to an election. The information cannot be sold for any reason. [fn 19: NRS 293.440(6)(b)]." The manual "was formally accepted by the Legislative Commission on December 17, 2025." — [NV 2026 Elections Procedures Manual (State Library epubs)](https://epubs.nsla.nv.gov/statepubs/epubs/779609-2026.pdf) (read 2026-11-25)
- NAC chapter 293 (Rev. 6/28/2026): I found no regulation restricting use of purchased voter lists. NAC 293.425 concerns bulk requests for registration application forms, not lists. — [NAC ch. 293](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NAC/NAC-293.html) (read 2026-11-25)
- The NevVoter page and its request form could not be fetched: nvsos.gov returns an Incapsula bot challenge. The prior researcher's snippet shows the same "prohibited from using such information for purposes not related to an election and from selling such information for any reason" language and a certification. — [NV SOS NevVoter](https://www.nvsos.gov/sos/elections/voters/nevvoter-statewide-list) (not readable 2026-11-25)

### Inferences
- The SOS's extension of 293.440(6) to all recipients goes beyond the statute's text. Still, a requester who certifies lawful, election-related use and then builds a commercial database would face misrepresentation and revocation risk, so the SOS route is NOT ALLOWED in practice.
- County route: a paid purchaser under 293.440(1) is not literally bound by (6). If a county clerk sells without an election-use certification, the use is arguably lawful. The list would be high value (full DOB and phone, unless withheld). Clerks are guided by the same Procedures Manual, though, so expect an election-only certification. Treat this as a question for counsel, not a go.

### Gaps
- I could not read the current SOS "Request for Access to the Statewide Voter Registration List" form or any county clerk's request form verbatim. I found no AG opinion or case.

## Texas — Tex. Elec. Code §§ 18.005, 18.008, 18.009, 18.066, 18.067; SOS form 24-1

### Takeaway
**Prior: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. Verified: ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS.** The SOS "shall furnish" statewide list information "to any person on request." The only use restriction, in both the statute and the sworn affidavit, is against use "in connection with advertising or promoting commercial products or services" (the affidavit says "to advertise or promote commercial products or services"). A violation is a Class A misdemeanor. The current SOS Voter Data Request Form 24-1 (rev. 10/2026) carries exactly that affidavit, notarized, and nothing broader. The record layout includes full DOB. **What changed:** the current affidavit text is now confirmed (not broader than the statute). The cross-reference "discrepancy" is explained: § 18.009 covers county registrar lists (§ 18.008), and § 18.067 covers the SOS statewide list (§ 18.066).

### Cited Findings
- § 18.066(a): "The secretary of state shall furnish information in the statewide computerized voter registration list to any person on request not later than the 15th day after the date the request is received." (b): information "may not include: (1) a voter's social security number; or (2) the residence address of a voter whose residence address is confidential under Section 13.004." (d): "To receive information under this section, a person must submit an affidavit to the secretary stating that the person will not use the information obtained in connection with advertising or promoting commercial products or services." (e): fees "may not exceed the actual expense incurred in reproducing the information requested." Last amended in 2019 (H.B. 2910). — [texas.public.law § 18.066](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._election_code_section_18.066) (mirrors statutes.capitol.texas.gov, accessed by the site May 26, 2025; read 2026-11-25)
- § 18.067: "(a) A person commits an offense if the person uses information in connection with advertising or promoting commercial products or services that the person knows was obtained under Section 18.066. (b) An offense under this section is a Class A misdemeanor." — [texas.public.law § 18.067](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._election_code_section_18.067) (read 2026-11-25)
- §§ 18.008-18.009 (county registrar lists): the registrar "shall furnish a copy of any list prepared under this subchapter to any person requesting it." § 18.009 is the parallel offense (Class A misdemeanor) for information "obtained under Section 18.008." § 18.005(a)(1): lists "contain the voter's name, date of birth, and registration number." — [texas.public.law § 18.008](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._election_code_section_18.008); [§ 18.009](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._election_code_section_18.009); [§ 18.005](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._election_code_section_18.005) (read 2026-11-25)
- SOS form 24-1 "Voter Data Request Form" (Prescribed by Secretary of State 10/2026): the requestor must give a name, a "Name of Business, Legislative Office, Agency, or Organization (required)," email and phone. Page 2 reprints § 18.067. The affidavit, sworn before a notary, reads: "I do solemnly swear that the information obtained from the Statewide Computerized Voter Registration List will not be used to advertise or promote commercial products or services." Fees are tiered by record count (from "$93.75 + $0.0005 per voter" up to "$328.13 + $0.0000625 per voter"; $11.00 per CD-ROM/DVD-R; 2.7% card fee). The record layout includes LAST/FIRST/MIDDLE/FORMER LAST NAME, **DOB** (8 characters), residence street fields and mailing address. — [SOS form 24-1f.pdf](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/forms/pol-sub/24-1f.pdf) (read 2026-11-25)
- SOS form 24-4 (subscription, 10/2026): "Election Cycle Subscription $12,000" for the November 2026–October 2027 cycle, with the same affidavit. — [SOS form 24-4f.pdf](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/elections/forms/pol-sub/24-4f.pdf) (read 2026-11-25)

### Inferences
- On the question asked: an internal database used only to locate people and link them to property and debt records does not "advertise" or "promote" a product or service. The plain meaning of those verbs is outward-facing marketing. So the use is outside § 18.067 if the data never feeds marketing. The risk sits downstream. If voter-derived contact data is used to solicit business, for example to market debt-buying, refinancing, property-purchase offers or "we can help with your debt" services, a prosecutor could call that "promoting commercial … services." Pure collection contact on an existing debt is not advertising, but it is a gray zone. Safest control: no voter-derived data in any outbound marketing, and a documented purpose limited to identity resolution.
- Full DOB makes Texas the highest-value file in this group for record linkage.
- The 10/2026 form revision date suggests 2025 session law did not change the operative text, since the form still quotes § 18.067 verbatim.

### Gaps
- statutes.capitol.texas.gov is now a JavaScript app and returned only a shell. Text was read from texas.public.law, whose snapshot is dated May 26, 2025. I did not independently confirm that no 89th Legislature (2025) bill amended §§ 18.066-18.067. The 10/2026 SOS form quoting the same text supports that none did.
- I found no AG opinion or case construing "advertising or promoting" for non-marketing internal use.

## Utah — Utah Code Title 20A, ch. 2, Part 6 (2026 SB 153); § 20A-2-104; 2026 SB 194

### Takeaway
**Prior: NOT ALLOWED (likely; text not verified). Verified: NOT ALLOWED.** 2026 SB 153 (Laws 2026, ch. 102) moved the list-access rules out of § 20A-2-104 into a new Part 6 (§§ 20A-2-601 to -608). Any person may request "standard voter data" (§ 20A-2-603), but § 20A-2-607(3)-(4) makes it unlawful to obtain or use list information for anything other than "a political purpose" or a purpose expressly permitted by Part 6, other state or federal law, or a court order. A violation is a class A misdemeanor (§ 20A-2-607(5)). The requester must also sign a form under penalty (§ 76-8-504 false statements). A locate/link database is not a political purpose, so the use is barred. DOB is no longer released: only an "age range." **What changed:** the verdict is firmed up from text. The old "qualified person" and birth-year regime in § 20A-2-104 is gone. The current § 20A-2-104 (eff. 5/6/2026) covers only the registration form. **Enrolled SB 194 does not amend § 20A-2-104 or Part 6.** Its "Utah Code Sections Affected" list omits them, so the 2027-01-01 date does not change list access.

### Cited Findings
- § 20A-2-607(3)-(5) (eff. 3/18/2026): "(3) A person may only obtain or use information from the list of registered voters for: (a) a political purpose; (b) another purpose for which the person is, under this part, expressly permitted to obtain or use the information; or (c) another purpose for which the person is permitted to obtain or use the information: (i) under a provision of state or federal law; or (ii) by order of a court with jurisdiction. (4) It is unlawful for a person to: (a) obtain information from the list of registered voters under false pretenses; (b) obtain or use information from the list of registered voters for a purpose other than [those purposes]; (c) knowingly post on the internet or otherwise disclose to the public, for a fee or free of charge, the list of registered voters or information obtained from the list…; or (d) knowingly disclose information from the list … in a manner that is not permitted by law. (5) A violation of Subsection (4) is a class A misdemeanor." — [le.utah.gov § 20A-2-607](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title20A/Chapter2/C20A-2-S607_2026031820260318.html) (read 2026-11-25)

## Washington — RCW 29A.08.710, .720, .740, .760; RCW 42.56.070(8); WAC 434-324-130/-140; SOS download terms

### Takeaway
**Prior: NOT ALLOWED (SOS terms + RCW 42.56.070(8)). Verified: NOT ALLOWED, with a corrected basis.** The statute (RCW 29A.08.720(3)(a) and .740) bars only using lists "for the purpose of mailing or delivering" ads, offers or solicitations, a class C felony with $5-per-item civil liability. But the Secretary of State's rule **WAC 434-324-130(2)** says flatly: "Such voter registration lists may not be used for commercial purposes." It covers lists furnished by "the auditor or secretary." RCW 29A.08.760 applies the 720/740 restrictions to the statewide file. **RCW 42.56.070(8) is a weaker basis than the prior note suggested.** It bars agencies from giving lists of individuals for commercial purposes "unless specifically authorized or directed by law," and RCW 29A.08.720(3)(a) "directs" the SOS and auditors to "promptly furnish current lists of registered voters … to any person requesting." **What changed:** the controlling text for the bar is now the WAC, not the PRA. The statutory felony does not reach a no-mail internal database. The SOS click-through still requires agreeing to use the data "in accordance with" RCW 29A.08.720, .740 and 42.56.070(8), which I could not read verbatim.

### Cited Findings
- RCW 29A.08.720(3)(a): "precinct lists and current lists of registered voters are public records … The county auditor or secretary of state shall promptly furnish current lists of registered voters in his or her possession, at actual reproduction cost, to any person requesting such information. The lists shall not be used for the purpose of mailing or delivering any advertisement or offer for any property, establishment, organization, product, or service or for the purpose of mailing or delivering any solicitation for money, services, or anything of value. However, the lists and labels may be used for any political purpose. The county auditor or secretary of state must provide a copy of RCW 29A.08.740 to the person requesting the material…" (4) defines "political purpose." [2023 c 361 s 8]. — [RCW 29A.08.720](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCW/RCW%20%2029A%20TITLE/RCW%20%2029A.%2008%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2029A.%2008%20.720.htm) (read 2026-11-25)
- RCW 29A.08.740(1): using data furnished under .720 "for the purpose of mailing or delivering any advertisement or offer … or … any solicitation for money, services, or anything of value is guilty of a class C felony punishable by imprisonment … not more than five years or a fine of not more than ten thousand dollars or both," plus "five dollars for each item mailed or delivered," with class actions and attorney fees available. (2) Each recipient "shall take reasonable precautions designed to assure that the data is not used for" those purposes, or is jointly and severally liable. — [RCW 29A.08.740](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCW/RCW%20%2029A%20TITLE/RCW%20%2029A.%2008%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2029A.%2008%20.740.htm) (read 2026-11-25)

The person making the request must be provided a copy of RCW 29A.08.740." (WSR 09-18-098, eff. 10/2/09). WAC 434-324-140: the auditor or secretary "may require each person who requests a list … to sign a request which includes penalty requirements." — [WAC 434-324-130](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wac/WAC%20434%20%20TITLE/WAC%20434%20-324%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20434%20-324%20-130.htm); [WAC 434-324-140](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wac/WAC%20434%20%20TITLE/WAC%20434%20-324%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20434%20-324%20-140.htm) (read 2026-11-25)

### Inferences
- Under the statute alone, an internal database that never mails or delivers anything is not the RCW 29A.08.740 felony. The SOS's own rule (WAC 434-324-130(2)) independently forbids "commercial purposes," and an internal for-profit locate/link database is plainly commercial. A purchaser who obtains the extract and uses it commercially breaches the rule and the click-through. The WAC itself has no stated penalty, so exposure is contractual and administrative (and possibly under RCW 42.56.070(8) theories), not the felony, unless mailing occurs.

## Indiana — county release under IC 3-7-27-6 and State Form VRG-24; IC 3-7-26.4-6 (statewide)

### Takeaway
**Prior: NOT ALLOWED statewide; county ambiguous, at best ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. Verified: statewide NOT ALLOWED (unchanged); county ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS, if the county election board's uniform policy permits copies.** Under IC 3-7-27-6(c), "notwithstanding any other statute," each county election board adopts a nondiscriminatory uniform policy that either permits or refuses duplicate copies of county SVRS voter data, at a uniform fee. A requester must sign State Form VRG-24 (IC 3-7-27-6(d)), agreeing (1) not to use the data "to solicit merchandise, goods, services, or subscriptions for a purpose other than political activities or political fundraising activities" and (2) not to "sell, loan, give away or otherwise deliver" it to any other person for a non-political purpose. VRG-24 has an "Other (please specify)" requester category, so non-political requesters are contemplated. The penalty for soliciting (IC 3-14-6-2) is a Class B infraction, or a Class A misdemeanor for a repeat knowing violation. **What changed:** the "exclusively for voter registration and election administration" worry is largely answered by the "notwithstanding any other statute" clause in 3-7-27-6(c). Also, the PAC has opined that ch. 26.4 restrictions (no DOB, voting history and so on) bind only the Election Division, not counties. County extracts can carry DOB and voting history unless the county board withholds them. The 2025 commercial-use ban in introduced HB 1680 does not appear among the enacted HEA 1680 (P.L. 241-2025) sections.

### Inferences
- The VRG-24 promises are act-based (no soliciting, no transfer). An internal locate/link database breaches neither, provided (a) voter-derived data is never used to solicit, including offering services to debtors, and (b) no copy or derived list goes to any third party, affiliates and clients included.
- The "exclusively for voter registration and election administration" clause (IC 3-7-26.3) governs the SVRS as a system. The county-copy provision expressly operates "notwithstanding any other statute," so it is not a bar to county-released copies. Moderate residual risk remains.
- Practical gating: each county board decides whether to release at all, and some (for example St. Joseph) refuse wholesale lists. Expect county-by-county outcomes, fees and an "Approved/Denied" decision on the requester category "Other."
- County extracts may include DOB, which is high value for linkage, unless the county withholds it.
