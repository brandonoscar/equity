# Voter file access and permitted uses: Northeast (CT, DE, DC, ME, MD, MA, NH, NJ, NY, PA, RI, VT)

Use case tested: a private company obtains the statewide voter file and uses it only internally, to locate people and link them to property and debt records. No redistribution, no solicitation, no mailing.

Research date: 2026-11-25. **How this was researched:** this session's network blocked ncsl.org and eac.gov ("ENOTFOUND", meaning those hosts are not on the session's allowed list), so I could not open any page in full. Every finding below comes from web-search result summaries and snippets of the statute, agency form or article named. None of it comes from reading the full current statute text. Points that rely on my own background knowledge are marked "(unverified)" and listed under Inferences or Gaps, not under Cited Findings. Confirm each state against the official statute text before acting. This is legal research for internal planning, not legal advice.

**Bottom line.** Eleven of the 12 jurisdictions limit the statewide file to election-related or political use, ban commercial use, or limit who can get it, in a way that covers an internal locate-and-link database. Two jurisdictions found no statutory use limit: **District of Columbia** (probably ALLOWED) and **New Jersey** (ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS). **Connecticut** used to be the main permissive state. A 2025 omnibus bill (SB 1516, § 23) bans "personal, private or commercial" use, but I did not confirm when or whether it was enacted.

## Who may obtain the voter file, and how

### Takeaway
Access methods vary widely. Some places sell to anyone: DC (free by email or secure link), NJ, RI (about $25), PA (with a signed statement) and CT (historically $300). Some sell only to in-state registered voters: MD, and probably DE. Some give the statewide file only to parties, candidates and committees: MA, and NH and ME for full data. Most require a signed statement or affidavit about how the file will be used.

### Cited Findings
- **CT:** Requests go in writing to the Secretary of the State's office, by mail or email, using a data-file request form and a credit-card processing form. — [CT SOTS, Centralized Voter Registration Information page](https://portal.ct.gov/SOTS/Election-Services/Election-Services/Centralized-Voter-Registration-Information---Voter-Privacy-Program)
- **CT:** Historically, marketers and other private entities could buy the statewide list for about $300. — [The Day](https://www.theday.com/?p=154002); [CT Post, "Bill would end state sale of voters' data"](https://www.ctpost.com/politics/article/Bill-would-end-state-sale-of-voters-data-13545698.php)
- **CT:** A 2018 report by the Connecticut legislature's research office (OLR) described CT as the only state that allowed sale of voter data for commercial purposes. — [CGA OLR 2018-R-0119](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2018/rpt/pdf/2018-R-0119.pdf); [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/policy/connecticut-secretary-of-state-moves-to-shield-voter-data.html)
- **DE:** The State Election Commissioner must give qualifying candidates lists free of charge. The Delaware Freedom of Information Act "shall not apply to information contained in voter lists and files" (15 Del. C. § 304). The statewide order form lists a no-cost option under § 304(e). — [Delaware Code Title 15 ch. 3](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title15/c003/index.html); [DE statewide voter registration order form](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf)
- **DE:** 15 Del. C. § 1303 covers addresses made confidential by court order. It is not the list-access section. — [Justia 15 Del. C. § 1303](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-15/chapter-13/section-1303/)
- **DC:** The DC Board of Elections data request form (2023) offers voter registration data by "E-mail or Secure Link ... Microsoft Excel (No Charge)". The full list is available only by secure link, with separate options with and without voter history. — [DCBOE Data Request Form 2023](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf)
- **DC:** An NCSL summary, as quoted in search results, lists DC as "Available to the public." — [NCSL (via search snippet)](https://www.ncsl.org/research/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists.aspx)
- **ME:** 21-A M.R.S. § 196-A controls who can buy data from the central voter registration system. Political parties, candidates and get-out-the-vote groups can buy a file with more fields. Other purchasers are limited to a narrower purpose (see the next section). — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)
- **MD:** "A copy of a list of registered voters shall be provided to a Maryland registered voter on receipt of a written application and a statement, signed under oath," about how the list will be used (Md. Code, Elec. Law § 3-506). The State Board's application cites § 3-506 and COMAR 33.03.02.03A and 33.03.02.04. — [Justia Md. Elec. Law § 3-506](https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/2013/article-gel/section-3-506); [MD SBE application](https://elections.maryland.gov/pdf/SBEAPPL.pdf)
- **MD:** County fees seen include St. Mary's County at $25 for one list type. The statewide fee was not found. — [St. Mary's County application 2025](https://www.stmaryscountymd.gov/docs/stmarysApplicationforVoterRegistrationData2025.pdf)
- **MA:** Under G.L. c. 51 § 47C, "the names and addresses contained in said central registry shall not be a matter of public record." They go to state party committees, statewide candidate committees, ballot question committees, the jury commissioner, the adjutant general and other bodies the Secretary names by regulation, at a fair cost. — [mass.gov, c. 51 § 47C (unofficial text)](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c51-ss-47c); [malegislature.gov § 47C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVIII/Chapter51/Section47C)
- **MA:** Bills to widen access did not appear to pass. These include 2011 S.297 (PACs at $1,000) and 2021 S.449 (party, statewide candidate and ballot committees). — [S.297 (2011)](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/187/S297/Senate/Bill/Text); [S.449 (2021)](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/192/S449/Senate/Bill/Text)
- **NH:** Under RSA 654:31, as shown on an unofficial mirror, local clerks give checklist information only for their own town or city, at $25 per town or ward copy. — [RSA 654:31 (ebench mirror)](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/new-hampshire/statute/654:31)
- **NJ:** N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1 provides registry lists "drawn from the Statewide voter registration system". A Government Records Council decision treats voter information as publicly accessible under 19:31-18.1(a). — [Justia N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1); [NJ GRC 2014-348](https://www.nj.gov/grc/decisions/pdf/2014-348.pdf)
- **NJ:** The only fee language found ($0.25 per copy per election district) is from the 2013 version of the statute. It may be outdated. — [Justia 2013 N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/2013/title-19/section-19-31-18.1/)
- **NY:** Individual records are available to the public on request under § 3-103(5), and "no cost shall be charged" (N.Y. Elec. Law § 3-112). — [NY Elec. Law § 3-112](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/3-112)
- **NY:** A Committee on Open Government (COOG) opinion says county voter lists must be disclosed under Election Law §§ 3-212 and 5-602, not under the Freedom of Information Law (FOIL). — [COOG FOIL AO 19677](https://docsopengovernment.dos.ny.gov/coog/ftext/f19677.htm); [COOG f15812](https://docsopengovernment.dos.ny.gov/coog/ftext/f15812.htm)
- **PA:** Anyone may obtain the list after showing ID to the official holding it and stating in writing that the information will be used only for permitted purposes (25 Pa.C.S. § 1404). A commission sets the copy fee under § 1404(c), and the fee must come with the request. The Department of State runs a "PA FULL Voter Export" purchase page. — [FindLaw 25 Pa.C.S. § 1404](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-25-pacsa-elections/pa-csa-sect-25-1404/); [PA DOS request form](https://Www.Dos.Pa.Gov/VotingElections/Documents/Elections%20Division/requestvoterlists.pdf); [PA Voter Services FULL export](https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/PurchasePAFULLVoterExport.aspx)
- **RI:** Registration cards and the registration lists prepared under § 17-9.1-21 are public records (R.I. Gen. Laws § 17-9.1-15). A local list copy goes "to any elector within the city or town upon payment of no more than the actual cost of reproduction" (§ 17-10-14). — [R.I. Gen. Laws § 17-9.1-15](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE17/17-9.1/17-9.1-15.htm); [Justia § 17-10-14 (2013)](https://law.justia.com/codes/rhode-island/2013/title-17/chapter-17-10/section-17-10-14/)
- **RI:** Under § 17-6-5 (2009 version), the Secretary of State furnishes prepaid lists, on written application, at a price set each year, to qualified candidates "and to no one else, except as provided" in the chapter. — [Justia § 17-6-5 (2009)](https://law.justia.com/codes/rhode-island/2009/title-17/chapter-17-6/17-6-5)
- **RI:** Ballotpedia, as of June 2025, lists RI's voter file as "Open" at $25. — [Ballotpedia, Availability of state voter files](https://ballotpedia.org/Availability_of_state_voter_files)
- **RI:** After taking office following the 2022 election, Secretary of State Gregg Amore made the complete voter database available on request again. — [Providence Journal via AOL](https://aol.com/full-transparency-restored-ri-secretary-085927992.html)
- **VT:** The Secretary of State's electronic-copy request form (Rev. 01/06/2023) cites 17 V.S.A. § 2154. It requires a sworn affirmation, under penalty of perjury (13 V.S.A. ch. 65), that the requester will not use the checklist for commercial purposes. — [VT SOS request form](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/sos/Elections_Division/voters/faq/request_electronic_copy_vermont_statewide_checklist.pdf); [17 V.S.A. § 2154](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/043/02154)

## Statutory use restrictions and penalties

### Takeaway
Most of these states use one of three formulas: "election/political purposes only" (MD, NY, PA, RI, DE), "no commercial purpose" (VT, ME, NH, and now CT), or "not a public record" with access limited to committees (MA). I found no use restriction for DC or NJ. Penalties range from misdemeanors (MD) and perjury or unsworn falsification for false affidavits (MD, PA, VT) to civil violations heard in Vermont's Judicial Bureau. Recent changes: CT (2025 SB 1516 § 23, status to confirm) and RI (full DOB released again, 2023).

### Cited Findings
- **CT (recent change):** The 2025 omnibus election bill SB 1516, § 23 ("Prohibited Voter Information Disclosures"), "prohibits the use of voter registration information the secretary of the state's office and local registrars of voters collect and maintain for personal, private or commercial purposes," including "advertising, marketing, selling or soliciting products or services". It allows use for "election-related, scholarly, journalistic, political or governmental purposes" and lets the Secretary adopt regulations. — [Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/connecticut-moves-to-strengthen-voter-privacy-in-sweeping-election-bill); [CGA bill analysis, SB 1516](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2025/ba/pdf/2025SB-01516-R000673-BA.pdf); [SB 1516 file copy](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2025/TOB/S/PDF/2025SB-01516-R02-SB.PDF)
- **CT:** A 2026 OLR report has a section titled "Disclosure of Voter Registration Information". I did not confirm the Public Act number or effective date. — [CGA OLR 2026-R-0082](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2026/rpt/pdf/2026-R-0082.pdf); [OLR 2025 Acts Affecting Town Clerks and Elections](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/olr/Documents/year/AA/2025AA-0094_2025%20Acts%20Affecting%20Town%20Clerks%20and%20Elections.pdf)
- **DE:** The statewide order form requires a statement that the data "will not be used for commercial solicitation or for any other purpose not related to the electoral process." — [DE statewide order form](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf)
- **DE:** The earlier version of § 1303 penalized obtaining or disclosing a court-ordered confidential address "for an impermissible use". — [Justia 15 Del. C. § 1303 (2020)](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/2020/title-15/chapter-13/section-1303/)
- **ME:** "Information obtained from the central voter registration system pursuant to this section may not be used for any commercial purpose, including, but not limited to, the sales and marketing of products and services" (earlier wording). The current § 196-A bars non-party purchasers from using or transferring data "for any purpose that is not directly related to evaluating the State's compliance with its voter list maintenance obligations" and from making it publicly available online. — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html); [Justia § 196-A (2012)](https://law.justia.com/codes/maine/2012/title21a/chapter3/section196a/)
- **ME:** P.L. 2021, c. 310, "An Act To Protect Data Privacy and Security in Elections," amended § 196-A, including a new "Discrimination prohibited" subsection. — [P.L. 2021 c. 310](https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/Laws/2021/2021_PL_c310.pdf)
- **MD:** The required oath says the list "is not intended to be used for commercial solicitation or any other purpose not related to the electoral process" (§ 3-506). Anyone who "knowingly allows a list of registered voters under their control to be used" for commercial solicitation or another non-electoral purpose is guilty of a misdemeanor. — [Justia § 3-506](https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/2013/article-gel/section-3-506); [FindLaw § 3-506](https://codes.findlaw.com/md/election-law/md-code-elec-law-sect-3-506/)
- **MD:** The 2018 Voter Registration List Protection Act (Ch. 552, HB 1717) added bans on distribution to third parties and on unauthorized publishing or republishing. — [2018 Md. Laws Ch. 552](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2018RS/chapters_noln/Ch_552_hb1717T.pdf)
- **MA:** The central registry is not a public record and goes only to the bodies listed in § 47C. I found no penalty provision. — [mass.gov § 47C](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c51-ss-47c)
- **NH:** "'Commercial purposes' means knowingly using, selling, giving, or receiving the checklist information for the purpose of selling or offering for sale any property or service unrelated to an election or political campaign" (RSA 654:31, unofficial mirror). — [RSA 654:31 (ebench mirror)](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/new-hampshire/statute/654:31)
- **NH:** 2006 HB 1238 proposed: "No person shall use or permit the use of checklist information provided by the secretary of state for commercial purposes," with unauthorized database disclosure a misdemeanor. I did not confirm that it was enacted. — [HB 1238 (2006)](https://bills.nhliberty.org/bills/2006/HB1238)
- **NJ:** The list "shall not include voter signatures" unless a court orders otherwise (19:31-18.1). P.L. 2021, c. 371 (Daniel's Law follow-on) amended 19:31-18.1. A912 (2024–2025) would bar publishing registry information online and was only introduced. — [Justia 19:31-18.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1); [P.L. 2021 c. 371](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF); [A912 (LegiScan)](https://www.legiscan.com/NJ/text/A912/2024)
- **NY:** The statewide voter registration list may not be used for non-election purposes. The State Board says "Election Law Sec 3-103(5) prohibits using information derived from voter registration records for non-election purposes." — [NY Elec. Law § 3-103](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/3-103); [NYSBOE page](https://elections.ny.gov/node/86)
- **NY:** A COOG opinion reads the restriction as applying only to the "statewide voter registration list", not to county election-district lists. It cites the provision as § 3-103(3), while the State Board cites § 3-103(5). — [COOG f15812](https://docsopengovernment.dos.ny.gov/coog/ftext/f15812.htm)
- **PA:** No one who inspects the list or acquires names from it "may use information contained in the list for purposes unrelated to elections, political activities or law enforcement" (25 Pa.C.S. § 1404(b)(3), as cited on county forms). Under § 1207(b), the information "may not be used for commercial or improper purposes". The DOS form also bars posting lists on the Internet (4 Pa. Code §§ 183.13(g), 183.14(k)). Affirmations are signed subject to 18 Pa.C.S. § 4904 (unsworn falsification). — [Berks County request form](https://berkspa.gov/getmedia/380d8d95-b73f-4cca-aef7-ab9f26a0dca8/Request-for-Public-Information.pdf); [Wyoming County PA form](https://wyomingcountypa.gov/wp-content/uploads/Request-for-public-information.pdf); [4 Pa. Code § 183.14](https://regulations.justia.com/states/pennsylvania/title-4/part-viii/subpart-e/chapter-183/section-183-14)
- **RI:** NCSL describes RI's purchase list as usable "for political purposes or in the furtherance of candidacy for political office, and no other purpose." That conflicts with a 2018 CT OLR report saying RI does "not appear to place any restrictions on access to or use of voter registration information." — [NCSL summary (via search)](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists); [CGA OLR 2018-R-0119](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2018/rpt/pdf/2018-R-0119.pdf)
- **VT:** The 2018 amendment (H.624) bars use of the checklist "for commercial purposes" and knowing disclosure to a foreign government or federal agency. A 2018 draft gave the Judicial Bureau (Vermont's civil-violation tribunal) jurisdiction over violations of § 2154(c)(2) and (d). — [H.624 as passed (2018)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2018/Docs/BILLS/H-0624/H-0624%20As%20Passed%20by%20Both%20House%20and%20Senate%20Official.pdf); [17 V.S.A. § 2154](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/043/02154)
- **Context (2026):** The U.S. Department of Justice sued Connecticut for the full statewide list, including full dates of birth. CT declined, saying state and federal law bar that disclosure. DOJ also sued RI. RI offered its public list and withheld confidential fields, and that case is on appeal. — [CT SOTS Q&A (2026)](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/press-releases/2026-press-releases/questions-and-answers-about-the-lawsuit-filed-by-the-department-of-justice-against-ct); [Law Monarch (RI)](https://www.lawmonarch.com/rhode-island-voter-data-lawsuit/)

### Inferences
- Penalties from background knowledge, not confirmed this session: NY misuse is a crime under Election Law article 17 (unverified); NH commercial misuse carries a civil penalty or misdemeanor (unverified); ME misuse carries a civil penalty under § 196-A (unverified).
- For PA, I recall litigation by Voter Reference Foundation (an organization that posts voter rolls online) over PA's ban on Internet posting (unverified; not found this session). It concerns publishing online, not internal use.

## Does the restriction cover an internal locate-and-link database?

### Takeaway
Yes, for every state with an "election/political purposes only" formula (MD, DE, NY, PA, RI as NCSL describes it). Locating people and linking them to property and debt records is plainly unrelated to elections, whether or not anything is mailed or redistributed. For "no commercial purpose" states (VT, ME, CT under SB 1516), the use fits the ordinary meaning of a commercial purpose. ME's current text and CT's "personal, private or commercial" wording leave little room to argue otherwise. NH's definition is the only one tied to selling: it covers data used to sell "any property or service unrelated to an election." Using the file to run a commercial locate or debt-linking service very likely falls under it. Only DC and NJ appear to have no statutory use limit.

### Cited Findings
- MD's oath excludes "any other purpose not related to the electoral process", not just solicitation. — [Justia § 3-506](https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/2013/article-gel/section-3-506)
- PA's test is purpose-based ("purposes unrelated to elections, political activities or law enforcement"). A commercial firm is not law enforcement. — [Berks County form citing § 1404](https://berkspa.gov/getmedia/380d8d95-b73f-4cca-aef7-ab9f26a0dca8/Request-for-Public-Information.pdf)
- NY bars "non-election purposes". — [NYSBOE](https://elections.ny.gov/node/86)
- ME's current purchaser-purpose limit is "evaluating the State's compliance with its voter list maintenance obligations". — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)
- CT's 2025 bill covers "personal, private or commercial purposes". — [Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/connecticut-moves-to-strengthen-voter-privacy-in-sweeping-election-bill)
- NH defines "commercial purposes" as using checklist information to sell "any property or service unrelated to an election or political campaign". — [RSA 654:31 mirror](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/new-hampshire/statute/654:31)

### Inferences
- **NY ambiguity:** COOG reads § 3-103's "non-election purposes" restriction as reaching only the statewide list. County lists are obtainable under §§ 3-212 and 5-602, which might allow assembling the file county by county without that restriction. This is a contested and aggressive reading, and county boards may still demand use statements. Any reliance on it needs NY counsel. ([COOG f15812](https://docsopengovernment.dos.ny.gov/coog/ftext/f15812.htm))
- **RI ambiguity:** RI treats registration lists as public records (§ 17-9.1-15) and gives local lists to electors (§ 17-10-14). Its purchase statute (§ 17-6-5) and NCSL's summary limit purchased lists to political purposes. The safe reading is that the purchased statewide file is limited to political use.
- **NJ:** The no-redistribution design matters because of Daniel's Law. Covered persons (judges, prosecutors, law enforcement and their families) can demand non-disclosure of their home address. Data brokers face private suits for disclosing it. An internal-only database lowers but does not remove risk if records are ever shown to clients. (Daniel's Law details are from background knowledge, unverified; only the P.L. 2021 c. 371 amendment to 19:31-18.1 is cited above.)
- An internal-only use with no solicitation does not escape "election-purpose only" formulas. Those formulas restrict any non-election use, not just distribution or solicitation.

### Gaps
- I found no guidance, AG opinion or case in any of these jurisdictions on whether "commercial purpose" covers purely internal analytics without solicitation.
- I did not verify how Daniel's Law (N.J.S.A. 56:8-166.1 et seq.) applies to voter-list recipients who use the data internally.

## Fields included and withheld

### Takeaway
Typical public fields are name, residential address (and mailing address), party, and voting history. Treatment of date of birth differs: CT has moved, or is moving, to birth year only; RI restored full DOB; ME bars making DOB public; NY and PA release DOB (unverified). Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, signatures and court-ordered confidential addresses are withheld everywhere I checked.

### Cited Findings
- **CT:** Historically, names, addresses, dates of birth and phone numbers were public. The 2025 bill limits DOB disclosure to birth year. Birth month and day are withheld from the public and from the federal government. Protected data includes names, addresses, birth dates and certain ID numbers. — [CGA OLR 2018-R-0119](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2018/rpt/pdf/2018-R-0119.pdf); [Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/connecticut-moves-to-strengthen-voter-privacy-in-sweeping-election-bill)
- **CT (2026):** The Secretary of State's office says state and federal law bar disclosing full DOB. — [CT SOTS Q&A](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/press-releases/2026-press-releases/questions-and-answers-about-the-lawsuit-filed-by-the-department-of-justice-against-ct)
- **DC:** The full list is offered with or without voter history. — [DCBOE Data Request Form](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf)
- **ME:** The State may not make dates of birth or mailing addresses publicly available. Parties, candidates and get-out-the-vote groups get an expanded field set. — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)
- **MA:** The registry holds names, addresses and other fields, some "for street list purposes only." — [malegislature.gov § 47C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVIII/Chapter51/Section47C)
- **NJ:** No voter signatures without a court order. — [Justia 19:31-18.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1)
- **NY:** Driver's license numbers and any portion of the SSN are not released. — [NY Elec. Law § 3-103](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/3-103)
- **RI:** Ballotpedia lists name, address, precinct, ward, district and party. Amore reversed the birth-year-only policy. RI withheld confidential fields from DOJ. — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Availability_of_state_voter_files); [ProJo via AOL](https://aol.com/full-transparency-restored-ri-secretary-085927992.html); [Law Monarch](https://www.lawmonarch.com/rhode-island-voter-data-lawsuit/)
- **DE:** Court-ordered confidential addresses are removed from voter records (§ 1303). — [Justia § 1303](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-15/chapter-13/section-1303/)

### Inferences
- From background knowledge (unverified): PA exports include DOB, phone if provided, party and voting history. NY's statewide file includes DOB and voting history. NJ files include DOB. ## Verdict by jurisdiction (internal locate-and-link use, no redistribution, no solicitation)

### Takeaway
DC is probably ALLOWED. NJ is ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS. CT is probably NOT ALLOWED (confirm enactment of SB 1516 § 23). The other nine (DE, ME, MD, MA, NH, NY, PA, RI, VT) are NOT ALLOWED.

### Cited Findings
- **Connecticut — NOT ALLOWED (probable).** The 2025 SB 1516 § 23 bans "personal, private or commercial" use and allows only election-related, scholarly, journalistic, political or governmental use. CT was historically open to commercial buyers, so confirm the enacted text and effective date. — [Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/connecticut-moves-to-strengthen-voter-privacy-in-sweeping-election-bill); [CGA bill analysis](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2025/ba/pdf/2025SB-01516-R000673-BA.pdf)
- **Delaware — NOT ALLOWED.** The required statement says the data won't be used "for any other purpose not related to the electoral process" (15 Del. C. § 304). — [DE order form](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf)
- **District of Columbia — ALLOWED (tentative).** The list is available to the public at no charge, and I found no use restriction. The statute was not verified. — [DCBOE form](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf)
- **Maine — NOT ALLOWED.** Commercial use is banned, and non-party purchasers are limited to evaluating list maintenance (21-A M.R.S. § 196-A). — [§ 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)
- **Maryland — NOT ALLOWED.** The list goes to MD registered voters only, under an oath of no non-electoral use; violation is a misdemeanor; third-party distribution is banned (Elec. Law § 3-506; 2018 Ch. 552). — [§ 3-506](https://law.justia.com/codes/maryland/2013/article-gel/section-3-506)
- **Massachusetts — NOT ALLOWED.** The central registry is "not a matter of public record" and goes only to listed committees and agencies (G.L. c. 51 § 47C). — [mass.gov § 47C](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c51-ss-47c)
- **New Hampshire — NOT ALLOWED.** RSA 654:31 restricts "commercial purposes", defined as use to sell property or services unrelated to elections. The statewide list is probably limited to parties and candidates (unverified). — [RSA 654:31 mirror](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/new-hampshire/statute/654:31)
- **New Jersey — ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS.** The list is public under N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1, and I found no use restriction. Conditions: handle Daniel's Law covered persons (suppress protected home addresses on request, and never disclose them); check for any enactment of A912 or similar limits on online publication; no signatures. — [19:31-18.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1); [P.L. 2021 c. 371](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF)
- **New York — NOT ALLOWED.** The statewide list may not be used for "non-election purposes" (Elec. Law § 3-103(5)). Whether county lists escape this is unclear (see COOG). — [§ 3-103](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/3-103)
- **Pennsylvania — NOT ALLOWED.** Use is limited to elections, political activities or law enforcement (25 Pa.C.S. § 1404(b)(3)); "not ... commercial or improper purposes" (§ 1207(b)); no Internet posting; a false statement is unsworn falsification (18 Pa.C.S. § 4904). — [§ 1404](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-25-pacsa-elections/pa-csa-sect-25-1404/)
- **Rhode Island — NOT ALLOWED (lean; sources conflict).** NCSL and § 17-6-5 limit purchased lists to political use and candidacy. A 2018 CT OLR report said RI had no restrictions. Lists are public records for inspection. — [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists); [Justia § 17-6-5](https://law.justia.com/codes/rhode-island/2009/title-17/chapter-17-6/17-6-5)
- **Vermont — NOT ALLOWED.** A sworn affidavit of no commercial use is required (17 V.S.A. § 2154, as amended 2018). — [VT SOS form](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/sos/Elections_Division/voters/faq/request_electronic_copy_vermont_statewide_checklist.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a multi-state build, NJ and DC are the only realistic sources in this region. The 2025–2026 trend (CT's restriction, DOJ litigation over CT and RI files, NJ's A912) points toward tighter access, so recheck before each acquisition.
- Signing the required use statements in DE, MD, PA or VT while intending a locate-and-link use would itself create criminal exposure (perjury or unsworn falsification), on top of the statutory use violation.

### Gaps
- None of the verdicts rest on a full reading of the current official statute text, because of the blocked hosts and summary-only search results. The highest priorities to confirm are CT (enactment of SB 1516 § 23), DC (statute text) and NJ (current 19:31-18.1 text, fee, and DOB inclusion).
