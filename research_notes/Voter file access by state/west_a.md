# Voter file access and permitted uses: Alaska, Arizona, California, Colorado, Hawaii, Idaho, Montana, Nevada

Use case tested in every state: a private company obtains the statewide voter file and uses it for a non-political, internal commercial purpose. The purpose is an internal records database that locates people and links them to property and debt records. There is no redistribution and no solicitation or mailing.

Research date: 2026-11-25. This is legal research for internal planning, not legal advice.

**Method caveat (read first).** In this session, www.ncsl.org and www.akleg.gov did not resolve because they are outside the network allowlist. Following the sandbox instruction, I stopped fetching pages directly. Every finding below therefore comes from web-search result summaries, which quote or paraphrase official statute pages (azleg.gov, mca.legmt.gov, data.capitol.hawaii.gov, legislature.idaho.gov, sos.ca.gov, coloradosos.gov, nvsos.gov) and compilations (EAC, Ballotpedia, Justia, FindLaw). I did not read the full current statute text for any state in a browser. Exact quotations are limited to text that appeared in search snippets. Anything I recalled from background knowledge is labeled as such and placed under Inferences or Gaps.

## Cross-state summary

### Takeaway
Of the 8 states, only **Alaska** has no statutory use restriction that I could find, so this use case is probably **ALLOWED** there. **Colorado** is **probably ALLOWED**, but secondary sources conflict. **Idaho** is **ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS**: its statute bans advertising and solicitation mailings, not internal use. **Arizona, California, Hawaii, Montana and Nevada** are **NOT ALLOWED**. Their statutes limit use to election, political or government purposes (or, in Montana, to noncommercial use), and Arizona makes misuse a class 6 felony.

| State | Who may obtain | Cost (statewide) | Operative restriction | Verdict for internal locate/link database |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AK | Any person (AS 15.07.127) | ~$20 (EAC estimate) | None found in statute; DOB/age, SSN, DL number, place of birth, signature confidential (AS 15.07.195) | ALLOWED (verify the current request form) |
| AZ | Limited to authorized uses (A.R.S. § 16-168) | Not confirmed | Not for "a commercial purpose as defined in section 39-121.03"; class 6 felony | NOT ALLOWED |
| CA | Only for "election, scholarly, journalistic, or political purposes, or for governmental purposes" (Elec. Code § 2194); application under § 2188 | Varies (county/SOS) | 2 CCR § 19004 bars "personal, private, or commercial purpose"; misdemeanor (Elec. Code § 18109) | NOT ALLOWED |
| CO | Any person (per SOS and compilations) | $50 per list; $1,000 cycle subscription | Conflicting: some surveys list "non-commercial"; 2018 CT report and others say no use restriction | PROBABLY ALLOWED; confirm the data request form terms |
| HI | Non-public data only "for election or government purposes" (HRS § 11-97, § 11-14) | n/a | Unlawful to use or distribute except as § 11-97 authorizes; misdemeanor (unconfirmed) | NOT ALLOWED |

### Cited Findings
- The EAC says "even the least restrictive states prohibit the commercial use of voter file information." — [EAC, Availability of State Voter File and Confidential Information](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf)
- Ballotpedia says "the majority of states have rules in place to prohibit the commercial use of voter file information." In July 2026 it reported that 21 states and D.C. have "open" availability. — [Ballotpedia, Availability of state voter files](https://ballotpedia.org/Availability_of_state_voter_files); [Ballotpedia News, 2026-07-28](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/07/28/twenty-one-states-and-d-c-make-voter-files-openly-available/)
- In 2025 the federal DOJ requested full statewide voter lists from states. Alaska shared its list under AS 15.07.195(c)(1) (a federal agency may receive confidential data if it uses the data only for government purposes). Nevada's Secretary of State questioned the request. This is context only and does not affect private access. — [Alaska Lt. Governor](https://ltgov.alaska.gov/?p=2103); [KUNR, 2025-09-26](https://www.kunr.org/nevada-state-government/2025-09-26/doj-demands-nevada-voters-social-security-and-drivers-license-numbers)

### Inferences
- Statutes framed as "election/political/government purposes only" (CA, HI, NV per its SOS) exclude any non-political internal business use, whether or not the data is redistributed.
- "Noncommercial" or "commercial purpose" statutes (AZ, MT) most likely reach an internal database built for a for-profit locate and collections business, even without resale. In Arizona's case, the definition of "commercial purpose" (see the AZ section) appears to cover anticipated indirect monetary gain.
- Idaho's restriction targets a specific act (mailing or delivering ads, offers or solicitations), so an internal database with no mailing falls outside its plain text. Using the data to contact debtors by mail would very likely fall inside it.

### Gaps
- I could not read the NCSL table (blocked host) to cross-check it.
- I did not view the full current text of any statute. Before relying on any verdict, confirm each one against the official legislature site.

## Alaska

### Takeaway
**ALLOWED (verify the request form).** "Any person" may buy the list. I found no statutory restriction on commercial or other use, and the confidential fields are withheld by law. I found only one proposed commercial-use ban, an old bill (HB 376, 20th Legislature, 1997-98), with no evidence that it was enacted.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and how:** AS 15.07.127: "any person may obtain a copy of the list, or a part of the list, or an electronic format containing both residence and mailing addresses of voters, by applying to the director and paying to the state treasury a fee as determined by the director." Access is subject to AS 15.07.195. — [Justia, AS 15.07.127](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-15/chapter-07/section-15-07-127/); [Justia, AS 15.07.195 (2022)](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/2022/title-15/chapter-07/section-15-07-195). A parallel provision for post-election lists of people who voted is in AS 15.15.400. — [Justia, AS 15.15.400](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-15/chapter-15/section-15-15-400/)
- **Division of Elections guidance:** "Alaska law allows for any person to request a statewide or house district voting list. These lists contain public information such as name, residence address (unless requested to be private)…" (FAQ revised 12/11/2023). — [Alaska Division of Elections, PFD AVR FAQ](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/doc/info/PFD%20AVR%20FAQ.pdf)
- **Fee:** The EAC estimates about $20 and classifies Alaska as "open." A Juneau Empire article (undated in results) said the database "costs $21." SB 192 (2017-18) proposed $1,500 for the statewide list, but I could not confirm it was enacted. — [EAC](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf); [Juneau Empire](https://www.juneauempire.com/news/trumps-request-for-voter-info-prompts-alaskans-privacy-concerns); [SB 192 file](https://www.akleg.gov/pdf/billfiles/Committee%20Bill%20Files/2017-2018%20-%2030th%20Legislature/SSTA/SB%20192.pdf)
- **Withheld fields:** AS 15.07.195(a) makes confidential "the voter's age or date of birth," the Social Security number or any part of it, the driver's license number, the voter identification number (per some versions), place of birth, and signature. Under § 195(b), a voter may elect in writing to keep the residential address confidential if they provide a separate mailing address. — [Justia, AS 15.07.195](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/2022/title-15/chapter-07/section-15-07-195); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-15-elections/ak-st-sect-15-07-195/)
- **Fields included:** Names, residential and mailing addresses, and party affiliation (NCSL table, as summarized in search results). — [NCSL (via search)](https://www.ncsl.org/research/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists.aspx)
- **Commercial use:** I found no statutory or form-level limit. The only proposal I found is HB 376 (20th Legislature, 1997-98), which would have added a ban to AS 15.07.127 on using "voter registration information for commercial purposes," with a class B misdemeanor penalty. The search results show only bill drafts. — [HB 376 text](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Text/20?Hsid=HB0376B). A vendor site states that Alaska places no statutory use restriction on the list. This is a low-authority source. — [voterfile.io](https://voterfile.io/alaska-voter-file-software)
- **Conflicting secondary source:** A Justia 50-state summary, as rendered in search results, described Alaska's use as limited to "non-commercial purposes, including a campaign, election, political activity…revision of election district boundaries." That wording matches Arizona's § 16-168, so it is probably a row misattribution. — [Justia 50-state survey](https://justia.com/administrative-law/voter-registration-lists-50-state-survey)

### Inferences
- The current statute does not appear to restrict use, so an internal locate and link database is permissible. The usable data has no DOB or age, which limits identity-matching quality: matching would rely on name and address only.
- The Justia "non-commercial" entry is probably an Arizona row bleeding into Alaska. The statute text in the results contains no use clause.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the current fee, whether the purchase form contains a use certification, or whether voting history is included. akleg.gov was blocked, so I could not view the current AS 15.07.127 directly or check for 2023-2026 amendments.

## Arizona

### Takeaway
**NOT ALLOWED.** A.R.S. § 16-168 limits precinct registers and registration-derived lists to specifically authorized purposes (political, election, government and similar), bars use "for a commercial purpose as defined in section 39-121.03," and makes violations a **class 6 felony**.

### Cited Findings
- **Use restriction:** Registers may be used only for specifically authorized purposes and "not for a commercial purpose as defined in section 39-121.03." "The sale of registers, lists and information derived from registration forms to a candidate or a registered political committee for a use specifically authorized by this subsection does not constitute use for a commercial purpose." — [azleg.gov, A.R.S. § 16-168](https://azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm); [Justia 2005 text](https://law.justia.com/codes/arizona/2005/title16/00168.html); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/az/title-16-elections-and-electors/az-rev-st-sect-16-168/)
- **Internet restriction:** "A person in possession of information derived from voter registration forms or precinct registers shall not distribute, post or otherwise provide access to any portion of that information through the internet…" — [azleg.gov § 16-168](https://azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm)
- **Penalty:** Violation is a class 6 felony. — [azleg.gov § 16-168](https://azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm); [HB2425 House engrossed text](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/bills/HB2425H.htm)
- **Register contents:** Official precinct registers "shall contain at least the names in full, party preference, date of registration and residence address of each qualified elector." — [azleg.gov § 16-168](https://azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm)
- **Withheld fields:** A 2022 House summary (HB2710) describes keeping the month and day of birth, the father's name or mother's maiden name, and signature records out of public access. The 2005 version listed birth year as a register field. — [HB2710 summary, 2022](https://azleg.gov/legtext/55leg/2R/summary/H.HB2710_032922_SENATEENGROSSED.DOCX.htm); [Justia 2005](https://law.justia.com/codes/arizona/2005/title16/00168.html). A.R.S. § 16-153 governs confidential registrants (protected-address voters). — [azleg.gov § 16-153](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00153.htm)
- **Recent amendments (2025):** HB2425 ("voter registration information; registers; violations") and SB 1375 proposed amending § 16-168. The HB2425 House summary describes removing the class 6 felony for possessing or buying registers and for unauthorized reproduction or sale. Its House-engrossed text still contains the commercial-purpose bar. I could not confirm whether either bill was enacted or vetoed. — [HB2425 summary](https://azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/summary/H.HB2425_013025_FMAE.DOCX.htm); [SB1375 House engrossed](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/bills/SB1375H.htm)

### Inferences
- From background knowledge (not fetched in this session): A.R.S. § 39-121.03 defines "commercial purpose" to include "any purpose in which the purchaser can reasonably anticipate the receipt of monetary gain from the direct or indirect use of the public record," in addition to sale or resale and solicitation. An internal database that supports a for-profit locate and debt-linking business fits the "indirect monetary gain" prong, even without redistribution or mailing. Arizona is therefore one of the clearest NOT ALLOWED states. Verify this text at azleg.gov/ars/39/00121-03.htm.
- Even if HB2425 lowered the felony classification, nothing I saw suggests it removed the commercial-purpose bar.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the final 2025 status of HB2425 and SB1375. I found no Arizona AG opinion or case interpreting "commercial purpose" for voter lists. County fees are not confirmed.

## California

### Takeaway
**NOT ALLOWED.** Voter registration information is "NOT public data." It is released only for "election, scholarly, journalistic, or political purposes, or for governmental purposes, as determined by the Secretary of State." Regulations expressly list "commercial purpose" uses as impermissible, and misuse is a misdemeanor.

### Cited Findings
- **Who:** Elec. Code § 2194 makes the information available "to any person for election, scholarly, journalistic, or political purposes, or for governmental purposes, as determined by the Secretary of State." — [Justia, Elec. Code § 2194 (2024)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-elec/division-2/chapter-2/article-5/section-2194); [california.public.law](https://california.public.law/codes/elections_code_section_2194)
- **How:** A written application is required under Elec. Code § 2188, with proof of purpose. For example, a political requester submits "documentation establishing compliance with Title 2 of the California Code of Regulations §19003(a)(4)," and a scholarly requester submits a "letter from a representative of the institution… on institutional letterhead." — [Sacramento County application](https://elections.saccounty.gov/content/dam/vre/documents/Application-to-Access-Voter-Registration-Information-English.pdf); [Monterey County application](https://montereycountyelections.us/files/Website_content/Forms/Data_Services/Application%20for%20Voter%20Registration%20Information.pdf); [SOS voter file request page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request)
- **Regulations:** 2 CCR §§ 19001-19013. Permissible uses are in § 19003. Section 19004 lists impermissible uses, including "any communication for any personal, private, or commercial purpose other than for those purposes permitted by Section 19003" and "solicitation of contributions or services for any personal, private, or commercial purpose." The Justia copy of § 19004 (through Sept. 2024) emphasizes fraud and bad-faith uses, so the versions differ. Check the SOS text. — [SOS regulations page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/administration/regulations/current-regulations/elections/access-voter-registration-information); [Justia 2 CCR § 19004](https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-2/division-7/chapter-1/article-1/section-19004). Section 19005 governs transfers and § 19012 storage and security. — [Cornell § 19005](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/2-CCR-19005); [Cornell § 19012](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/2-CCR-19012)
- **SOS form:** "voter registration information is NOT public data and state laws govern how and by whom voter registration information may be used"; "a violation of the above constitutes a misdemeanor." — [SOS request-for-contact form](https://elections.cdn.sos.ca.gov/pdfs/request-for-contact.pdf); [Placer County 2026 application](https://www.placercountyelections.gov/Uploads/documents/Application_to_Access_Voter_Registration_Information_2026.pdf)
- **Penalty:** Elec. Code § 18109(a) makes it a misdemeanor for a person in possession of the information to use it other than as permitted. Section 18109(b) makes it a misdemeanor "knowingly to acquire possession or use of voter registration information… without first complying with Section 2188." Section 2194 requires each disclosure to carry notice of §§ 18109 and 18540. — [Justia § 18109](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2022/code-elec/division-18/chapter-2/section-18109/); [SOS common fraud violations](https://elections.cdn.sos.ca.gov/fraud-complaints/pdfs/common-elections-fraud-violations.pdf?os=f)
- **Confidentiality cite:** County forms cite former Gov. Code § 6254.4, which has been recodified as Gov. Code § 7924.000. — [Sacramento County form](https://elections.saccounty.gov/Documents/Application-to-Access-Voter-Registration-Information-English.pdf)

### Inferences
- An internal commercial locate database fits none of the five permitted purposes and falls squarely within § 19004's "personal, private, or commercial purpose." Applying would also require a false statement of purpose on the § 2188 application, which is itself a misdemeanor risk.
- From background knowledge: the California file includes DOB, phone and email where provided, while DL number, ID number, SSN and signature are confidential under § 2194(b). Confidential voters (Safe at Home, Elec. Code § 2166 et seq.) are excluded. This matters little because the use is barred.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the current text of § 19004 (the versions conflict) or the exact field list. The statewide SOS fee is not confirmed. I found no AG opinion specific to commercial locate uses.

## Colorado

### Takeaway
**PROBABLY ALLOWED (verify).** The Secretary of State sells the statewide list to any requester ($50 per list, $1,000 per election-cycle subscription). Several sources say Colorado places no restriction on use. Others, the EAC and the Justia survey, label Colorado "non-commercial." I could not locate a statute or rule imposing a commercial-use ban, so confirm the current Elections Data Request Form terms before relying on this.

### Cited Findings
- **Cost:** Statewide Registered Voter List (EX-003) is $50, and an election-cycle subscription is $1,000. The 2025-2026 data request form (revised Nov 2024) carries the same figures. — [CO SOS fee schedule](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/info_center/fees/elections.html); [CO SOS Elections Data Request Form](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/forms/dataRequests.pdf); [CO SOS Public Voter Data FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html)
- **Fields:** Voter ID, names, residential address, birth year (not full DOB), gender identity if provided, party, and phone if provided. Excluded: SSN, DL number, month and day of birth, signature and email. These come from secondary sources. — [J-PAL admin data catalog](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/admindatacatalog/colorado-voter-records); [Ezel survey](https://ezel.ai/surveys/voter-registration-list-access-use-requirements/colorado)
- **Statutes:** C.R.S. § 1-2-227 (county clerk custody of registration records; public inspection, with confidentiality under C.R.S. § 24-72-204(3.5) for voters who request it and "nothing in this subsection (2) shall be construed to require any request, application, or fee for such confidentiality"). § 1-2-302 (SOS maintains the master list). § 1-2-213.3 (registration records available for public inspection per § 1-2-227). — [colorado.public.law § 1-2-227](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_1-2-227); [CO SOS Title 1 Art. 2](https://coloradosos.gov/pubs/info_center/laws/Title1/Title1Article2.html). C.R.S. § 24-72-204 refers to preparing a registration list for "distribution or sale to any person." — [Justia § 24-72-204 (2024)](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-24/public/article-72/part-2/section-24-72-204/)
- **"No restriction" sources:** A 2018 Connecticut OLR report said Colorado and Rhode Island did not appear to restrict access to or use of voter registration information. — [CT OLR 2018-R-0119](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2018/rpt/pdf/2018-R-0119.pdf). Ezel says any person may request the public list. — [Ezel](https://ezel.ai/surveys/voter-registration-list-access-use-requirements/colorado)
- **"Non-commercial" sources:** The EAC Voter Roll Privacy document (2024) and the Justia 50-state survey list Colorado's purpose of use as non-commercial. — [EAC Voter Roll Privacy 2024](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf); [Justia survey](https://www.justia.com/administrative-law/voter-registration-lists-50-state-survey/). A 2018 Montana legislative compilation reportedly says commercial misuse is a "serious misdemeanor," but its row alignment was unclear in the results. — [MT SAVA 2018 compilation](https://archive.legmt.gov/content/Committees/Interim/2017-2018/State-Administration-and-Veterans-Affairs/Meetings/Sept-2018/Voter%20List%20Use%20and%20Distribution%202018.pdf)
- **Rules and recent activity:** SOS Election Rule 2 (8 CCR 1505-1) governs voter data. A December 2025 permanent rule adoption exists, but I could not see its content. A September 2025 Colorado Newsline op-ed urged voters to "go confidential," implying the list is widely accessible. — [8 CCR 1505-1 Rule 2](https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/rule_making/CurrentRules/8CCR1505-1/Rule2.pdf); [CO SOS Dec 2025 rule adoption](https://coloradosos.gov/pubs/rule_making/files/2025/20251222ElectionsPermAdoptionSB.pdf); [Colorado Newsline](https://coloradonewsline.com/2025/09/25/colorado-voters-should-go-confidential/)

### Inferences
- If Colorado's only limits are confidentiality-based (§ 24-72-204(3.5) confidential voters, Address Confidentiality Program, protected identifiers), then internal commercial use is allowed. The EAC "non-commercial" label may be a generalization, or it may reflect a term on the request form. The likely condition is to comply with any certification on the SOS data request form and exclude confidential voters.
- Birth year (no full DOB) gives moderate matching value.

### Gaps
- I could not view the data request form's certification language, the full text of § 1-2-227, or the December 2025 rule change. This is the single most important open item for Colorado.

## Hawaii

### Takeaway
**NOT ALLOWED.** Only name, district/precinct and voter status are public. All other affidavit data is confidential and may be released only "for election or government purposes," under a sworn certification. Any other use or distribution is unlawful (HRS §§ 11-97, 11-14; HAR § 3-177-160).

### Cited Findings
- **Public fields:** HRS § 11-97: a voter's "full name, district/precinct designation, and voter status shall be public; but all other personal information, as provided on the voter registration affidavit, shall be confidential." Non-public information "may be released for election or government purposes in accordance with rules adopted by the chief election officer, pursuant to chapter 91." — [HRS § 11-97 (current)](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol01_Ch0001-0042F/HRS0011/HRS_0011-0097.htm)
- **Use restriction:** HRS § 11-14: the general county register "shall be available for election or government purposes only in accordance with section 11-97." Section 11-14(d): "Unless authorized under section 11-97, it shall be unlawful for any person to use, print, publish, or distribute any voter registration information acquired directly or indirectly from the voter registration affidavits or any list prepared…" — [HRS § 11-14 (current)](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol01_Ch0001-0042F/HRS0011/HRS_0011-0014.htm)
- **Rule and certification:** HAR § 3-177-160: the register and data "shall be released or used for election or government purposes only, unless otherwise provided by law." Requests for non-public information require a sworn certification of purpose (a self-subscribing oath). The rule implements HRS §§ 11-14, 11-97 and 19-6. — [Cornell, HAR § 3-177-160](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-3-177-160)
- **OIP opinion:** Hawaii's Office of Information Practices said a county clerk may deny access to the General County Register under the UIPA to a member of the public who is not seeking it for "election or government purposes." — [Hawaii OIP](https://ags.hawaii.gov/oip/?p=1147)
- **Penalty:** The 2012 amendment (Act 225) to § 11-14 ties violations to a misdemeanor, but the snippet was incomplete. Chapter 19 ("Election Offenses," incl. § 19-6) is the likely penalty provision. — [SLH 2012 Act 225](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/sessionlaws/Years/SLH2012/SLH2012_Act225.pdf)
- **Address protection:** HRS § 11-14.5 provides residence-address confidentiality in life-threatening circumstances. — [HRS § 11-14.5](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrsarchive/hrs1999/Vol01/hrs011/HRS_11-14_5.htm)

### Inferences
- Even the public subset (name, precinct, status) has no address, so it has little value for locating people. The full file cannot be obtained or used for a commercial locate database.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the exact penalty classification (misdemeanor vs. other) in HRS § 19-6.

## Idaho

### Takeaway
**ALLOWED WITH CONDITIONS.** The SOS sells statewide reports ($20 per report, with or without voting history). Idaho Code § 34-437A(3), and § 34-437 for county lists, bars using list information "for the purpose of mailing or delivering any advertisement or offer for any property, establishment, organization, product or service," or solicitation. An internal database with no mailing or solicitation falls outside that text. The conditions: (1) sign the SOS acknowledgement, (2) never use the data to mail or deliver offers or solicitations, including debt-collection or property-offer mail, and (3) do not redistribute it in a way that enables such use.

### Cited Findings
- **Restriction text:** "[No person] … shall use any information contained therein for the purpose of mailing or delivering any advertisement or offer for any property, establishment, organization, product or service or for the purpose of mailing or delivering any solicitation for money, services or anything of value" (SOS order form language). The lists "may be used for any political purpose." — [Idaho SOS PRR / voter registration report order form](https://sos.idaho.gov/downloads/PRR_Absentee_VoterReg_Report.pdf); [Idaho Code § 34-437A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title34/T34CH4/SECT34-437A); [§ 34-437 (Justia 2023)](https://law.justia.com/codes/idaho/title-34/chapter-4/section-34-437)
- **Acknowledgement:** The SOS acknowledgement form cites "Section 34-437A (3), Idaho Code" and requires signers to acknowledge the prohibition. It notes that "by submitting this form the information provided will become public record." — [Idaho SOS acknowledgement](https://sos.idaho.gov/PressRelease/CommissionDetails/Voter%20Registration%20-%20Acknowledgement.pdf)
- **County certification:** Ada County requesters certify "the acquired information of registered electors will not be used for any purposes restricted under Idaho Code Section 34-437." The fee is $20 for all public voter file requests. — [Ada County Elections](https://adacounty.id.gov/elections/?p=1741)
- **Fee:** $20 per report, including the report with voter history. — [Idaho SOS order form](https://sos.idaho.gov/downloads/PRR_Absentee_VoterReg_Report.pdf)
- **Amendment history:** One source says § 34-437A was last amended in 2003. — [FindLaw § 34-437A](https://codes.findlaw.com/id/title-34-elections/id-st-sect-34-437a/)

### Inferences
- The prohibition is act-based (mailing or delivering ads, offers or solicitations), not purpose-based ("commercial use"). An internal locate and link database is facially outside it. The risk is downstream: if the database feeds collection letters or property-acquisition mailers, that would likely be "delivering any solicitation for money" or "any … offer for any property." The safest condition is a hard internal control that voter-derived addresses are never used for mail or delivered outreach.
- Voting history is available. Whether DOB or age is included is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- I did not find the penalty for violating § 34-437A(3) (misdemeanor or civil) or the exact released field list (DOB vs. birth year vs. age). I found no Idaho AG opinion on non-mailing commercial use.

## Montana

### Takeaway
**NOT ALLOWED.** The SOS and local administrators furnish voter files only "for noncommercial use" (MCA §§ 13-2-122, 13-2-115). An internal database for a for-profit locate and debt-linking business is commercial use under any ordinary reading. "Noncommercial" is undefined, and the SOS tells requesters to consult an attorney.

### Cited Findings
- **Statute:** MCA § 13-2-122 (2025): "Upon request, a local election administrator shall furnish to an individual, for noncommercial use" precinct registers, elector lists and mailing labels at no more than actual cost. The SOS furnishes "available extracts and reports from the statewide voter registration system" on the same noncommercial basis. The 2013 amendment (Ch. 336, effective 2014) changed "elector" to "individual." The 1999 text required a "written request." — [MCA § 13-2-122 (current)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0130/chapter_0020/part_0010/section_0220/0130-0020-0010-0220.html); [1999 text](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/1999/mca/13/2/13-2-122.htm)
- **§ 13-2-115:** The SOS must furnish a current list of registered electors "to any elector, for noncommercial use," on written request. — [MCA § 13-2-115 (1999)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/1999/mca/13/2/13-2-115.htm)
- **SOS page:** The fee is $1,000 for one copy of the complete statewide file and $5,000 for a one-year subscription. The full fee schedule and field list are in ARM 44.3.1101. The page says "if you need legal advice on the definition of non-commercial use, please consult with an attorney." — [Montana SOS voter file page](https://sosmt.gov/?p=29810); [ARM 44.3.1101](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/montana/regulation/44.3.1101)
- **Litigation:** ACORN International v. State (Mont., DA 23-0382) challenged whether the fees exceed "actual cost" under § 13-2-122(1). The challenge concerned fees, not use. — [ACORN Int'l v. State](https://statecourtreport.org/sites/default/files/fastcase/converted/ACORN%20Int%27l%20v.%20State%2C%20Mont.%20DA%2023-0382.pdf)
- **Related statute:** MCA § 2-6-1017 (amended by 2019 SB 46) separately restricts the use of public-agency distribution lists for unsolicited contact. — [MCA § 2-6-1017](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/2015/mca/2/6/2-6-1017.htm); [SB 46](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/2019/SB0099/SB0046_1.pdf)
- **2025-26 interim:** The SAVA SJ 35 study of voter list maintenance notes that citizens may buy an annual subscription. — [SAVA staff memo Nov 2025](https://archive.legmt.gov/content/Committees/Interim/2025-2026/SAVA/11.17.2025/6.2-SJ35-Staff-Memo-Nov25.pdf)

### Inferences
- Without a definition, "noncommercial" most naturally excludes use in the course of a for-profit business, even if purely internal. Montana should be treated as NOT ALLOWED.

### Gaps
- I did not find the penalty for commercial misuse or the exact text of any requester certification. The ARM 44.3.1101 field list (whether DOB or birth year is included) is unverified.

## Nevada

### Takeaway
**NOT ALLOWED (per SOS interpretation; statutory text ambiguous).** Any person may obtain lists under NRS 293.440, and the SOS provides statewide reports free through a NevVoter account and a signed form. The SOS and the 2026 Elections Procedures Manual state that any recipient is "prohibited from using such information for purposes not related to an election and from selling such information for any reason" (citing NRS 293.440(6)(b)). Older statute text applied the ban only to party committees that received free lists.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and how:** "Any person who desires a copy of any list of the persons who are registered to vote in any precinct, district or county may obtain a copy by applying at the office of the county clerk and paying therefor a sum of money equal to 1 cent per name" (2013 text). The statewide list requires a two-step SOS process: create an Online Services web account, then submit a signed "Official Request for List of Registered Voters" form. Qualifications are verified when the form arrives. — [Justia NRS 293.440 (2013)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/2013/chapter-293/statute-293.440/); [NV SOS NevVoter Statewide List](https://www.nvsos.gov/sos/elections/voters/nevvoter-statewide-list)
- **Use restriction (SOS reading):** "any person, candidate, party or organization who receives information requested on this form is prohibited from using such information for purposes not related to an election and from selling such information for any reason." The requester certifies the data will be used only for purposes state or federal law authorizes, and the SOS "retains full authority to grant, deny, or revoke access." — [NV SOS NevVoter](https://www.nvsos.gov/sos/elections/voters/nevvoter-statewide-list). The 2026 Elections Procedures Manual says the information cannot be sold for any reason and cites NRS 293.440(6)(b). — [NV Elections Procedures Manual 2026](http://epubs.nsla.nv.gov/statepubs/epubs/779609-2026.pdf)
- **Statutory text (older versions):** The ban is addressed to "Any state or county central committee of a major political party, any executive committee of a minor political party or any member or representative of such a central committee or executive committee who receives without charge a list…" Such recipients "shall not" use it for purposes unrelated to an election or sell it. — [Justia NRS 293.440 (2013)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/2013/chapter-293/statute-293.440/); [Justia NRS 293.440 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-293/statute-293-440/)
- **Fields:** NRS 293.440 lists include address, date of birth and telephone number "except as otherwise provided in NRS 293.5002 and 293.558." Inactive voters are included unless the requester asks to exclude them. — [Justia NRS 293.440](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-293/statute-293-440/); [NV LCB Research, Election Security 2020](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Research/Documents/ElectionSecurity2020NevadaGeneralElection.pdf)
- **Withheld:** NRS 293.558 withholds address and phone on the voter's request, email addresses, SSN, and DL or ID card number. Clerks "may not release a registered voter's electronic mail address to a third party." NRS 293.5002 covers confidential-address (fictitious address) voters. — [Justia NRS 293.558 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-293/statute-293-558/)
- **Cost and voting history:** A 2019 SOS guide says "ALL Statewide Voter Registration List reports are available free of charge" and that voter history "only tracks the method by which a voter casts their ballot, if any." The EAC separately estimated $20,000, which conflicts. — [NV SOS report guide (2019)](https://nvsosuat.nv.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/2398/636754585927900000); [EAC](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf)

### Inferences
- Even if the strict statutory ban reaches only free party recipients, the SOS conditions access on a certification of lawful purpose and applies the election-only limit to everyone. It can deny or revoke access. A non-election commercial requester cannot truthfully complete the request as the SOS administers it, so treat Nevada as NOT ALLOWED unless counsel concludes the statute does not restrict paid requesters at the county level.
- Nevada would otherwise be high value (full DOB and phone), which makes the ambiguity worth a targeted legal check of current NRS 293.440(6) and of county-level practice.

### Gaps
- I did not see the current full text of NRS 293.440(6), so I could not settle whether its ban covers all recipients or only free ones. Current fee data conflict (free vs. EAC's $20,000). I found no AG opinion or case.
