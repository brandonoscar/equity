# Voter file access and permitted uses: Southeast (AL, AR, FL, GA, KY, LA, MS, NC, SC, TN, VA, WV)

Use case being tested: a private company gets the statewide voter file and uses it **internally** to locate people and link them to property and debt records. No redistribution, no solicitation or mailing, non-political.

Research date: 2026-11-25. **Method limitation (read first):** in this session WebFetch could not reach the primary hosts (ncsl.org and law.lis.virginia.gov did not resolve, so per the session rules no further direct fetches were tried). Everything below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages (official legislature, SoS/SBE pages, request forms, Justia copies, EAC and NCSL). **I did not read any statute in full.** Quotes are the snippet text as the search tool returned it. Anything I add from background knowledge is labeled "unverified" and kept in Inferences or Gaps. Before relying on a verdict, confirm the current statute text on the official site.

## Q1. Who may obtain the statewide voter file, and how (request method, fee, affidavit)?

### Takeaway
Only **Arkansas, North Carolina, Mississippi and (probably) Florida** let any member of the public get the file with no purpose certification. Georgia and Alabama also sell to anyone, but both now attach a no-commercial-use rule. **Virginia** limits recipients to listed political and civic categories. **Tennessee** sells only to people who certify a political purpose. **South Carolina** sells only to registered SC voters. **Kentucky** requires a sworn request form and refuses commercial requesters. **Louisiana** and **West Virginia** sell to the public but only for noncommercial use.

### Cited Findings
**Alabama**
- A 2007 Alabama AG opinion read Ala. Code § 17-4-38 as requiring the SoS "to establish and charge a reasonable, consistent fee for production of the voter registration list and to make such list available to anyone who applies for it." — [AG opinion 2007-132 (ebench copy)](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/alabama/opinions/250223130-ago-2007-132-august-27-2007); [Justia § 17-4-38 (2019)](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2019/title-17/chapter-4/article-2/section-17-4-38/)
- The SoS says the statewide list "is available for purchase online pursuant to Alabama Code Section 17-4-38." — [AL SoS](https://www.sos.alabama.gov/node/3271)
- Fee before 2026: one cent per voter record, $1.00 minimum. **HB67 (2026 RS)** caps the fee at "not exceeding one thousand dollars ($1,000) per electronic voter list produced." — [AL SoS press release (Nov 2025)](https://www.sos.alabama.gov/newsroom/secretary-state-wes-allen-backs-bill-protect-voter-data-and-lower-cost-alabamas-voter-list); [HB67 enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-enr.pdf)
- Enactment of HB67: LegiScan marks it "Enacted," dated May 8, 2026. Ballotpedia (Apr 28, 2026) lists HB67 among 14 election bills that Gov. Ivey signed and describes it as capping the electronic voter list fee at $1,000. The two sources give different dates. — [LegiScan HB67](https://legiscan.com/AL/bill/HB67/2026); [Ballotpedia News](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/04/28/alabama-enacts-post-election-audit-law-13-other-election-related-bills-in-2026-session/)

**Arkansas**
- NCSL (as summarized): anyone may request a list for a fee. — [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/research/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists.aspx)
- A 2017 EPIC letter says the SoS charged $2.50 per statewide voter registration data file. This figure may be out of date. — [EPIC 2017 letter](https://Epic.org/privacy/voting/EPIC-17-07-13-AR-SOS-20170713-Request.pdf)
- Requests go on the SoS "Data Request Form." — [AR SoS Data Request Form](https://www.sos.arkansas.gov/uploads/elections/Data%20Request%20Form.pdf); [updated form](https://www.sos.arkansas.gov/uploads/Data_Request_Form_Updated.pdf)
- County clerks may charge a fee to cover reproduction costs. The 2017 code capped a CD list of 1–5,000 voters at $10. That page is marked superseded. — [Justia Ark. Code § 7-5-109](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/2017/title-7/chapter-5/subchapter-1/section-7-5-109/)

**Florida**
- The Division of Elections issues a monthly statewide "voter extract" disk by mail or pickup, "NOT available by email or online." Requesters give contact information and a mailing address. "A one-time request may be made each year to receive all monthly extract disks to be issued for the calendar year." Any further request "will be processed as a public records request and subject to administrative fees for processing." — [FL DOS Voter Extract Request page](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/data-statistics/voter-registration-statistics/voter-extract-request/)
- Format: zipped tab-delimited files, 67 county registration files plus 67 county voting-history files. — [FL Voter Extract File Layout (rev. 2025-05-16)](https://files.floridados.gov/media/708728/final-voter-extract-disk-file-layout-rev-20250516.pdf)
- Historical versions (2001 § 98.0979, 2004 § 98.095) limited copies to municipalities, government agencies, candidates, political committees, parties and incumbent officeholders, and required an oath. The 2004 text also said "registration books of each county ... are public records." — [Fla. Stat. 98.095 (2004)](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2004/98.095); [98.0979 (2001)](https://leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_Mode=Display_Statute&StatuteYear=2001&URL=Ch0098/Sec0979.htm)
- A vendor site says the statewide extract is available to anyone with no purpose oath, and that the older restriction was repealed in 2005. Not verified against an official source. — [voterfile.io (vendor)](https://voterfile.io/florida-voter-file-software)

**Georgia**
- Statewide voter list price: **$485.00**, under the SoS pricing policy adopted July 15, 2024. Older county pages still show $250. — [GA SoS Order Voter Registration Lists and Files](https://sos.ga.gov/page/order-voter-registration-lists-and-files); [Peach County (outdated $250)](https://peachcounty.gov/?p=1439)
- Orders use the SoS voter list order form. — [GA SoS order form](https://sos.ga.gov/voterlistorderform031822)

**Kentucky**
- Requests go on form SBE 84, "Request for Voter Registration Data," under oath. The State Board decides eligibility from the requester's stated use. — [KY SBE 84 (fillable)](https://elect.ky.gov/Candidates/Documents/SBE%2084%20%20Request%20for%20Voter%20Registration%20Data%20-%20FILLABLE.pdf); [SBE 84](https://elect.ky.gov:443/Candidates/Documents/SBE%2084%20Request%20for%20Voter%20Registration%20Data.pdf)
- Under KRS 117.025 (2009 text), the Board "shall not furnish precinct lists to persons who intend to use the lists for commercial use." Current versions cite the duty as subsection (3)(h) or (3)(i). — [Justia KRS 117.025 (2009)](https://law.justia.com/codes/kentucky/2009/117-00/025.html); [31 KAR 3:010](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/031/003/010/)

**Louisiana**
- Purchase is through the SoS online "Purchase Voter Lists" tool: the buyer defines a selection, gets a price quote, pays in advance, and receives a tab-delimited file by email. NCSL also says 25 or more qualified voters may request a copy in writing. These details come from third-party guides and NCSL, not an official SoS page. — [Activate guide](https://letsactivate.us/voter-data/louisiana); [Motion51](https://www.motion51.com/states/louisiana/voter-file); [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists)
- The registrar "shall permit the copying of a list prepared pursuant to R.S. 18:1311(A) upon the request of a single person of the age of majority." — [Justia La. R.S. 18:154 (2019)](https://law.justia.com/codes/louisiana/2019/revised-statutes/title-18/rs-154/)

**Mississippi**
- Copies of statewide voter files are provided under the Public Records Act at no more than actual production cost, with one free update within a year. — [Miss. Admin. Code 1-10-7.2](https://regulations.justia.com/states/mississippi/title-1/part-10/chapter-7/rule-1-10-7-2)
- The SoS's **2025 Voter File Weekly Distribution Program** gives a statewide voter file and voter-history file weekly, free, to people who sign up. It covers active and inactive voters. — [MS SoS 2025 Weekly Distribution Program](https://www.sos.ms.gov/content/documents/about_us/2025%20Voter%20File%20Weekly%20Distribution%20Program.pdf)
- Earlier, SoS Hosemann was quoted saying "anyone" paying $2,100 could get the master list (article undated). A 2022 bill (HB1352, introduced) proposed $500 for candidates and parties and $2,500 for others; I could not confirm it passed. — [Mississippi Free Press](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/?p=125141); [LegiScan HB1352 (2022)](https://legiscan.com/MS/text/HB1352/2022)

**North Carolina**
- The State Board posts public statewide voter registration files as free downloads, refreshed weekly. Removed voters drop off once their last vote is more than 10 years old. — [NCSBE Voter Registration Data](https://ncsbe.gov/results-data/voter-registration-data)

**South Carolina**
- "You must be a registered South Carolina voter to purchase a list." The State Election Commission (SEC) cites S.C. Code § 7-3-20(D)(13). Sales go through the SEC "saleable data" portal. — [SC SEC Sale of Voter Registration Lists](https://scvotes.gov/resources/sale-of-voter-registration-lists/); [SC Voter Purchase Data portal](https://info.scvotes.sc.gov/eng/saleabledata/home.aspx)
- In a MuckRock records exchange, the SEC said state law makes the lists available for purchase by registered SC voters. — [MuckRock](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/south-carolina-302/south-carolina-voter-registration-file-108450/)

**Tennessee**
- T.C.A. § 2-2-138: the list "shall be available for purchase to any person who certifies on a form provided by the state election commission that such list will be used for political purposes only." Lists compiled by the coordinator of elections (statewide) "shall be sold at a price established by the secretary of state." — [Justia T.C.A. § 2-2-138 (2021)](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/2021/title-2/chapter-2/part-1/section-2-2-138/); [Hamilton County EC](https://elect.hamiltontn.gov/VoterListNew.aspx)
- Pricing follows the uniform fee schedule set by the State Election Commission. — [Maury County EC](https://mauryelections.happyfox.com/kb/article/12-purchasing-voter-registration-lists-voter-rolls/)

**Virginia**
- Va. Code § 24.2-405: the Department of Elections provides lists to candidates, political party committees, political action committees, incumbent officeholders reporting to constituents, and certain nonprofits that promote voter participation, among others. The search summary did not return the full current list of recipients. — [Va. Code § 24.2-405 (LIS)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title24.2/chapter4/section24.2-405/); [Justia 2019](https://law.justia.com/codes/virginia/2019/title-24-2/chapter-4/section-24-2-405/)
- The Department sells lists "at a reasonable cost to qualified persons or entities." — [VA ELECT Client Services](https://www.elections.virginia.gov/candidatepac-info/client-services)

**West Virginia**
- Voter data is provided through the SoS under W. Va. Code § 3-2-30(f). The requester signs a request and affidavit. — [WV SoS Voter Data Request](https://sos.wv.gov/FormSearch/Elections/Informational/Voter%20Data%20Request.pdf); [W. Va. Code § 3-2-30](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/3-2-30/)

### Inferences
- Of these 12 states, only NC (free download) and MS (free weekly program) offer a no-cost, no-certification statewide channel. Florida is free once a year, but whether a current purpose restriction exists is unresolved (Q2).
- In SC, the buyer must be a registered SC voter. A company cannot buy in its own name. It would need an individual voter to buy, which may itself be problematic if the data then serves a commercial purpose.

### Gaps
- Current fees could not be confirmed for AR, LA, MS (paid channel) or the TN statewide list. The figures shown are dated.
- The full current § 24.2-405 recipient list (VA) was not retrieved.
- The current Louisiana SoS purchase terms were not retrieved from an official page.

## Q2. Statutory use restrictions and penalties (operative language)

### Takeaway
Explicit commercial-use bans (statute, regulation, or affidavit) apply in **AL (from 2026), GA, KY, LA, VA (purpose-limited), TN (political only) and WV (noncommercial)**. **SC** bans "commercial solicitation" through its general public-records law. **AR** says no use restrictions exist. **NC and MS**: none found. **FL**: older law restricted use; the current text was not verified.

### Cited Findings
**Alabama (new 2026 restriction)**
- HB67 enrolled: "it shall be unlawful to sell or publish any portion of the voter list or use voter data or information maintained by the Secretary of State under this section for commercial purposes." The exceptions are "as provided in this section and Sections 11-46-36, 17-4-1, and 17-16-2." — [HB67 enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-enr.pdf)
- The engrossed text says use "for campaign or election purposes" under the Fair Campaign Practices Act "does not constitute commercial use." The engrossed effective date is June 1, 2026. — [HB67 engrossed](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-eng.pdf)
- SoS Allen (Jan 2026 editorial): the bill "will make it illegal to use information obtained from the state's voter list for any commercial purpose." — [Mountain Eagle editorial](https://www.mountaineagle.com/opinion/an-editorial-in-support-of-hb67-by-alabama-secretary-of-state-wes-allen/article_64e1bf68-0e7e-59ba-9c26-4aa4486128f2.html)

**Arkansas**
- The SoS says "there are no state laws that place restrictions on the use of data that we release." It notes that federal and state laws restrict some fields. — [AR SoS FAQ](https://www.sos.arkansas.gov/frequently-asked-questions/35)

**Florida**
- 2004 § 98.095: the information "shall not be used for purposes which are not related to elections, political or governmental activities, voter registration, or law enforcement." Requesters signed an oath, and commercial use was barred. — [Fla. Stat. 98.095 (2004)](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2004/98.095)
- An EAC table describes Florida requests as limited to non-commercial purposes. NCSL lists the Florida file as available to the public. These two sources conflict. — [EAC Voter Roll Privacy (Mar 2024)](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf); [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists)
- A vendor says the restriction was repealed in 2005 (unverified). — [voterfile.io](https://voterfile.io/florida-voter-file-software)

**Georgia**
- O.C.G.A. § 21-2-225(c): the data "may not be used by any person for commercial purposes." — [Justia § 21-2-225 (2024)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-21/chapter-2/article-6/section-21-2-225)
- O.C.G.A. § 21-2-601: "Any person who intentionally uses the list of electors provided for in Code Section 21-2-225 for commercial purposes shall be guilty of a misdemeanor." County order forms repeat this. — [Justia § 21-2-601](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2020/title-21/chapter-2/article-15/section-21-2-601/); [Fulton County order form (Aug 2023)](https://fultoncountyga.gov/-/media/Departments/Registration-and-Elections/Registration-and-Elections--Ordering-Voter-List-FormAugust-2023.pdf)

**Kentucky**
- 31 KAR 3:010 defines "commercial use" as "use of a voter registration list, or any part thereof, in any form, for profit, the solicitation of donations, or for the sale or advertisement of any good, service, or publication." It includes transfer or sale of the list to another person. The requester may not give the list to anyone who intends commercial use. — [31 KAR 3:010 (engrossed amendment, doc 16761)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/downloads/docs/16761/document.engrossed.pdf); [SBE 84](https://elect.ky.gov:443/Candidates/Documents/SBE%2084%20Request%20for%20Voter%20Registration%20Data.pdf)
- Exceptions: uses for "scholarly, journalistic, political (including political fund raising), or governmental purposes," and publications sold to qualified candidates or committees. — [31 KAR 3:010](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/031/003/010/); [ebench copy](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/kentucky/regulation/31%20KAR%203:010)
- An amendment to 31 KAR 3:010 was open for public comment until Dec 31, 2025. Whether it was adopted, and what it changed, was not confirmed. — [KAR version 16503](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/031/003/010/16503/)

**Louisiana**
- R.S. 18:154 bars the registrar and Department of State from "circulating on a commercial list or otherwise disclosing" listed items: assistance status, SSN, DL number, day and month of birth, mother's maiden name, email, SMS number, and applications from 16–17-year-olds. — [Justia R.S. 18:154 (2017)](https://law.justia.com/codes/louisiana/2017/code-revisedstatutes/title-18/rs-18-154/); [legis.la.gov](https://legis.la.gov/legis/law.aspx?d=81488)
- **Act 225 of 2023 (HB 174)**, effective Aug 1, 2023: also bars disclosing a UOCAVA absentee voter's active-duty or dependent status and the address the ballot is mailed to. — [HB174 digest/text](https://legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1304980)
- Third-party guides and the NCSL chart describe the purchased list as "commercial use prohibited" or "non-commercial use." Permitted uses: election, political, governmental, scholarly, journalistic and political fundraising. The operative statutory sentence was not retrieved. — [Activate](https://letsactivate.us/voter-data/louisiana); [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists)

**Mississippi**
- No commercial-use ban was found in the rule or the SoS program materials I reviewed. — [Miss. Admin. Code 1-10-7.2](https://regulations.justia.com/states/mississippi/title-1/part-10/chapter-7/rule-1-10-7-2); [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Availability_of_state_voter_files)

**North Carolina**
- The files are public records under G.S. 163-82.10. No use restriction appeared in the State Board page or statute summaries. — [NCSBE](https://ncsbe.gov/results-data/voter-registration-data); [G.S. 163-82.10](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-82.10.html)

**South Carolina**
- SEC notice: "Obtaining or using public records for commercial solicitation directed to any person in this State is prohibited under South Carolina Code Section 30-2-50." Knowingly violating it is a misdemeanor under § 30-2-50(D). — [SC SEC Sale page](https://scvotes.gov/resources/sale-of-voter-registration-lists/); [SEC privacy statement](https://scvotes.gov/about-the-sec/sec-website-privacy-statement/); [S.C. Code 30-2-50 (Justia 2021)](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/2021/title-30/chapter-2/section-30-2-50/)
- A 2018 W&M commentary says purchasers "cannot use it for commercial purposes," which reads broader than the SEC's solicitation framing. — [State of Elections (W&M), 2018](https://stateofelections.pages.wm.edu/2018/02/26/loophole-state-law-u-s-election-fraud-commission/)

**Tennessee**
- Purchasers certify the list "will be used for political purposes only" (T.C.A. § 2-2-138). False certification is a Class B misdemeanor. Unauthorized vendor use is a Class B misdemeanor and grounds for decertification. — [Justia § 2-2-138](https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/2021/title-2/chapter-2/part-1/section-2-2-138/); [Maury County EC](https://mauryelections.happyfox.com/kb/article/12-purchasing-voter-registration-lists-voter-rolls/); [Knox County "political purposes" form](https://www.KnoxCounty.org/election/pdfs/political_purposes.pdf)

**Virginia**
- Recipients "may not publish on the Internet any of the information contained in such list as a list, database, or other similar searchable format" (§ 24.2-405). — [Va. Code § 24.2-405](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/24.2-405)
- § 24.2-407: "Any person receiving lists pursuant to § 24.2-405, 24.2-406, 24.2-706, or 24.2-710 shall sign the following statement." The statement commits the signer to use the lists only for the purposes prescribed, not to permit copying by unauthorized persons, and acknowledges felony penalties for false statements under § 24.2-1016. — [Va. Code § 24.2-407](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/24.2-407/); [W&M eBenchbook](https://ebenchbook.wm.edu/virginia/statutes/%c2%a7-24-2-407-statement-for-persons-receiving-lists-of-persons-registered-or-voting-penalties/)
- § 24.2-407.1 makes it unlawful to disclose SSNs from a list. — [Va. Code § 24.2-407.1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title24.2/chapter4/section24.2-407.1/)

**West Virginia**
- Official catchline of § 3-2-30: "...providing voter lists for noncommercial use; prohibition against resale of voter lists for commercial use or profit." — [W. Va. Code § 3-2-30](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/3-2-30/)
- The SoS request document says the requester acknowledges that WV law bars selling or using the list "for commercial or charitable solicitations or advertising." The affidavit text was not retrieved in full. — [WV SoS Voter Data Request](https://sos.wv.gov/FormSearch/Elections/Informational/Voter%20Data%20Request.pdf)
- Amended in 2013 (SB482, preparation and sale of lists) and 2008 (SB496, protecting certain information). — [2013 SB482](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/signed_bills/2013/2013-RS-SB482-SUB1%20ENR_signed.pdf); [2008 SB496](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/signed_bills/2008/2008-RS-SB496-SUB1%20ENR_signed.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Recent amendments (2023–2026):**
  - Alabama HB67 (2026) is the most significant: it moves AL from open-use to no-commercial-use.
  - Louisiana Act 225 (2023) adds confidential fields.
  - Kentucky's 31 KAR 3:010 amendment was in process in 2025.
  - Georgia's July 2024 price change is an SoS policy, not a statute.
- No search turned up a 2023–2026 change for AR, MS, NC, SC, TN, VA or WV, but I did not check session laws for those states directly.

### Gaps
- **Penalties not found:** KY (KRS 117.995 text not retrieved), LA, WV, and the AL HB67 penalty clause. For VA, a specific misdemeanor class for list misuse (beyond § 24.2-1016 felony false statement) was not confirmed.
- **Current Florida § 98.095:** whether the 2004-era use limits (elections, political, governmental, voter registration, law enforcement only; oath) survive in the current statute is unresolved. This is the single most important open item in this region.
- **Louisiana:** the exact current wording and subsection of the commercial-use prohibition on purchased lists (as opposed to the protected-field ban) was not retrieved.

## Q3. Does the restriction cover our use case (internal locate/link database, no redistribution, no solicitation)?

### Takeaway
Where the rule says "commercial purposes" or "commercial use" without a solicitation qualifier, an internal for-profit locate/link database is very likely covered: **GA, AL (2026), KY (which expressly includes "for profit")**, and probably **LA and WV**. Political-only (TN) and recipient-limited (VA) regimes exclude us outright. **SC** (solicitation-only ban, but voters-only buyers) is the ambiguous one. **AR, NC and MS** impose no use restriction I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Kentucky's definition reaches use "for profit," not just solicitation or advertising. An internal profit-making database fits that text. — [31 KAR 3:010 (doc 16761)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/downloads/docs/16761/document.engrossed.pdf)
- Georgia's text is unqualified: "may not be used by any person for commercial purposes"; intentional commercial use is a misdemeanor. — [O.C.G.A. § 21-2-225](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-21/chapter-2/article-6/section-21-2-225); [§ 21-2-601](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2020/title-21/chapter-2/article-15/section-21-2-601/)
- Alabama HB67 bars any "use [of] voter data ... for commercial purposes." Its only carve-out is campaign and election use. — [HB67 enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-enr.pdf); [HB67 engrossed](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-eng.pdf)
- South Carolina's cited prohibition targets "commercial solicitation directed to any person in this State" (§ 30-2-50). Our case has no solicitation. — [SC SEC](https://scvotes.gov/resources/sale-of-voter-registration-lists/)
- West Virginia's operative acknowledgment targets "commercial or charitable solicitations or advertising," but the statute's catchline frames the lists as provided "for noncommercial use." — [WV SoS](https://sos.wv.gov/FormSearch/Elections/Informational/Voter%20Data%20Request.pdf); [§ 3-2-30](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/3-2-30/)
- Arkansas SoS: "no state laws that place restrictions on the use of data that we release." — [AR SoS FAQ](https://www.sos.arkansas.gov/frequently-asked-questions/35)
- Ballotpedia notes that once a file is purchased, reuse is hard to police. This is context, not a legal safe harbor. — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Availability_of_state_voter_files)

### Inferences
- **WV:** a narrow reading (solicitation, advertising, resale) would arguably allow internal non-solicitation use. However, the requester may also have to attest to noncommercial use, so treat WV as not allowed unless the WV SoS confirms otherwise in writing.
- **SC:** a narrow reading of § 30-2-50 would allow internal use. Two problems remain: the SEC sells only to registered SC voters, and the 2018 commentary reads the restriction more broadly. Practical risk stays meaningful.
- **Florida:** if the 2004-era limits remain, our use is outside "elections, political or governmental activities, voter registration, or law enforcement," so it would be **not allowed**. If they were repealed, as the vendor claims, it would be allowed. This must be resolved against the current statute.
- **Skip-trace and debt use:** linking people to debt records could be characterized as supporting collection, which looks like commercial use even if no one is contacted from the voter file. A regulator would likely look at the downstream purpose, not just the absence of mailings.

### Gaps
- No AG opinions or court cases interpreting "commercial purpose" for internal (non-solicitation) use turned up for any of the 12 states. The 2007 AL AG opinion concerns fees and availability, not use.
- **Unverified background:** I recall that federal courts and the DOJ have pressed states on NVRA public-disclosure access (52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)). That provision concerns list-maintenance records and generally does not override state limits on commercial use. Not verified here.

## Q4. Fields included and withheld

### Takeaway
**Florida** (likely) and **North Carolina** are the richest files for linking. NC gives age or birth year, not full DOB. **Georgia** gives birth year only. **Mississippi** withholds DOB, age, phone and email, which makes it weak for identity linking. **Alabama** (2026) withholds month and day of birth, phone, email, DL number and voter ID number. **Louisiana** withholds day and month of birth, email and SMS. SSN and DL are withheld everywhere they were checked.

## Q5. Verdict per state

### Takeaway
**Allowed:** AR, NC, MS. **Conditional or unresolved:** FL (hinges on current § 98.095), SC (solicitation-only ban, but only registered SC voters may buy). **Not allowed:** AL (from 2026), GA, KY, LA, TN, VA, WV (likely).

### Inferences
- **SC:** even under the narrow reading, a corporate requester would need an SC-registered individual to buy, and that individual's use on the company's behalf could be read as a commercial purpose. Get written SEC guidance before relying on it.
- **AL:** files bought before HB67's effective date may still be subject to the new use ban once it takes effect. The text bars "use," not just acquisition. Treat any AL voter data as off-limits for commercial use from 2026.
